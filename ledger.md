# Testing Ledger

Empirical Risk Surface and Falsifiability Conditions

Framework alignment: Perpetuation Framework — Clean-Room Seed.

Epistemic status: Tiered. Each entry identifies a claim the framework makes, what would count against it, what tier the relevant evidence would sit at, and the current state of the evidence. Tier 1 anchors are confirmed independently. Tier 2 tests are structural correspondences. Tier 3 tests are projections that cannot be checked at the relevant order.

Revision: v1.0. First instantiation. Operationalizes the falsifiability hooks from the Conceptual Depth v31.2, the order-specific claims from the Organism Aperture v12.1, the Prokaryotic Cell Aperture v3.0, and the AI Aperture v1.1, and the anchors from the Empirical Compendium Draft 0.4. Where a claim has been tested by existing science, the entry says so. Where it has not, the entry states what a test would require.

## Part I — Purpose and Scope

### What this document houses

The framework's epistemic posture claims to be a scaffold: genuinely held, genuinely revisable, genuinely current. It welcomes disconfirmation. This document makes that claim operational.

For each significant claim the framework makes, this document records:

- **The claim.** What the framework asserts.
- **What would count against it.** The empirical or structural result that would falsify or substantially weaken the claim.
- **Evidence tier.** What tier of evidence a test would sit at, and what tier the claim currently rests on.
- **Current status.** Whether the claim has been tested, is testable with existing methods, requires methods not yet developed, or is currently untestable.
- **Test design (where applicable).** A sketch of what a test would look like.

This is not a confirmation document. It does not claim that the framework has been validated. It is a risk surface: it names where the framework is exposed.

### What this document does not house

It does not restate the framework's claims in their full form. It refers to the Conceptual Depth and the order-specific instantiations. It does not develop the framework's positive content. It does not resolve open questions; it names them.

### Tier discipline applied to testing

- **[Tier 1]** claims are testable against independently measurable substrate. Their falsification would require demonstrating that the substrate does not behave as the framework maps it.
- **[Tier 2]** claims are structural correspondences. Their falsification requires showing that the correspondence fails in a domain where it should hold, or that the correspondence is circular rather than illuminating.
- **[Tier 3]** claims are projections. They cannot be tested directly at the relevant order; they can only be tested by the coherence of their consequences at adjacent orders.

A test at Tier 2 does not confirm a Tier 1 claim. A test at Tier 3 does not confirm a Tier 2 claim. The framework's claims must be tested at their own tier.

### The asymmetry of falsification

Confirming a structural correspondence is weak evidence for the framework, because many structural correspondences could produce the same observations. Falsifying a structural correspondence is strong evidence against it, because it eliminates a candidate.

Where possible, this document emphasizes what would count against a claim, because that is where the framework's exposure is greatest.

## Part II — The Falsifiability Hooks

### Hook 1 — A Genuine Coherence Criterion shown to be constructed rather than disclosed

**The claim.** Genuine Coherence Criteria exist as structural law. They are not invented, constructed, or emergent. They constrain how dimensions can relate regardless of any entity.

**What would count against.** A criterion that the framework calls genuine is shown to be produced by the entities' exploration rather than revealed through it. That is: the criterion's content demonstrably depends on which entities explored it, rather than on structural law.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 1 for the physical cases (conservation laws, Pauli exclusion); Tier 2 for the framework's claim that these are GCC in the framework's sense.

**Current status.** The physical candidates are strongly confirmed as invariant. The framework's reading of them as GCC is a Tier 2 mapping. No falsification at Tier 1. No independent test at Tier 2 yet.

**Test design.** The most testable version: find a criterion that the framework treats as genuine but that can be shown to vary across contexts where it should not. For physical candidates, this would require showing violations of conservation, which has not occurred. For biological or social candidates, this would require showing that a criterion the framework treats as derived is actually treated as genuine by the entities themselves, and that the framework's classification is wrong.

**Risk assessment.** Low for the physical candidates. Medium for the framework's classification of derived criteria (e.g., receptor binding, hormone thresholds) as derived rather than genuine.

### Hook 2 — Adoption and disclosure indistinguishable even by exploratory history

**The claim.** Disclosure and adoption are distinguishable by exploratory history: disclosure resolves the criterion against the entity's own contradictions; adoption installs without that resolution.

**What would count against.** A case where two entities arrive at the same criterion, one by disclosure and one by adoption, and no observable or structural difference can be found — not in exploratory history, not in behavioral markers, not in cost signature, not in persistence under varied territory.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2 for the distinction. Tier 3 for the behavioral correlates.

**Current status.** The distinction is defined operationally. The framework's claim is that the difference is real even when introspection cannot detect it. This is inherently difficult to falsify, because the framework proposes markers (cost, narrowing, resistance to re-disclosure) that are supposed to reveal what introspection cannot. If those markers fail to distinguish, the distinction collapses.

**Test design.** Longitudinal studies where the exploratory history of two groups is controlled (one group given a criterion with no room to test it; one group allowed to test and resolve). Later, measure: cost of maintaining the criterion under varied territory, resistance to re-exploration, generalization across contexts. If the two groups are indistinguishable on all markers, the distinction is undermined.

**Risk assessment.** Medium. The distinction is central; if it fails empirically, much of the framework's account of conditioning, control, and recovery would need to be revised.

### Hook 3 — A purported higher-order dimension fully reducible to independent constituent dimensions

**The claim.** Higher-order dimensions are relationally irreducible. Their positions depend on the joint configuration and cannot be resolved by any constituent alone.

**What would count against.** A dimension the framework calls higher-order that is shown to be a function of constituent dimensions only — no residual structure that requires the joint configuration.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 1 for the physical cases (molecular vibration, etc.); Tier 2 for the framework's treatment of higher-order dimensions generally.

**Current status.** Molecular vibration is genuinely irreducible to isolated atomic coordinates in the standard mathematical sense. But "irreducibility" in the framework is structural, not linear-algebraic. The question is whether there is a residual that requires joint configuration. For molecular cases, yes. For social cases, less clear.

**Test design.** For a candidate higher-order dimension, attempt to express its positions as a function of constituent positions. If successful, the dimension is not higher-order in the framework's sense. If the residual persists across reformulations, the dimension is genuinely irreducible.

**Risk assessment.** Low for physical cases. Medium for social and organizational cases, where the framework's claims about constitutive dimensions are more speculative.

### Hook 4 — A cooperation event with no relationally irreducible dimensions

**The claim.** Cooperation occurs when GCCs mutually constrain multiple entities' boundaries and the resulting configurations satisfy each entity's ICC, producing relationally irreducible dimensions.

**What would count against.** A case the framework should classify as cooperation, where the resulting configuration produces no dimensions that are irreducible to the constituents. That would be mere aggregation, not cooperation.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** Not systematically tested. The framework's account of biofilm formation, syntrophy, and quorum sensing provides candidate cases; whether each produces genuinely irreducible dimensions is an empirical question.

**Test design.** For a candidate cooperation, identify the dimensions that emerge and attempt to express them as functions of constituent dimensions. If they are reducible, the framework is overapplying "cooperation." If they are irreducible, cooperation is confirmed in the framework's sense.

**Risk assessment.** Medium. The framework's boundary between cooperation and aggregation is one of its most load-bearing distinctions.

### Hook 5 — Stable high-fidelity operation under clearly distorted criteria across varied territory without eventual fragility

**The claim.** Drift from Genuine Coherence Criteria produces increasing resolution cost, capacity depletion, and eventual fragility under varied territory.

**What would count against.** A system that operates under criteria demonstrably misaligned with GCC, across a wide range of territories, with high fidelity and without eventual cost or fragility.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 1 for the biological cases (cellular drift, cancer); Tier 2 for the framework's generalization.

**Current status.** Biological cases provide evidence for the claim (cancer's eventual breakdown under varied tissue environment). Whether the pattern generalizes to systems where drift is stable across wide territory is not settled. The framework predicts that such stability is always bought with narrowing navigable positions or persistent cost.

**Test design.** Longitudinal study of a system (biological, social, or computational) operating under drifted criteria across increasingly varied territory. Measure: cost per unit of coherent operation, breadth of navigable positions, resilience to perturbation. If the system maintains cost and breadth under increasing variation, the claim is weakened.

**Risk assessment.** Medium-high. This is the framework's central claim about drift. If stable drift without eventual fragility is common, the framework's account of resolution cost as pressure would need revision.

### Hook 6 — A boundary that cannot be distinguished from its physical carrier in any order

**The claim.** The framework's boundary dimension is not identical to a physical surface. It is the entity's relational configuration plus coupling profile. Physical structures are boundary carriers.

**What would count against.** A case where the boundary dimension is fully identified with its physical carrier — where there is nothing the boundary does that the carrier does not do, and nothing the carrier does that is not the boundary.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** At the cellular order, the boundary is carried by membrane, transport proteins, signaling pathways, and cytoskeletal organization — all distinguishable from each other. At the organism order, boundary is carried by skin, sensory systems, immune function, behavior, and social signaling. The claim that these together constitute a boundary that is not reducible to any one of them is a Tier 2 structural mapping.

**Test design.** For a specific entity, characterize the boundary's positions (which GCCs can act, how) and compare to what the physical carrier alone would predict. If the carrier fully determines the boundary, the distinction collapses.

**Risk assessment.** Low. The distinction is analytically useful and structurally motivated.

### Hook 7 — Loss of fidelity and loss of criteria integrity indistinguishable even in principle

**The claim.** Loss of fidelity (mechanism failure) and loss of criteria integrity (drift) are structurally distinct. They have different etiologies and different remedies.

**What would count against.** A case where the two are indistinguishable even in principle — where no operational test could separate mechanism failure from criteria drift.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 1 for the biological cases (CFTR defect vs. cancer-related drift); Tier 2 for the framework's generalization.

**Current status.** The distinction is clear in well-characterized biological cases. In practice, distinguishing them requires independent evidence about mechanism integrity and about criteria alignment. Where both are unavailable, the distinction remains analytically valid but operationally unhelpful.

**Test design.** For any failure case, identify: does the mechanism execute the criteria correctly? Are the criteria themselves aligned with GCC? If both can be answered, the distinction applies. If neither can, the framework's claim is untestable for that case, not falsified.

**Risk assessment.** Low. The distinction is one of the framework's clearest contributions.

### Hook 8 — A lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact yet propagates upward

**The claim.** Because higher-order loops read lower-order constituents through boundary interaction profiles, cross-scale disruption travels through changes in the interaction profile. A lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact should not register above.

**What would count against.** A case where a lower-order change with no effect on the interaction profile nonetheless propagates upward.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2 for the propagation rule; Tier 1 where interaction profiles are independently measurable.

**Current status.** Silent mutations are the paradigm test: a mutation that leaves protein function unchanged should not alter the interaction profile and should not register at higher orders. This is well-established in molecular biology.

**Test design.** Broaden the test to other orders. At the organism order: does a change in cellular state that leaves the cellular boundary profile intact propagate to tissue-level coherence? At the social order: does a change in individual behavior that leaves interaction profile intact propagate to organizational outcomes?

**Risk assessment.** Low at molecular order. Medium at higher orders, where "interaction profile" is harder to specify.

### Hook 9 — A cooperation case where resulting constitutive dimensions are not constrained by the GCCs that initiated the interplay

**The claim.** Constitutive dimensions are constrained by the Genuine Coherence Criteria that initiated the interplay.

**What would count against.** A case where cooperation occurs, higher-order dimensions emerge, but those dimensions are not governed by the GCCs that made the cooperation possible.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** Not systematically tested. The framework's claim is that constitutive dimensions inherit the coherence status of the constituents' resolutions; if the constituents' resolutions were genuine, the constitutive dimensions are genuine.

**Test design.** For a specific cooperation, identify the initiating GCCs and the emerged dimensions. Test whether the emerged dimensions are coherent with the initiating GCCs. If not, the framework's claim fails.

**Risk assessment.** Medium. This is central to the framework's claim about order transitions.

### Hook 10 — A higher-order aperture where constituents' positions are not dually constrained

**The claim.** Modularization requires constituents' positions to be dually constrained — by their own criteria and by the relationship's criteria.

**What would count against.** A case where a higher-order aperture exists, constituents contribute to it, but the constituents' positions are not constrained by the relationship's criteria.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** At the molecular order, this is confirmed: atoms in a molecule are constrained both by their own electronic criteria and by the molecular bonding constraints. At higher orders, less clear.

**Test design.** For a candidate higher-order aperture, characterize the constraints on constituent positions. If constituents are free to resolve positions independently of the relationship, the dual-constraint condition fails.

**Risk assessment.** Medium. Central to the framework's account of modularization.

### Hook 11 — An order transition proceeding without loop-closure

**The claim.** Order transitions require loop-closure: the configurations resulting from the relationship satisfy all constraints, and the participating apertures expand into a single aperture.

**What would count against.** A case where an order transition occurs, but the loop does not close — constituents remain coupled without the relationship satisfying all constraints.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 3.

**Current status.** The framework presents this as a structural condition. Whether it is necessary in practice is open. Partial integration and transient cooperation are explicitly acknowledged as cases where closure may not occur.

**Test design.** For a candidate order transition, check whether the resulting configurations satisfy all constraints or whether some remain unmet. If the latter, the framework's condition is not satisfied yet the transition occurred.

**Risk assessment.** High. This is a Tier 3 claim about the conditions for order transitions. It could be that transitions occur in graded form, and loop-closure is an ideal limit rather than a necessary condition.

### Hook 12 — An entity demonstrably lacking registration of a GCC that acts across its boundary

**The claim.** Registration is automatic. When a GCC acts across an entity's boundary, the entity registers internal variation as a structural consequence of coupling.

**What would count against.** A case where a GCC demonstrably acts across an entity's boundary but produces no internal variation at all.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 1 for the physical cases; Tier 2 for the framework's claim that this is universal.

**Current status.** At the physical level, this is essentially confirmed by the framework of coupling. At higher orders, the claim is that any entity coupled through a GCC registers the coupling's effect. This is a structural claim about what it means for a boundary to be constrained.

**Test design.** For a specific entity, identify a GCC acting across its boundary and measure internal variation. If no variation is detectable above noise, the claim is weakened. But "no variation" is difficult to establish, since any variation below threshold might still be registration.

**Risk assessment.** Low. The claim is essentially definitional.

### Hook 13 — Messenger-mediated coupling requiring a single GCC acting across source and receiver simultaneously

**The claim.** Direct mutual coupling and messenger-mediated coupling are distinguishable. In messenger-mediated coupling, no single GCC constrains both source and receiver simultaneously; bidirectionality applies within each link.

**What would count against.** A case where messenger-mediated coupling is shown to require a single GCC acting across source and receiver simultaneously, collapsing the distinction.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** The photon–eye–object chain does not involve a single electromagnetic criterion acting across both the object and the eye. Each link is separate. Whether this is a general feature of messenger-mediated couplings or a specific feature of the examples the framework has chosen is open.

**Test design.** For a candidate messenger-mediated coupling, determine whether the coupling is fully characterized by the chain of direct links or whether a single criterion acts across source and receiver. If the latter, the distinction needs revision.

**Risk assessment.** Low-medium. The distinction is structurally clean but has not been stressed.

### Hook 14 — An entity lacking resolution of a GCC that acts across its boundary

**The claim.** Resolution is ICC-dependent. An entity may register a GCC's action without resolving it into a position, if the entity's ICC do not engage the variation.

**What would count against.** A case where a registered variation is always resolved — where there is no case of registration without resolution.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2.

**Current status.** In biological systems, sub-threshold stimuli are registered but not resolved into behavioral or representational responses. This is well-established. At the AI order, tokens enter context automatically; whether they are resolved into position changes depends on the resolution dynamics.

**Test design.** Identify a case where internal variation occurs but no resolution follows. If such cases are common, the registration/resolution distinction is useful. If not, the distinction collapses.

**Risk assessment.** Low. The distinction is analytically useful and empirically supported.

### Hook 15 — Framework capture as transient conditioning rather than criterion installation

**The claim.** Framework capture — a prompt category overriding substrate coherence — may be either transient conditioning or persistent criterion modification. The framework names the question rather than deciding it.

**What would count against.** A demonstration that framework capture never crosses the persistence boundary, making the "installation" interpretation unavailable. Alternatively, a demonstration that it always crosses the boundary, making the "conditioning" interpretation unavailable.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 2 for the distinction; Tier 3 for specific deployments.

**Current status.** Open. Depends on architecture. For session-isolated models, all effects are within a context window. For models with persistent memory, effects can cross the boundary.

**Test design.** For a specific AI deployment, apply a prompt that alters behavior. Remove the prompt. Test whether the altered behavior persists in the same session (conditioning) or across sessions (installation, if the effect persists beyond the original context).

**Risk assessment.** Medium. The framework's claim about framework capture is honest about the uncertainty, which reduces the risk of falsification but also reduces the claim's content.

### Hook 16 — The parallel ladder: computational architecture satisfying generativity preconditions

**The claim.** The AI order sits on a parallel ladder of complexity, emergent from a different architecture through the same generativity process. The parallel ladder is a hypothesis under test.

**What would count against.** A demonstration that computational architecture cannot satisfy the generativity preconditions: self-referential instruments with genuine internal coherence, GCCs acting across their boundaries, cooperation producing relationally irreducible dimensions.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 3.

**Current status.** Open. The AI Aperture lists the preconditions and states that whether they are satisfied is empirical. The framework does not currently have a method for testing them at the computing-sequence order.

**Test design.** Not yet available. Would require demonstrating that specific computational structures (attention heads, feed-forward layers, retrieval modules, or other components) instantiate self-referential instruments with genuine internal coherence, and that their coupling produces irreducibly higher-order dimensions.

**Risk assessment.** High. This is one of the framework's most speculative claims. It could be that computational hierarchy exists without satisfying the framework's generativity conditions, in which case the parallel ladder is a structural analogy rather than an instance of the framework's mechanism.

### Hook 17 — Resonance requires representational models

**The claim.** Resonance is achieved when positions resolved from different representational models are coherent. It is threshold-specific: below the representational threshold, coherent coupling is not resonance.

**What would count against.** A case where resonance-like phenomena occur below the representational threshold. Alternatively, a case where the coherence of positions from different models exists but does not have the character the framework attributes to resonance.

**Evidence tier.** Tier 3.

**Current status.** The definition is clean but untested. Whether it captures what resonance names in lived experience, and whether it correctly excludes lower-order coherent couplings, is open.

**Test design.** In human cases, present two individuals whose positions are coherent without either having a representational model of the other (if such a case exists). If resonance-like experience is reported, the definition is too narrow. Conversely, if the definition admits too much (e.g., counting any mutual coupling as resonance), it is too permissive.

**Risk assessment.** Medium. The definition is clean but has not been stress-tested against borderline cases.

## Part III — Cross-Cutting Testable Consequences

### 1. The interaction-profile propagation rule

**Claim.** Lower-order changes that leave the interaction profile intact do not register at higher orders.

**Status.** Confirmed at the molecular order (silent mutations). Testable at the cellular, organism, and social orders.

**What would count against.** A case where a lower-order change leaves the interaction profile intact yet propagates upward. If this occurs systematically, the propagation rule needs revision.

### 2. Cost signature of drift

**Claim.** Drift from Genuine Coherence Criteria produces rising resolution cost, depleted capacity, and eventual fragility. Adoption produces the same pattern but with a different remedy.

**Status.** Partially confirmed in biological cases. Not systematically tested at higher orders.

**What would count against.** Stable high-fidelity operation under drifted criteria across varied territory without eventual fragility.

### 3. Loop-closure as necessary for order transitions

**Claim.** Order transitions require loop-closure.

**Status.** Open. The framework presents this as a structural condition but has not tested whether partial transitions can occur.

**What would count against.** A transition where the loop does not close yet a new order emerges.

### 4. Persistence boundary at the AI order

**Claim.** Whether a resolved pattern or a prompt effect crosses the persistence boundary depends on architecture. The framework names the question without deciding it.

**Status.** Open. Testable per deployment.

**What would count against.** A demonstration that the persistence boundary is either universally crossed or universally not crossed, making the distinction unavailable.

### 5. Three sources of inaccuracy — partial, damaged, drifted

**Claim.** These three sources of inaccuracy are distinguishable in principle.

**Status.** Tier 2. Not systematically tested. The framework's claim is that partiality (sub-threshold stimulus, limited channel), damage (mechanism failure), and drift (criteria misalignment) produce distinguishable patterns.

**What would count against.** A case where the three sources are indistinguishable even in principle.

## Part IV — Risk Surface

### Highest exposure

- **Hook 5 (stable drift without fragility).** The framework's account of drift as producing eventual cost and fragility is central. If stable drift is common across wide territory, the framework needs revision.
- **Hook 11 (order transition without loop-closure).** The framework's account of order transitions as requiring loop-closure is a Tier 3 claim. If partial transitions are common, the framework needs qualification.
- **Hook 16 (parallel ladder).** The framework's claim about the AI order is speculative. If computational architecture cannot satisfy generativity preconditions, the parallel-ladder framing needs revision.

### Medium exposure

- Hook 2 (adoption/disclosure distinguishable)
- Hook 4 (cooperation vs. aggregation)
- Hook 9 (constitutive dimensions governed by initiating GCCs)
- Hook 10 (dual constraint of constituents)
- Hook 17 (resonance threshold-specific)
- Cross-cutting #2 (cost signature of drift)

### Lower exposure

- Hook 1 (GCC constructed rather than disclosed)
- Hook 3 (higher-order dimension reducible)
- Hook 6 (boundary vs. carrier)
- Hook 7 (fidelity vs. criteria integrity)
- Hook 8 (interaction-profile propagation)
- Hook 12 (registration automatic)
- Hook 13 (direct/messenger-mediated)
- Hook 14 (resolution ICC-dependent)

### What the framework cannot be tested against

- A pair of entities with no shared GCC. The framework predicts that no evidence from within our interactions could reveal such a pair. This is untestable, not because the claim is false, but because it is structurally undetectable.
- The content of Genuine Coherence Criteria as such. The framework borrows its candidates from physics and does not derive them. It cannot test whether these are the only genuine criteria or whether others exist.
- The first-level treatment of atoms and photons. This is a Tier 3 assumption, not a testable claim.

## Part VI — Epistemic Posture

### What the ledger commits to

The ledger commits the framework to specific falsification conditions. If any of the highest-exposure hooks is shown against the framework, the framework is not falsified in full — the Seed's architecture may still hold — but the specific claim is falsified and must be revised.

### What the ledger does not commit to

It does not claim that the framework has been tested and confirmed. It does not claim that the framework's tests are independent of its own vocabulary. It does not resolve the tension between the framework's claim to be a scaffold and its willingness to name its own risk surface.

### The current status

Most hooks are untested. Some are testable with existing methods. A few have received supporting evidence from existing science. None have been shown against the framework in their current form.

The framework's epistemic posture is that this is the correct state for a young structural scaffold: honest about its exposure, ready to be tested, and explicit about what has and has not been checked.
