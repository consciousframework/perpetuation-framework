# Perpetuation Framework: Empirical Compendium

*Draft 0.4. Companion to the Seed. Mappings illuminate; they do not confirm.*

Revision note: Draft 0.4 revises the resonance entry (Section 7) and the corresponding note in Section 12, following the redefinition in the Organism Aperture v12.1 and the AI Aperture v1.1. Resonance is now defined as the coherence of positions resolved from different representational models. The genuine/false distinction is replaced by two tracking conditions — coherent and reinforcing. No other entries changed.

## 0. How to read this document

Each entry anchors one Seed concept in a real-world case. Entries have four parts: the example, the Tier 1 fact (independently measurable), the Tier 2 mapping (framework vocabulary applied by structural correspondence), and what the example does not show. Tier 3 marks process projections that cannot be checked directly at the relevant order.

Four working conventions. First, atoms and photons are treated as first-level or non-divergent entities (a Tier 3 assumption), so they illustrate dimensions, templates, boundaries, the first encounter outcome and cooperation, but not drift, co-option or catalysis; those begin at cells and above. Second, wherever integration or drift is claimed, the entry says where exploration happens: in a lineage (variation and selection) or in an individual. Third, the human practice of setting limits with others is called limit-setting here, to avoid confusion with the Seed's boundary dimension. Fourth, where the chain of Seed concepts is the point (entity, boundary, coupling, loop, constitutive dimension, module), the Tier 2 mapping is narrated step by step. Social-scale entries are Tier 3 and are marked.

## 1. Dimensions, positions, coherence criteria

**Example: the hydrogen atom.** Dimensions: principal quantum number n, orbital angular momentum l, its projection m\_l, and spin projection m\_s. A position is a value on one dimension, for example n = 2.

**Tier 1.** Allowed combinations are restricted: l runs from 0 to n−1, m\_l from −l to +l, m\_s is ±1/2, and in multi-electron atoms no two electrons share all four values (Pauli exclusion).

**Tier 2.** These restrictions play the role of coherence criteria: they state which positions across dimensions can coexist without contradiction. The constraint runs both ways, which is what makes it a loop. The value of l must be smaller than n, so choosing n = 1 leaves no free choice of l, and equally a state with l = 2 cannot have n below 3. No position is independently fixed, and changing any one forces the others to re-resolve. This is the Seed's aperture in miniature: the set of coherent configurations is what remains once all the criteria are satisfied together.

**Second example: a gas.** Pressure, volume and temperature are dimensions, and the ideal gas law (approximate) is the criterion. For a fixed amount of gas, fixing two positions fixes the third, so no position is independently fixed.

**Does not show:** that coherence criteria are anything more than a description of these regularities.

## 2. Genuine Coherence Criteria

**Candidates:** conservation of energy, momentum, angular momentum and charge; Pauli exclusion; the constraints of the electromagnetic interaction.

**Tier 1.** These hold in every measured interaction, and Noether's theorem links the conservation laws to symmetries.

**Tier 2.** Read as structural law, they constrain how dimensions relate regardless of any entity. They are also bidirectional: when an atom absorbs a photon, the photon's momentum is transferred to the atom as recoil, and the same conservation law constrains both. The asymmetry is in magnitude (a photon's momentum is tiny relative to the atom's mass), not in direction.

**Two levels.** Tier 2: the criteria above are candidates for the genuine level. Many constraints that matter in practice sit one level down and depend on specific materials, such as rhodopsin's absorption band or a hormone's binding to its receptor. These are derived: they hold because the materials are what they are, given the genuine level. The compendium calls a criterion genuine only when it holds across materials.

**Does not show:** why these criteria and not others. The framework borrows them from physics and does not derive them, and it has no independent test yet for calling a criterion genuine rather than merely well-fitting. The contrast between internal criteria and genuine ones cannot be illustrated at the atomic order; it first appears at the organism order.

## 3. Finitude

**Example: a cell's energy budget.**

**Tier 1.** A cell has a finite supply of ATP and a finite number of molecules. Maintaining gradients and processing signals costs energy, so cells regulate a small set of key variables (pH, ion concentrations, membrane potential) instead of every molecule.

**Tier 2.** Finitude forces compression: stable regulation of a few variables replaces tracking everything. It also shapes the boundary, because the cell exposes only the couplings it can afford to maintain, such as receptors for the signals that matter and pumps for the gradients that must be held. In humans, limited working memory (about four chunks by some estimates) plays a similar role.

**Does not show:** a unit or measure of capacity that applies across orders.

## 4. Aperture, template, entity, Self-Referential Instrument

**Atom.** The atomic aperture is the set of coherent configurations of electrons around a nucleus. A template fixes some dimensions and leaves others free: an element fixes nuclear charge Z, while electron occupation varies (ground, excited, ionized states) within the criteria. A particular sodium atom in its ground state is an entity instantiating the sodium template. If it loses its outer electron, it occupies another position within the same template, the ion, and its interaction profile changes with it.

**Species.** A species can be read as a template for organisms: some dimensions are held stable across individuals (body plan, core physiology) and others vary within constraints (size, behavior). An individual organism is an entity. This is Tier 2; the species concept is contested in biology, and reading biodiversity as exploration of coherent configurations is Tier 3.

**Self-Referential Instrument.** Tier 1: the hypothalamus regulates body temperature against a set point, and in fever pyrogens raise that set point, so a person with a rising fever feels cold and shivers in a warm room. Tier 2: the organism resolves its position from its own internal criteria (the set point), not directly from the environment. The same input therefore resolves differently once the criteria are updated: the room has not changed, but a body whose set point is higher registers it as too cold. The criteria update at the entity level without changing the species template.

**Does not show:** that set points correspond to coherence criteria in the Seed's strict sense.

## 5. Boundary

**Atom.** The boundary position pairs a configuration with its interaction profile. In the ground state, with no perturbation, the electron configuration fixes valence, polarizability and electronegativity, which are what other atoms can couple to.

**Tier 1.** Two approaching atoms typically attract at moderate distance and repel at very short distance (the potential energy curve). Helium, with a closed shell, couples extremely weakly to other atoms.

**Tier 2.** Attraction is the encounter acting through the profile, and repulsion at close range is the configuration resisting overlap. Helium shows a boundary whose profile exposes almost no couplings, close to non-constraining. Ionization shows the boundary reorganizing with the configuration: when sodium loses its outer electron, the configuration changes and so does the profile. Neutral atoms interact through short-range dispersion forces, while ions carry a net charge and couple to other charges over long range (Tier 1).

**Cell.** A hormone acts only on cells that carry the matching receptor. Tier 2: the receptor repertoire is part of the cell's interaction profile, so which criteria can act on the cell depends on it.

**Organism.** Behavior is the observable, external expression of the current configuration. It is the interaction profile being enacted: the smile, the gaze, the posture, the tone, the movement. The internal configuration is private. Behavior is what other entities can register. When a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across the organism's boundary, behavior is the carrier of the outgoing constraint. It is not the constraint itself; the constraint is the effect of the behavior under the criterion on the other entity's boundary. [Tier 2]

**Does not show:** what an entity's full interaction profile is. Profiles are inferred from the couplings observed so far.

### 5.1 Weak coupling and the limit of no coupling

The Seed says that entities with no Genuine Coherence Criterion acting across their boundaries have no encounter and leave no trace in each other. The cases below run from weak coupling to the edge of what has been observed.

**Photon and glass.** Tier 1: visible light crosses window glass, but it is not unaffected. Its phase velocity drops to about the speed of light divided by 1.5, its direction bends at each surface (refraction), about 4 percent reflects at each surface at normal incidence, and a little scatters. Glass does not absorb visible light because its electronic transitions lie in the ultraviolet, above the energy of any visible photon. Photons are bosons, so Pauli exclusion plays no part here; that principle is what keeps the filled electron shells of matter from overlapping. Tier 2: absorption needs a resonance between the photon's frequency and a transition in the molecule's interaction profile, and glass's profile offers none in the visible range, so that coupling is absent. A weaker, non-resonant coupling remains, acting through the same electromagnetic criterion: the photon's field distorts the glass's electrons, and their response changes the photon's phase velocity and direction. This is an encounter without absorption, and the change in the photon is the evidence that a criterion acts.

**Neutrinos.** Tier 1: neutrinos interact only through the weak interaction and gravity. Tens of billions of solar neutrinos cross every square centimeter of the Earth each second and almost all pass through with no effect, but a very small fraction interact, and huge detectors (a cubic kilometer of ice, tens of thousands of tonnes of water) record the flashes of light that result. Tier 2: a criterion acts across the neutrino's boundary and that of ordinary matter, but the neutrino's interaction profile exposes only the weak coupling (it has no electric charge and does not feel the strong force), so encounters are rare. When they happen they are real and leave traces, which is how the criterion is known. The neutrino is the nearest observed case to a non-encounter and is still not one.

**Dark matter.** Tier 1: galaxies rotate faster at large radii than their visible matter can explain, gravitational lensing reveals mass where little light is seen, and cosmological models need about five times as much dark matter as ordinary matter. Direct searches have found no non-gravitational coupling to ordinary matter so far. Tier 2: the pattern fits entities coupled to ours through gravity alone, or through couplings too weak to have been detected. We know of them only through residuals: effects on our dimensions that the known dimensions cannot absorb. That is the rule for positing unobserved dimensions applied in practice: posit one when a residual demands it, and count it as observed when independent instruments converge, as lensing, rotation curves and the cosmic microwave background do. Tier 3: whether dark matter has internal structure. Observations limit how strongly it interacts with itself (for example in colliding galaxy clusters), so there is currently no support for rich structure, and some of the same observations are explained by modified gravity, on which the framework takes no side.

**Does not show:** a pair of entities with no shared criterion at all. Every known pair shares at least gravity. If such a pair exists, the Seed implies that neither would leave any trace in the other, so no evidence from inside our own interactions could reveal it, and the framework cannot test the idea. Where a criterion is shared, the entities are in encounter and are in principle detectable, however weakly.

### 5.2 Direct mutual coupling and messenger-mediated coupling

**Example: two people talking.** Sound waves carry the acoustic signal between them.

**Tier 1.** Speech production involves vocal fold vibration and articulatory modulation, generating pressure variations in air. These pressure variations propagate as sound waves. The listener's cochlea transduces the mechanical pressure variation into neural signals. The speaker's articulatory movements and the listener's cochlear mechanics are both confirmed physiology.

**Tier 2.** A coupling between two organisms can take two forms. **Direct mutual coupling** occurs when a single Genuine Coherence Criterion constrains both organisms' boundaries simultaneously. **Messenger-mediated coupling** occurs when constraint travels through one or more intermediate entities. The conversation is messenger-mediated: the speaker's behavior constrains the pressure wave (a direct coupling between speaker and air), and the pressure wave constrains the listener's boundary (a direct coupling between air and listener). Bidirectionality applies within each link — the pressure wave constrains the speaker's articulators in return, and the listener's body constrains the air — but no single criterion constrains both speakers at once. The chain as a whole is the composite topology.

Most organism-order couplings are messenger-mediated. Vision travels through photons. Hearing travels through pressure waves. Smell travels through molecules. Touch travels through mechanical contact. Direct mutual coupling, in which one criterion constrains both boundaries simultaneously, is the special case, not the general one.

**Does not show:** that messenger-mediated coupling is weaker or less genuine than direct mutual coupling. The framework treats each link as a direct coupling governed by the same criteria. What differs is the topology, not the structural law.

### 5.3 Chained and mutual topology: the gaze example

**Example: two individuals, A and B.**

**Scenario 1 — A looks at B, B does not notice.**

**Tier 1.** A's visual system is directed at B. Photons reflect from B's surface and enter A's eyes. A's retina transduces the photons, and A's visual cortex resolves a representation of B. B is in encounter with photons (ambient light strikes B's skin, some is absorbed, some reflected), but B's visual system is not directed at A and does not resolve the photons from A into a representation of A. B may register a change in ambient light, but not "A is looking at me." The asymmetry is confirmed: gaze detection requires the observer's visual system to be directed at the observed.

**Tier 2.** The coupling is a chain: light source → B → photons → A's retina. A's boundary is constrained by the photons carrying information about B. A registers internal variation and resolves a representation of B. B registers photon impacts, but those registrations do not encode A's state. B does not register A. The relation is asymmetric at the level of representation. This is **chained topology**: no return path exists, and constraint flows in one direction. A represents B. B does not represent A.

The asymmetry is structural. It does not require either party to be incoherent. A's ICC may be genuine, and A's representation of B may be accurate. B's ICC may be genuine, and B's failure to represent A may simply reflect that B's attention is elsewhere. What differs is the topology, not the criteria.

**Scenario 2 — B turns and looks at A.**

**Tier 1.** B's visual system is now directed at A. Photons from A reach B's retina. B resolves a representation of A. Simultaneously or in turn, A's visual system is directed at B, and A resolves a representation of B. Both are now registering each other's gaze direction. Eye contact detection is confirmed in human infants from early months, and gaze following develops within the first year.

**Tier 2.** The return path closes the loop. The topology is now **mutual**: photons travel from B to A and from A to B. Each organism's behavior (gaze direction, posture, expression) is an outgoing constraint on the other's behavior under the same electromagnetic criterion. The loop closes.

Each entity constructs a model of the other's model. A models what B represents, including a model of A-as-seen-by-B. B does the same. The models are private. A's model of B's model of A can diverge from B's actual model of A. Both can be internally coherent. The divergence is a consequence of representational asymmetry and finite access, not of drift.

**Does not show:** that either party has direct access to the other's internal state. Each entity registers only the other's behavior (the interaction profile in action) and constructs its own model from its own ICC. The self-model — the representation of one's own state under the inferred perspective of the other — is generated from the organism's own criteria, not from access to the other's interior.

## 6. Registration, representation, and nested representation

### 6.1 Registration and representation

**Registration.** Every entity registers internal variations produced by encounter. A cell registers membrane potential changes, ion concentrations, sensor-protein conformations. An organism registers sensory and interoceptive states. Registration is automatic: the encounter produces internal variation, and the entity's structure holds that variation as a state. [Tier 1: sensory transduction and interoception. Tier 2: the mapping.]

**Representation.** The organism order adds representation: a stable, reusable correlation structure, built from registrations, that can be navigated without immediate input. It holds correlations with external entities, with the organism itself, and with its relationships. [Tier 1: memory, learning, and prediction in nervous systems. Tier 2: the mapping.]

**Does not show:** that registration alone constitutes representation. Registration is the substrate. Representation is the correlation structure built from it. The distinction matters because an entity can register a variation without representing it — as when B registers photon impacts without resolving a representation of A.

### 6.2 Nested representation and modeled perspectives

**Example: theory of mind and false belief tasks.**

**Tier 1.** Children develop the capacity to attribute false beliefs to others around ages 4–5, as measured by the Sally–Anne task: the child must represent what another person believes, even when that belief is incorrect. Gaze following and joint attention develop earlier, within the first year. Social anxiety involves heightened modeling of others' models of the self. Projection involves attributing one's own states to others.

**Tier 2.** At the representational threshold, the organism can construct models of other entities' models, including models of itself as seen by another. The structure is a hierarchy of registrations and constructions. Consider A and B in mutual topology:

1. **B's actual internal state.** B's configuration, resolved from B's ICC. A has no direct access to it.
2. **A's registration of B's behavior.** A registers B's gaze, posture, expression. This is directly supported by territory-mediated variation. It is a registration, not a model of B's interior.
3. **A's model of what B represents.** A constructs a model of B's internal representation from A's own ICC and representational structure. This is inference, not access.
4. **A's model of A-as-seen-by-B.** A constructs a model of its own state under the inferred perspective of B. This is a self-model. It is generated from A's own ICC and representational structure.

Only #2 is directly registered. #3 and #4 are constructed. They can diverge from B's actual internal state without either A or B being incoherent.

**Self-perception is not perception of the self as an external object.** It is the organism's representation of its own state under a modeled external perspective. ICC supplies the criteria. The representational mechanism constructs the model. The model can contain the self as represented under those criteria. This capacity is restricted to the representational slice: social mammals and humans, to different degrees.

**Does not show:** that the capacity is fully developed in all social mammals. The Tier 3 caveat applies: which species instantiate which levels of the hierarchy is an empirical question, not settled by the framework.

### 6.3 Models are not entities

**Example: two individuals with different weather models.** A represents a warm, sunny day. B represents coats and umbrellas.

**Tier 1.** No physical entity corresponds to "a model" as a bounded object. Models are correlation structures distributed across neural tissue. They have no membrane, no boundary, no independent existence.

**Tier 2.** A representation is an internal correlation structure. It is not an instantiation of an aperture template at its own order. It has no boundary dimension in the Seed's sense. Genuine Coherence Criteria act across the boundaries of entities, through messenger entities or direct coupling. They do not act between two entities' internal models. There are no Genuine Coherence Criteria between models.

When two models appear incoherent — A represents a warm day, B represents coats and umbrellas — the incoherence is not between the models. It is between each model and the territory, or between the two entities' positions on a shared dimension. The apparent contradiction is a diagnostic signal, not evidence of a coupling between models.

The intuition that two incoherent models imply a GCC between them is strong and wrong. It mistakes the correlation structure for an entity. Models are not entities. They do not couple. They are navigated by the organism that holds them.

**Does not show:** that models are unrelated. They are constrained by the same GCCs that constrain the territory they represent, because they are resolved from ICC that may be genuine. But that is shared structural law, not a coupling between models.

## 7. Resonance, coherent and reinforcing

**Example: two people in conversation who feel "in sync."**

**Tier 1.** Physiological synchrony is measurable: heart rate coupling, respiratory synchrony, mirroring of gestures and postures, and emotional contagion have all been documented in conversation, psychotherapy, and parent–infant interaction. Rapport in psychotherapy research correlates with these markers.

**Tier 2.** **Resonance is achieved when positions resolved from different representational models are coherent.** It requires representation, so it appears at or above the representational threshold. Below that threshold, entities have no representational models, and their positions emerge directly from their ICC. There is coherent coupling there, but not resonance. Resonance names what happens when coherence is achieved between positions that each came through a private model.

The models remain private. A's model of B's model of A can diverge from B's actual model of A. What resonance requires is not that the models match, but that the positions they produce are non-contradictory with each other. Each entity resolves from its own ICC; the coherence is achieved at the level of positions, not at the level of the models that generated them.

**Resonance does not imply genuineness.** Positions from two drifted models can be coherent with each other while both are misaligned with territory. The coherence is real; whether it tracks the Genuine Coherence Criteria is a separate question. The distinction is expressed as two tracking conditions, not two kinds of resonance.

- **Coherent resonance.** The coherence between positions tracks the Genuine Coherence Criteria the entities are coupled through. Both parties hold accurate emission and selective permeability; the relationship closes without persistent cost. The coherence is the observable correlate of balanced coupling.
- **Reinforcing resonance.** The coherence between positions tracks drift. Each entity's positions are non-contradictory with the other's because each has installed the other's criteria, or because both have drifted in compatible ways. The control/porous pairing is the paradigm: stable resonance, non-genuine criteria, mutual reinforcement of drift. The resonance is real; what it tracks is not the Genuine Coherence Criteria.

The distinction is in what the resonance is aligned with, not in the resonance itself. From the inside, coherent and reinforcing resonance can be indistinguishable. The structural markers — persistent cost, narrowing of navigable positions, resistance to re-disclosure — separate them.

**Recursiveness is development, not constitution.** Once resonance is achieved, the feedback loop between the models through observed positions can deepen it. Each entity registers the other's positions, updates its model, resolves new positions, and the coherence is tested again. This is what makes resonance sustained over time, capable of correcting or reinforcing drift, and capable of deepening into joint understanding. It is not what makes it resonance. A single exchange can be resonant.

**Tier 3.** Felt resonance does not diagnose genuineness. The structural markers — persistent cost, narrowing, resistance to re-disclosure — are the tests. From the inside, reinforcing resonance can be indistinguishable from coherent resonance. The distinction requires external markers and independent convergence, which is consistent with the framework's epistemic posture.

**Does not show:** how to distinguish coherent from reinforcing resonance from the inside alone. The distinction requires external markers, which is consistent with the framework's epistemic posture.

## 8. Outcomes of encounter

### 8.1 Preserving internal criteria (atoms)

**Tier 1:** two hydrogen atoms attract and bond when their electron spins are antiparallel, and repel when the spins are parallel (the singlet and triplet energy curves). Helium atoms, with closed shells, repel at short range because overlap of filled shells is excluded (Pauli exclusion). Tier 2: the same criteria act in every case, and what differs is whether the two configurations can be satisfied together. Antiparallel hydrogen atoms can, so the relationship survives each atom's own constraints and a bond forms, which is cooperation. Parallel spins cannot, and neither can overlapped closed shells, so each atom keeps its configuration and the atoms are pushed apart. This is the first outcome, preserving internal criteria, and it shows that the outcome depends on positions across the dimensions, not on the entities alone. **Does not show:** that atoms have internal criteria that could differ from the genuine ones; at this order only preservation and cooperation are illustrated.

### 8.2 Installing external criteria: conditioning (human scale, Tier 3)

A child's cry is a registration of the child's state, a variation resolved through the child's boundary. A caregiver who labels it weakness, or answers it with alarm, supplies a criterion. If the child adopts that criterion without resolving it against its own registration, the criterion is installed, which is the second outcome. The asymmetry of capacity is what lets the installation succeed: the child cannot test the criterion against alternatives, and the relationship it depends on makes adoption the way to stay coherent with the caregiver. The child's later limit-setting is then resolved from the installed criterion, and the contradiction between what the child registers and what the criterion prescribes is resolved by suppressing the registration. Because the installed criterion resolves accurately, fidelity is intact, and the drift shows up only as persistent cost: a contradiction that goes away only by suppression. **Does not show:** anything about developmental psychology. This is a structural illustration, and real cases involve many more factors.

### 8.3 Catalytic outcome

Human scale (Tier 3): A navigates similar dimensions to B, notices that B resolves a situation with criteria better aligned with a genuine one, and signals positions to test (shows an example, asks a question). B tests them against its own contradictions before adopting any criterion, so B's resolution is never bypassed. The three routes to a criterion differ only in who proposes the positions and whether the entity's own resolution is kept: in its own exploration, the entity proposes and tests; in catalysis, another entity proposes and the entity still tests; in installation, the entity's own resolution is bypassed.

Lower-order candidate (Tier 3): chaperone-assisted protein folding. For many proteins the sequence determines the native fold (Anfinsen), and chaperones prevent aggregation and give kinetic assistance without dictating the structure. The contrasting case is prion templating, where a misfolded protein imposes its conformation on others, which is closer to installation. **Does not show:** that proteins have internal coherence criteria that update; the mapping is a candidate. Chemical catalysis is a different notion and is not evidence here.

## 9. Dimensional integration and failure modes

**Ion regulation in prokaryotes.** Tier 1: bacteria and archaea maintain internal K+, Na+ and H+ levels against external fluctuations using uptake systems, antiporters and pumps, and use mechanosensitive channels as emergency valves under osmotic downshock. Their ion gradients and membrane potential then serve as the driving force for ATP synthesis, transport and, in many cells, signaling. Tier 2, step by step: external ion and osmotic variation reaches the cell through electromagnetic and chemical criteria; the cell's existing internal criteria cannot resolve the full range of that variation, and without regulation the disruption exceeds capacity. The pumps and antiporters are mechanisms that produce positions on the ion dimension from inside the cell, so the dimension is integrated and the boundary dimension extends. Its coupling capacity changes with it, since the gradients the cell now controls become something it can use. The criterion being disclosed is physics (electrochemical equilibrium, as in the Nernst relation), and exploration occurs at lineage level: variation and selection disclose which mechanisms satisfy it, and variants that satisfy it poorly pay a cost or die, which is how mis-disclosure is removed at this order.

Tier 3: that this was the first integration. Whether the last universal common ancestor already had its own pumps is contested, and allosteric enzymes or chemical feedback systems may be counterexamples to 'no integration below cells'. **Does not show:** why this mechanism and not others.

**Human limit-setting (Tier 3 analogy).** When external demands call for positions that contradict a person's internal coherence, three outcomes are available: preserve the criteria by refusing contact, adopt a criterion unresolved (co-option), or develop a capacity to set limits that makes the variation navigable. The third is analogous to integration. Exploration here occurs within the individual.

**Capacity-limit failure.** Tier 1: bacteria under extreme hypo-osmotic shock can lyse when regulation is overwhelmed. Tier 2: the configuration is destroyed because capacity is exceeded, not because the criteria were wrong.

**Loss of fidelity.** Tier 1: cystic fibrosis results from defects in a chloride channel (CFTR), and retinal degeneration damages photoreceptors. Tier 2: the regulatory aim is intact but the resolution mechanism is damaged (material etiology). **Does not show:** how to tell mechanism failure from criteria drift in practice; cost and response to repair may help.

## 10. Cooperation and higher orders

### 10.1 Two atoms into a molecule

Tier 1: two hydrogen atoms bond, and the molecule has a bond length, vibrational modes and rotational states. Molecular interactions run largely through them: infrared absorption couples to vibrational modes through changes in the dipole, and long-range forces depend on multipole moments and polarizability.

Tier 2, step by step: each hydrogen atom is an entity whose boundary pairs its configuration with its interaction profile. Electromagnetic and exchange criteria act across the two boundaries, so the atoms are in encounter. When the spins are antiparallel the configurations survive each atom's own constraints and the relationship closes into a loop; the molecule's aperture then incorporates the atoms' dimensions and the emerged ones. Bond length, vibration and rotation are constitutive dimensions: their positions depend on both atoms and cannot be navigated by either alone. Normal modes re-express the 3N atomic coordinates, so degrees of freedom are conserved; what is new is coordinates adapted to the constraint structure, in which the constraints are simpler. Mutual coherence holds in both directions: nuclear positions and the potential energy surface constrain each other, and where the Born–Oppenheimer separation fails (conical intersections) the coupling cannot be dropped. The atoms are modularized: they keep their identity as constituents, and groups of atoms such as the functional groups of organic chemistry act as reusable modules that new molecules instantiate without re-resolving them. The molecule then has a boundary of its own, whose interaction profile (dipole, polarizability, vibrational and rotational states) sets what photons and other molecules can couple to. A stable molecule is a closed loop, and a transient collision complex that separates is a candidate for a relationship without closure (Tier 3).

**Does not show:** that a collective coordinate carries more than the constituents' degrees of freedom. The reading that higher-order positions depend only on the interaction profile holds at long range and fails at short range, where electron clouds overlap and the detailed configuration matters.

### 10.2 Photon, eye and the retinal network

One chain, narrated step by step. Each step gives what is observed (Tier 1) and how the Seed's concepts read it (Tier 2).

**Step 1. The photon as an entity.** Tier 1: a visible photon is characterized by a frequency, a direction of propagation and a polarization, tied by fixed relations. Energy equals Planck's constant times frequency, momentum is energy divided by the speed of light and points along the direction, and polarization lies perpendicular to that direction. Phase is only defined relative to something else. Tier 2: these are the photon's dimensions, mutually constrained by criteria that hold in every measured interaction. Choosing a frequency fixes energy and the size of the momentum together, and choosing a direction restricts the allowed polarizations, so no position can change without the others re-resolving. The photon is an entity instantiating that template (a Tier 3 first-level assumption).

**Step 2. How we know a criterion couples the photon to a molecule.** Tier 1: the same photon is absorbed by some molecules and not by others. Light near 500 nm is absorbed by the retinal chromophore of rhodopsin, while water, the lens and the vitreous humor transmit it. Absorption requires the photon's energy to match an electronic transition of the molecule, and its probability depends on the angle between the photon's polarization and the molecule's transition dipole. Transparent media are not fully uncoupled: they refract light, bending its direction and slowing its phase velocity, which is a weak coupling. Tier 2: coupling is inferred from selective change. Where the photon is altered, a criterion acts across the photon's boundary and the molecule's; where it passes with no measurable change, the coupling is absent or too weak to register. Retinal's interaction profile exposes a transition that this photon's frequency can match.

**Step 3. The encounter.** Tier 1: absorption excites retinal, and within about 200 femtoseconds it twists from the 11-cis to the all-trans form, with a quantum yield of about 0.65. In quantum electrodynamics the photon no longer exists as a separate excitation afterward. Tier 2: while the encounter lasts, the photon and the chromophore vary their related dimensions coherently. Photon energy becomes electronic excitation, polarization and angular momentum select which transition occurs and how likely it is, and the photon's momentum appears as a negligible recoil. Their boundaries are mutually constrained, and a higher-order dimension emerges from the relationship: the excited chromophore's electronic state together with its twist angle, a coordinate neither constituent had alone. Its positions depend on both (the photon's frequency and polarization, retinal's geometry) and constrain them in return, because the excited-state energy surface limits the twist and the twist determines the electronic state. The loop closes briefly and leaves a changed retinal. The photon's dimensions are modularized into this joint configuration; in the framework's bookkeeping they are transferred, which describes the same event as the photon ceasing and is not a competing claim.

**Step 4. The molecule as a component of the cell.** Tier 1: retinal sits inside the protein opsin, embedded in the membranes of rod outer-segment discs, and the protein tunes which wavelengths retinal absorbs. Isomerization changes the protein's shape, which activates the G protein transducin, which activates phosphodiesterase, which lowers cGMP and closes cGMP-gated channels, hyperpolarizing the cell. One absorbed photon produces a measurable electrical response in a rod. Tier 2: rhodopsin is a module of the cell's aperture, and its conformation is coherently related to the cell's other dimensions: cGMP concentration, channel states and membrane potential. In the dark, open channels sustain an inward current, and the molecular product shifts the position that this loop (membrane potential, channel states, ion fluxes) resolves to. Amplification, in which one activated rhodopsin activates many transducin molecules, is what carries the product from the molecular order up to the cellular one.

**Step 5. From cells to a network.** Tier 1: photoreceptors connect to bipolar and horizontal cells, and horizontal cells feed back onto photoreceptors. Retinal ganglion cells have center–surround receptive fields that respond to local contrast, not to absolute light level, and in many species some respond to the direction of motion. Orientation selectivity arises mainly later, in visual cortex. Tier 2: the synaptic couplings run both ways, so the cells' boundaries mutually constrain and the loop closes among them. Contrast is a constitutive dimension: its position depends on many photoreceptors and their neighbors and cannot be navigated by any single one, which stays a module. The retinal output then becomes substrate for the next order, where visual cortex builds orientation as a further constitutive dimension, the same recursion repeated.

**Step 6. The loop reaches the organism.** Tier 1: eyes move several times per second, and the head and body move, so what reaches the retina depends on where the organism looks. Tier 2: the organism's motor outputs change the light arriving at the retina, so one loop spans photon, molecule, cell, network and organism. A disturbance at any order alters what the others can resolve, through the boundary positions that couple them. What the organism registers is a correlation between its internal variations and the source, resolved against its own internal criteria, never direct access: a bee, with receptors tuned to ultraviolet, blue and green, couples to a different subset of the same light.

**Does not show:** why this machinery exists and not orphaned alternatives (selection explains persistence, and the framework describes structure). Quantum electrodynamics has no individual photon identity or trajectory, so 'the photon persists' is only bookkeeping. The first-level treatment of the photon is a Tier 3 assumption. Related views: enactivism and predictive processing.

### 10.3 Exploratory case: the double slit

Included because it exercises constitutive dimensions and graded coupling. Tier 2 to 3.

Tier 1: single electrons build an interference pattern one dot at a time. Adding which-path information reduces fringe visibility in a graded way (V² + D² ≤ 1), erasing the information restores it, and larger molecules show interference when decoherence is small.

Tier 2, step by step: the electron and the slit structure couple through a shared criterion, and their relationship gives rise to a constitutive dimension (relative phase) that neither navigates alone. A detector adds a coupling that carries path correlations; the constraint changes and the fringes vanish without any observer. Anything that carries the correlation counts, including a stray gas molecule or a thermally emitted photon, which is why larger molecules lose interference as decoherence grows (Tier 1). The path dimension is un-navigated until something couples to it, so the one-position rule is not violated. At the screen, a much stronger coupling constrains the electron's configuration without locking it to a single value. A superposition has a constitutive dimension, and a mixture does not.

**Does not show:** outcome selection, the Born rule, a stance on Bell, or non-spatial superpositions (spin, energy levels). It closely resembles relational quantum mechanics and quantum Darwinism; the distinct claim is genuine criteria as structural law.

## 11. Cross-scale coherence disruption

Because bidirectional constraint closes one loop across orders, loss of coherence at one order alters what the other orders can resolve through boundary positions.

**Cancer (Tier 2).** Tier 1: cancer cells acquire insensitivity to growth-suppressing signals and sustain proliferative signaling. Chemical carcinogens and other damage can cause the mutations behind this, among other routes, and the tissue environment influences which variants persist. Tier 2, step by step: a cell is a module in the tissue's loop, and its boundary profile includes receptors for the signals that keep it coherent with the tissue. Damage alters the cell's configuration so that the profile no longer couples to those signals, and the cell stops responding through its boundary. Its internal criteria drift, and exploration here occurs within the body, as clonal variation and selection among the cell's descendants, which can favor variants that proliferate regardless of tissue signals. As these cells stop resolving coherently with their neighbors, tissue-level coherence degrades, which is the bottom-up direction. Top-down, higher-order incoherence does not create the deviant cell, but it can change which variants persist (Tier 3).

**Company leadership (Tier 3, social scale).** Inconsistent direction from the integrating level sends contradictory signals to teams. Teams protect local coherence by closing channels, which further fragments the whole.

**Testable consequence (Tier 3).** Degradation should travel only through changes in the interaction profile. A lower-order change that leaves the profile intact should not register above, as with silent mutations that leave protein function unchanged.

**Does not show:** how far degradation spreads; capacity and internal criteria decide that, and cancer in particular is multifactorial.

## 12. What these entries do not establish

- No entry makes a quantitative prediction that standard physics or biology does not already make.
- Candidate genuine criteria are borrowed from physics, and the framework has no independent test for genuineness yet.
- The first-level treatment of atoms and photons is a working assumption.
- Each integration or drift claim depends on where exploration occurs, stated entry by entry.
- The weak-coupling cases (glass, neutrinos, dark matter) do not show a pair with no shared criterion. Such a pair would be undetectable from within, so the framework cannot test it.
- The nested representation hierarchy (registration → model of other → model of other's model → self-as-seen-by-other) is Tier 2 with a Tier 3 caveat about which species instantiate which levels.
- Resonance is the coherence of positions resolved from different representational models. It does not imply genuineness. Coherent and reinforcing resonance can be indistinguishable from the inside; the distinction requires external markers and independent convergence.
- Models are not entities. They have no boundary dimension and do not couple. Apparent contradictions between two models are diagnostic signals about each model's relation to territory, not evidence of a coupling between models.

**What would count against the mappings:** non-monotonic loss of fringe visibility as which-path coupling grows; a lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact yet propagates upward; persistent drift with no detectable cost or unresolved contradiction; a species that demonstrably instantiates the full nested representation hierarchy without the representational slice's neural architecture.
