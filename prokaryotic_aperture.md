Vocabulary note

The cell's constitutive dimensions are higher-order dimensions that emerge from cooperation among lower-order entities (macromolecules, supramolecular assemblies, metabolic networks). They are genuine by emergence to the degree the lower-order constituents' resolutions are genuine.

The cell's integrative dimensions are dimensions the cell gains through dimensional integration (horizontal expansion). At the cellular order, this is the paradigmatic case: ion regulation, osmolarity, pH, thermal tolerance, nutrient acquisition, redox management. Integrative dimensions are where genuineness must be disclosed through exploration.

The cell's boundary dimension is the emergent dimension representing how the cell constrains and is constrained by external territory. Its positions are pairs: (configuration, interaction profile). Boundary carriers include the membrane, cell wall, capsule, transport proteins, secretion systems, pili, and flagella.

Exploration at the cellular order occurs at lineage level: variation and selection among cell variants disclose which mechanisms hold. The individual cell does not explore. This is stated entry by entry where integration or drift is claimed.

No representational threshold. The cell does not build a model of territory that requires choosability. Its ICC resolve automatically from its structure. The cell is not a chooser. It is a resolver.

Part II — The Prokaryotic Cell as Entity

The cell's aperture template

The cell is an entity: an instantiation of an aperture template at the cellular order. The cell's aperture is constituted by a set of dimensions whose positions are mutually constrained by Genuine Coherence Criteria. The mutual constraint closes into a loop. The configurations that satisfy this closed mutual constraint are the aperture's fixed points. [Tier 1: the cell as a bounded, self-maintaining system is confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The cell's template fixes a subset of dimensions as stable positions and leaves another subset open to constrained variation. The fixed dimensions are the cell's inherited structural givens: membrane architecture, ribosome structure, genetic code, core metabolic pathways, cell-division machinery. The free dimensions are the cell's accumulated state: gene expression profile, metabolic flux distribution, ion concentrations, membrane potential, stress-response state. [Tier 1: inherited vs. accumulated structure is confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The cell's Self-Referential Instrument

The cell is a Self-Referential Instrument. Its dimensional positions are resolved from its internal coherence criteria. The cell does not compare incoming variation against a fixed position. It compares incoming variation against its coherence criteria: does this variation produce internal changes that are coherent with the cell's existing dimensional structure, or does it produce contradiction? [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

The cell's reference is not a position. The cell's reference is its coherence criterion itself. The cell's identity is the pattern of coherence criteria it instantiates — regulatory networks, feedback couplings, homeostatic set-points — not the specific positions it occupies at any moment. Positions vary within the constraints of the criteria. The criteria are what persist.

The Interiority Principle at the cellular order

The cell does not navigate external territory directly. It navigates the internal variations produced in it by external territory impacting its boundary through at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion. The cell's Self-Referential Instrument operates entirely on this internal registration substrate. [Tier 1: the cell registers internal states — membrane potential, cytoplasmic concentrations, sensor-protein conformations — not external objects directly. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The cell's sensors, receptors, and transport systems are the boundary carriers through which external variation is registered internally. The cell never compares its ICC to "the outside world." It compares its standing internal coherence against live, unresolved internal variations produced by territory.

Registration and resolution at the cellular order

Registration is automatic. When a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across the cell's boundary, the cell registers internal variation — membrane potential shifts, concentration changes, sensor-protein conformational changes. Registration is a structural consequence of coupling and occurs whether or not the cell's regulatory network produces a resolution.

Resolution is the cell's own. The cell's regulatory network resolves the registered variation against its ICC. The response is determined by the cell's current structural configuration and by the lineage-disclosed mechanisms the configuration includes. There is no choosability, no deliberation, no weighing of criteria. Resolution is automatic in the sense that it is fully determined by the cell's ICC; it is the cell's own in the sense that it proceeds from the cell's own structural criteria, not from anything imported from territory.

The distinction is structural. It applies to the cell as it applies to any entity. At the cellular order, the distinction does not introduce new capacities, but it clarifies that the cell's response to any encounter has two stages: the automatic registration, and the ICC-determined resolution. [Tier 1: signal transduction and gene regulation are confirmed automatic. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The cell's finitude

The cell's finitude is measured. Its capacity is limited by ATP budget, membrane surface area, ribosome count, genome size, diffusion rates, and metabolic throughput. Because capacity is finite, the cell cannot hold, process, or change positions across all dimensions simultaneously. Finitude forces compression and constrained organization. [Tier 1: metabolic and structural limits are confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Part III — The Cell's Dimensions

Constitutive dimensions: from cooperation among lower-order entities

The cell's constitutive dimensions emerge from cooperation among lower-order entities: macromolecules, supramolecular assemblies, and metabolic networks. Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain these lower-order entities' boundaries in a coherent relationship. Higher-order dimensions emerge from the boundary positions of the lower-order entities. [Tier 1: cellular integration of molecular subsystems is confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The cell's constitutive dimensions include:

· Genetic expression: Which proteins are produced, when, and in what quantities. Emerges from the cooperation of DNA, RNA polymerase, ribosomes, transcription factors, and regulatory RNAs.
· Metabolic regulation: Flux through metabolic pathways. Emerges from the cooperation of enzymes, substrates, cofactors, and allosteric regulators.
· Membrane integrity: Selective permeability, potential maintenance, lipid and protein composition. Emerges from the cooperation of phospholipids, membrane proteins, and the cytoskeletal-like MreB system in many bacteria.
· Replication fidelity: Genome maintenance across divisions. Emerges from the cooperation of DNA polymerase, mismatch repair, recombination machinery, and segregation systems.

These constitutive dimensions are genuine by emergence to the degree the lower-order constituents' resolutions are genuine. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Integrative dimensions: from dimensional integration

The cell integrates dimensions through horizontal expansion. When the cell is coupled to an external dimension through Genuine Coherence Criteria but cannot sustain internal coherence under the full spectrum of the external dimension's positions, the cell integrates the dimension. By integrating, the cell gains the capacity to navigate those variations from its internal resolution process, preventing capacity-limit failure. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

At the cellular order, integrative dimensions include:

· Ion regulation: Na⁺, K⁺, H⁺, Ca²⁺ concentrations and ratios, maintained through pumps, antiporters, and channels. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· Osmolarity: Internal solute concentration relative to external. Maintained through mechanosensitive channels, transport systems, and compatible-solute synthesis. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· pH regulation: Cytoplasmic and periplasmic pH. Maintained through proton pumps, antiporters, and buffer systems. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· Thermal tolerance: Maintenance of function across temperature ranges. Maintained through heat-shock proteins, membrane lipid adjustments, and cold-shock proteins. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· Nutrient acquisition: Uptake and assimilation of carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur, and trace metals. Maintained through transport systems, secreted enzymes, and siderophores. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· Redox management: Balance of oxidative and reductive states. Maintained through respiratory chains, antioxidant systems, and redox-sensitive regulators. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]
· Toxin and antibiotic resistance: Maintenance of function under chemical challenge. Maintained through efflux pumps, modifying enzymes, target modification, and permeability barriers. [Tier 1: confirmed biology.]

Integrative dimensions are where genuineness must be disclosed. The genuine criterion for each integrated dimension is pre-existing. The cell's lineage must disclose it through exploration. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

The boundary dimension

The cell's boundary dimension emerges as a result of the cell navigating coherently its constituent dimensions. The boundary dimension represents how the cell constrains and is constrained by external territory. Each position on this dimension corresponds to a complete configuration of all constituent dimensions at a single resolution, together with the coupling capacity that configuration confers: which Genuine Coherence Criteria can act on the cell, and how. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

From the cell's internal perspective, the boundary is its binding element. It represents the cell's state with its current position, determined by the coherent configuration of constituent dimensions.

From an external perspective, the boundary is the cell's interaction profile: the coupling capacities conferred by its current configuration. It becomes the component through which Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain the cell with external territory.

Boundary carriers at the cellular order

The framework's boundary dimension is not reducible to any single physical structure. At the cellular order, boundary functionality is carried by:

· the cytoplasmic membrane (the primary selective barrier);
· the cell wall (in most bacteria, and in archaea with distinct chemistry);
· the capsule or S-layer (in many species);
· transport proteins, pumps, and channels;
· secretion systems (Type I–VI in Gram-negatives);
· pili, fimbriae, and flagella (adhesion and motility);
· quorum-sensing receptors and signal-production systems;
· the outer membrane in Gram-negatives.

The boundary dimension is carried by all of these together. Removing any one reduces the boundary's functionality, but the boundary is not identical to any one carrier. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Part IV — The Boundary's Relational Dynamics at the Cellular Order

Incoming and outgoing constraining criteria

From the cell's perspective, there are incoming and outgoing constraining criteria stemming from Genuine Coherence Criteria acting across its boundary. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

Incoming constraining criteria require changes in the cell's internal configuration based on its internal coherence criteria. External territory acts on the cell's boundary through a shared Genuine Coherence Criterion, producing internal variations. The cell must resolve these variations against its ICC.

Outgoing constraining criteria require changes in external territory. The cell's boundary configuration constrains external entities' positions. The cell's interaction profile — secreted compounds, surface structures, metabolic byproducts — determines how Genuine Coherence Criteria act on other cells' boundaries.

Constraint states at the cellular order

The cell's outgoing and incoming constraining criteria can be in three states. Unlike at the organism order, these states are not chosen. They are determined by the cell's lineage-level structural configuration and by the cell's current state within that configuration. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Over-constrained criteria are criteria the cell expresses or admits beyond what its boundary coherence can sustain. Over-constrained outgoing criteria: the cell secretes or exposes compounds (toxins, antibiotics, signaling molecules, metabolic byproducts) that impose on other cells' territories beyond what the cell's own regulatory coherence supports. Over-constrained incoming criteria: the cell admits compounds or signals from territory without regulating the admission — leaky transport, uncontrolled uptake, unregulated signal reception.

Under-constrained criteria are criteria the cell fails to hold. Under-constrained outgoing criteria: the cell fails to express compounds or surface structures needed for its own coherence in its ecological position. Under-constrained incoming criteria: the cell admits territory without any selective constraint — membrane damage, loss of transport specificity, loss of regulatory feedback.

Regulated criteria are criteria the cell resolves coherently from its ICC. The cell's lineage has disclosed which mechanisms hold, and the individual cell's regulatory network maintains them. Regulated outgoing criteria: the cell expresses compounds or surface structures coherently with its own regulation and with the GCCs that couple it to territory. Regulated incoming criteria: the cell admits variation selectively, through its ICC.

The term "regulated" is used rather than "balanced" because at the cellular order there is no choosability. The cell does not balance competing criteria. It maintains a regulatory configuration that its lineage disclosed.

What fails is not the encounter but the resolution into a coherent configuration. The Seed's non-encounter case requires no GCC acting across boundaries. That case does not apply here, because all cells in contact share at least electromagnetic and chemical criteria.

Part V — Exploration at the Lineage Level

No representational threshold

The cell does not build a model of territory that requires choosability. Its ICC resolve automatically from its structure. The cell is not a chooser. It is a resolver. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

The Organism Aperture's representational threshold, choosability, Genuine Initial Stance, and balanced/conditioned configuration vocabulary do not apply at the cellular order. The cell's regulatory network resolves incoming variation without a choice-point.

The cell's resolution is automatic

When external variation acts on the cell's boundary, the cell's regulatory network resolves the variation through its ICC. The response is determined by the cell's current structural configuration and by the lineage-disclosed mechanisms the configuration includes. There is no choosability, no deliberation, no weighing of criteria. [Tier 1: signal transduction, gene regulation, and metabolic feedback are confirmed automatic. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Exploration occurs at lineage level

The cell's ICC are the current state of a lineage-level exploration. The individual cell does not disclose. The lineage does. Variation among cells produces different configurations; selection among cells determines which configurations persist; the persisting configurations are the lineage's disclosed criteria. [Tier 1: variation and selection are confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Where this document says "the cell integrates a dimension" or "the cell's criteria drift," the individual cell is the site where the integration or drift is expressed, but the exploration that produced it occurred at lineage level.

Horizontal gene transfer as installation

Horizontal gene transfer — plasmid acquisition, transformation, transduction, conjugation — is the cellular-order structural analogue of installation. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

A gene acquired by HGT introduces a criterion (a regulatory or catalytic function) that was not resolved against the recipient cell's own contradictions. The gene is installed. Whether the installation proves coherent or produces drift depends on whether the acquired function fits the recipient's existing structural configuration.

When HGT installs a function that coheres with the recipient's ICC, the lineage stabilizes on it. When HGT installs a function that does not cohere, the lineage pays a cost — reduced fitness, regulatory conflict, metabolic burden — and selection removes it or compensates around it. The lineage's exploration continues.

The distinction between individual resolution and lineage exploration

At the cellular order, two processes operate at different levels:

· Individual resolution: the individual cell resolves each encounter through its ICC. This is automatic, fast, and does not involve exploration.
· Lineage exploration: the lineage explores configurations across generations through variation and selection. This is slow and produces the ICC the individual cell inherits.

The framework applies at both levels, but the terms "disclosure," "installation," and "drift" apply primarily at the lineage level. The individual cell expresses the lineage's current state.

Part VI — Criteria Drift, Disclosure, and Installation at the Cellular Order

Criteria drift

Internal coherence criteria may drift from Genuine Coherence Criteria, gradually or suddenly. At the cellular order, drift occurs when a lineage stabilizes on a criterion that is not resolved against the relevant territorial constraints. The individual cell expresses the drift; the lineage carries it. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

As drift increases, resolution may become increasingly costly, depleting finite capacity. The cell resolves positions from drifted criteria. The positions may be internally consistent but misaligned with the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

Routes to drift at the cellular order

1. Mis-disclosure. The lineage explores but stabilizes on a criterion close to, but not identical with, the Genuine Coherence Criterion. Example: a transport system that maintains ion balance at low external concentrations but fails at high ones, which the lineage has not yet encountered.
2. Installation. The lineage adopts a criterion via HGT without resolving it against the cell's own structural contradictions. Example: a plasmid-borne antibiotic resistance gene that confers resistance but imposes a metabolic burden the cell's regulatory network does not accommodate.
3. Regulatory capture. A pathway that was once regulated by coherent feedback becomes decoupled from its inputs, producing positions that no longer track the GCC. Example: a mutation that disables a feedback-inhibited enzyme, producing constitutive flux through a pathway.

Drift primarily enters through integrative dimensions, because those are the dimensions where the cell must resolve previously external or disruptive variation into internal coherence. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Disclosure

Disclosure at the cellular order is lineage-level. The lineage discloses a criterion when exploration has produced a configuration that coherently resolves the relevant territorial variation, and selection has stabilized it. The criterion was not created by the exploration; the exploration revealed a pre-existing constraint. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

Re-disclosure

A lineage that has drifted can re-disclose the genuine criterion through continued exploration. Reversion, compensatory mutation, or re-acquisition of a lost function are the cellular-order expressions of re-disclosure. [Tier 1: reversion and compensatory evolution are confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The structural analogue of control

At the cellular order, the structural analogue of control occurs when one cell's criteria determine another cell's resolution. Examples include:

· Quorum sensing: a signal-producing cell's state determines the receiving cell's gene expression.
· Parasitic manipulation: a parasite's gene products alter the host cell's regulatory state.
· Predation and lysis: one cell's secreted compounds determine another cell's capacity to maintain its boundary.

The cellular analogue of control is structural, not psychological. The controlled cell resolves positions from the controlling cell's criteria, not from its own ICC. The controlled cell's Self-Referential Instrument is no longer self-referential for the duration of the coupling. [Tier 1: quorum sensing, parasitism, and predation are confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The organism-order term "control" was developed with choosability as its reference point. At the cellular order, the structural analogue is present, but the cell does not choose to submit or resist. The submission or resistance is determined by the cell's ICC and by the lineage's history.

Part VII — Cooperation at the Cellular Order

Cooperation as genuine mutual constraint

Cooperation at the cellular order occurs when Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple cells' boundaries in a coherent relationship. A higher-order dimension set emerges from the boundary positions of the cells forming the relationship. These higher-order dimensions are constitutive dimensions of the higher-order entity. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

Cooperation requires:

1. Genuine Coherence Criteria acting across multiple cells' boundaries.
2. The resulting configurations surviving the additional constraints of each cell's ICC.
3. The cells' ICC being genuine (lineage-disclosed, not installed without resolution).

Where cells' ICC are drifted, cooperation does not occur in the coherent sense. The drifted criteria do not survive contact. The cells fall into the complementarity of imbalance: over-constrained outgoing criteria meeting under-constrained incoming criteria, or vice versa. The coupling is non-genuine.

Cooperation at the cellular order: examples

· Biofilm formation. Cells coordinate through quorum sensing, extracellular matrix production, and spatial organization. Higher-order dimensions — gradient structure, matrix composition, collective metabolic state — emerge from the collective configuration and cannot be navigated by a single cell independently. [Tier 1: biofilm biology is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]
· Syntrophy. Two or more cell types exchange metabolites in a way that neither can sustain alone. The metabolic relationship produces dimensions — interspecies metabolite flux, redox balance across the community — that emerge from the relationship. [Tier 1: syntrophy is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]
· Quorum sensing. Collective behavior emerges from signal exchange. Population density, signal concentration, and collective gene expression are higher-order dimensions. [Tier 1: quorum sensing is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Endosymbiosis as a paradigm case

Endosymbiosis is the paradigm case of cooperation at the cellular order producing a durable higher-order entity. A prokaryote is taken up by a host cell, and over evolutionary time the relationship stabilizes: the prokaryote becomes an organelle (mitochondrion, chloroplast), its genome is reduced, and its functions are integrated into the host's regulatory network. [Tier 1: endosymbiotic theory is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

In framework terms:

· The prokaryote's boundary is modularized. It becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of the host's aperture. Its positions are dually constrained — by its own reduced ICC and by the host's criteria.
· The host's aperture expands vertically. The relationship produces constitutive dimensions (organellar function, host-organelle signaling, division coordination) that neither cell could navigate alone.
· The loop closes. The prokaryote no longer exists as an independent entity at the cellular order; it is a module within the eukaryotic cell.

This is the clearest biological case of a cooperation event producing a new order. It also illustrates the general principle: modularization preserves constituent identity and constitutive structure, not independent state. The mitochondrion retains its own genome and membrane, but it does not retain independent regulatory autonomy.

[Tier 1 for the biology; Tier 2 for the framework mapping.]

Modularization at the cellular order

Modularization is the process by which a cell's boundary dimension, with the configuration it expresses, becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of a higher-order aperture. At the cellular order, this occurs when a cell becomes a module in a biofilm, a tissue, an organ, or an endosymbiotic relationship.

Modularization is vertical — the cell contributes to a higher order. Dimensional integration is horizontal — the cell gains control over a new dimension within its own order.

Part VIII — Cross-Scale Dynamics at the Cellular Order

The loop is one, expanded vertically

The cell's constitutive dimensions emerge from cooperation among lower-order entities (macromolecules, supramolecular assemblies, metabolic networks). The loop that closes among these constituents is not a separate condition from the higher-order dimensions that emerge. The higher-order constitutive dimensions are the coherent expression of that same closed cycle at the higher order. The loop is one, expanded vertically, with each constituent's own loop nested within it as a module. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

Because the loop is one, coherence at the cellular order is not independent of coherence at lower and higher orders. A loss of coherence at the cellular order alters what lower-order modules can resolve. A loss of coherence at lower orders alters what the cellular order can resolve. Coherence propagates in both directions through boundary positions.

Bottom-up disruption

A lower-order module loses coherence — a protein misfolds, a membrane component degrades, a metabolic intermediate accumulates to toxic levels. Its boundary position changes. Because the cell's loop depends on that boundary position as substrate, the cell's constitutive dimensions re-resolve or fail to close.

This is not a signal traveling upward through a hierarchy. It is the automatic consequence of nested loop dependence. The higher-order relation was constituted by the lower-order interaction profile; when that profile changes, the relation changes.

Top-down disruption

A higher-order loop fragments. Its constitutive dimensions no longer provide coherent constraint to lower-order boundaries. At the cellular order, this means the cell receives contradictory or unstable positional demands from the tissue, organ, or community loop it is a module of.

The cell responds as it would to any external variation: by resolving through its ICC. If the fragmented higher-order signals cannot be reconciled, the cell may preserve local coherence by altering its coupling profile, closing channels, or stabilizing on protective but narrowing configurations. This further prevents the higher-order loop from closing.

Interaction-profile propagation rule

Because higher-order loops read lower-order constituents through boundary interaction profiles, cross-scale disruption should travel through changes in the interaction profile.

A lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact should not register above.

Example: a silent mutation that leaves protein function unchanged does not alter the molecular interaction profile in a way that propagates to cellular coherence. [Tier 1: the biology is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

This gives the framework a testable structural consequence at the cellular order.

The cell as a module in higher-order loops

The cell is simultaneously an entity at the cellular order and a modularized constituent of higher-order loops — biofilms, tissues, organs, organisms, symbiotic relationships. It does not cease being an entity at its own order. It can occupy positions at multiple orders simultaneously, which is what the recursion requires. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Part IX — Failure Modes at the Cellular Order

Capacity-Limit Failure

The cell cannot occupy a coherent dimensional position due to lack of finite capacity. The cell's finitude is exceeded. The configuration is destroyed. No compensation was running; the capacity is simply exceeded. [Tier 1: lysis under osmotic shock, starvation, thermal death, and radiation damage are confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Capacity-limit failure is a classification of contact, not of coherence. The cell's ICC may be genuine and its resolution mechanism may be accurate, but the perturbation exceeds the cell's finite structural limits. The cell did not lose coherence; it was overwhelmed.

Loss of Fidelity

Loss of fidelity during positional resolution produces occasional or consistent loss of coherence that may cause the cell's finite capacity depletion. Fidelity is the integrity of the coupling between the position the cell's ICC prescribe and the position the cell actually resolves. Fidelity is a property of the resolution mechanism, not of the criteria themselves. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

At the cellular order, loss of fidelity occurs through two etiologies:

Material etiology: Protein misfolding, membrane damage, DNA damage, ribosome malfunction, enzyme inactivation, sensor degradation. The resolution mechanism is damaged or overwhelmed. The cell cannot resolve accurately because the mechanism is impaired. Remedy: mechanism repair (chaperone response, membrane repair, DNA repair, protein turnover). [Tier 1: these mechanisms are confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework classification.]

Systemic etiology: Cross-scale noise, contradictory signaling from higher-order loops. The resolution mechanism receives contradictory inputs and cannot resolve a coherent position. The cell's resolution is disrupted by fragmented higher-order signals. Remedy: governance restoration — restoring coherent signaling from the higher-order loop. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

Loss of Coherence Criteria Integrity

Internal coherence criteria may drift from the aperture's Genuine Coherence Criteria, gradually or suddenly. As drift increases, resolution becomes increasingly costly, depleting finite capacity. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from Seed.]

At the cellular order, loss of coherence criteria integrity is the lineage-level expression of criteria drift. The lineage's integrative dimensions are drifted. The individual cell resolves from drifted criteria. The positions are internally consistent but misaligned with the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

The consequence is the same as loss of fidelity: the cell's coherence degrades. But the cause is different. Loss of fidelity is a failure of the resolution mechanism. Loss of coherence criteria integrity is a failure of the criteria themselves.

The cancer case at the cellular order

Cancer is the paradigm case of loss of coherence criteria integrity at the cellular order. [Tier 1: cancer biology is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping, with the lineage-level adjustment.]

A cell lineage within a multicellular organism drifts from the criteria that keep it coherent with the tissue. Its boundary profile changes — receptors for growth-suppressing signals are lost, proliferative signaling becomes constitutive. The cell stops resolving coherently with its neighbors. The tissue-level loop can no longer resolve the former coherent configuration. Tissue-level coherence degrades.

The drift occurs at lineage level: clonal variation and selection among the cell's descendants favor variants that proliferate regardless of tissue signals. The individual cell expresses the drifted criteria; the lineage carries them.

Higher-order incoherence does not create the deviant cell, but it can change which variants persist. This is a Tier 3 projection: the tissue environment influences which drifted lineages expand.

Part XI — Epistemic Posture

Territory agnosticism

This document does not require a settled answer to what territory is prior to encounter. Territory is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the Genuine Coherence Criteria constraining them. The cell navigates only its internal registrations of territory, not territory itself.

The order limitation

The cell, at the cellular order, cannot confirm what the next order's transition actually requires. It also cannot reliably distinguish developments that constitute the leading edge of that transition from developments that merely resemble it.

Two senses of scaffold

Scaffold-holding is a stance toward one's own compression: genuinely held, genuinely revisable, genuinely current.

Scaffold as an epistemic status names a claim implied by the architecture but not yet independently checkable.

The current status

The architecture described here has not yet failed a check it was run against. That is the entirety of the claim this document is prepared to stand behind.

This framework is a scaffold. It welcomes coherent additions from any dimension, provided they survive the same check applied everywhere else: would this addition still hold if generated fresh against what is actually here?
