
# The Perpetuation Framework — Seed

## Dimension, Position, and Coherence

### Dimension

A **dimension** is an axis of variation, and a position is a specific value along that axis.

**Coherence criteria** are the constraining rules that describe how positions across dimensions can coexist without contradicting each other.

### Coherence

**Coherence** is the regime in which positions across multiple dimensions coexist, mutually constraining each other under coherence criteria. 

### Genuine Coherence Criteria

**Genuine Coherence Criteria** are coherence criteria existing as structural law. They are not invented, constructed, or emergent. They are structural features of reality that constrain how dimensions can relate and coexist.

Genuine Coherence Criteria are bidirectional: a criterion that relates positions on one dimension to positions on another constrains both in return, without any side dominating the relationship.

## Finitude

**Finitude** is the condition of possessing limited capacity.

Finitude is what makes structure necessary. Because capacity is finite, positions cannot be held, processed, and changed across all dimensions simultaneously. Finitude forces compression and constrained organization, making stable structural patterns necessary for coherent dimensional navigation.

## Aperture and Aperture Template

### Aperture

An **aperture** is constituted by a set of dimensions whose positions are mutually constrained by Genuine Coherence Criteria. Each dimension's positions are determined by their relations to positions in the other dimensions — no position is independently fixed; each is constrained by the whole. The aperture is the space of coherent configurations available within those dimensions: the set of configurations in which all criteria are simultaneously satisfied. Because each dimension's positions depend on the others, and the others depend on it in return, the mutual constraint closes into a loop: a cycle of determination in which no position can vary without the others re-resolving. 

### Aperture Template

An **aperture template** is a class of coherent configurations within an aperture.

Because of finitude, positions cannot vary across the full dimensional space simultaneously. Therefore, a template fixes a subset of the aperture’s dimensions as stable positions and leaves another subset open to constrained variation. The fixed dimensions act as parameters; the free dimensions vary only within the remaining constraint structure. An aperture template has stability without absolute stability, and it does not necessarily navigate all aperture dimensions.

The diversity of aperture templates may represent the exploration of coherent configurations within the aperture space.

## Territory and entity

### Territory
**Territory** is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the genuine coherence criteria constraining them.

### Entity
An entity is an instantiation of an aperture template. While an aperture represents all positional variations an entity may occupy, an entity does not vary across all dimensional space due to finitude.

Since an entity inherits the aperture’s dimensional configuration, it cannot hold two independent positions along the same dimension for the same coherent configuration.

An entity instantiates an aperture configuration and resolves its positions through internal coherence criteria that may remain aligned with, or diverge from, the aperture's Genuine Coherence Criteria.

An entity is also a **Self-Referential Instrument**, because its dimensional positions are resolved from its internal coherence criteria.

Additionally, an entity is in live encounter with external territory. Live encounter occurs when a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across the boundary of an entity and any dimensional configuration of external territory. Without such a criterion, no encounter occurs.

## Boundary Dynamics and Internal Coherence Updating

As a result of the entity navigating coherently the set of constituent dimensions, a single **boundary** dimension emerges that represents how the entity constrains and is constrained by external territory.

Each position on this dimension corresponds to a complete configuration of all constituent dimensions at a single resolution, together with the coupling capacity that configuration confers: which GCCs can act on the entity, and how. The boundary dimension's positions are pairs: a configuration and its interaction profile. 

A genuine coherence criterion acting across two boundaries constrains both entities. Asymmetries lie in capacity and magnitude, not in the direction of constraint.

From one entity's perspective there may be incoming and outgoing constraining criteria stemming from a GCC. Incoming constraining criteria may require changes of entity's internal configuration based on internal coherence. Outgoing constraining criteria may require changes in external territory. Further changes may be required at both sides as entities update configurations due to additional GCC acting on any  boundary constituting the relationship.

The boundary dimension is ontologically dependent: it is defined to the degree that its constituent dimensions are simultaneously coherent. As constituents lose coherence the boundary becomes progressively ill-defined; if coherence is lost entirely, it ceases to be defined and the entity no longer exists as such.

From the entity's internal perspective, the boundary is its binding element. It represents the entity state with its current position, determined by the coherent configuration of constituent dimensions.

From an external perspective, the boundary is the entity's interaction profile: the coupling capacities conferred by its current configuration. It becomes the component through which Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain the entity with external territory.

The boundary is not merely a property of the entity. It is the structural determinant of its relational dynamics. When multiple entities couple, their respective boundaries mutually constrain.
 
Entities are non-constraining when no GCC acts across their boundaries. In that case there is no encounter, and neither entity's positions are altered by the other.

The boundary is thus both the internal unity of the entity and its external coupling profile. It is the dimension whose positions determine what the entity is and what it can do, in relation to any other entity whose boundary a GCC acts across. 

Genuine Coherence Criteria may cause internal entity variations as a consequence of external variations, through the boundary, and the entity additionally reorganizes its positional configuration according to internal coherence criteria.

Multiple related outcomes may occur.

### Boundary's Relational Dynamics

One possible outcome is that the entity cannot find an internal configuration satisfying both the internal coherence criteria and the internal changes caused by external variation. The entity would then be incoherent with the source of external variation because there is a contradiction between the external genuine coherence criteria, and the internal resolution it demands.

What follows depends on the entity's capacity and the nature of its relationship with the source of variation. Outcomes fall into these categories:

· Entity preserves its internal coherence criteria.
· Entity updates its internal coherence criteria, installing external criteria and degrading relative to Genuine Coherence.
· Entity updates its internal coherence criteria through its own resolution of the positions the encounter signals, repairing or regaining internal coherence relative to Genuine Coherence.

### Dimensional Integration

When an entity is coupled to an external dimension through genuine coherence criteria but cannot sustain internal coherence under the full spectrum of the external dimension's positions, the entity may integrate the dimension. By integrating the dimension, the entity gains the capacity to navigate those variations from its internal resolution process, preventing capacity-limit failure. The positions of those variations emerge from the entity's internal coherence, and the dimension no longer occupies disrupting positions. This means, by integrating a new dimension, the entity gained stabilization and adaptability. The entity may still need to find the pre-existing Genuine Coherence Criterion when no encounter has provided it.

Integrating a dimension adds it to the entity's constituent set, so the boundary dimension reorganizes: its position space extends to include the new dimension, and the entity's coupling capacity changes accordingly.

New dimensions may be internally resolved and integrated into the entity, this means the entity incorporating internal mechanisms that produce the positioning, enabling the capacity to coherently navigate this new kind of variation, and keep internal variations independent from unexpected, uncontrolled and potentially disrupting external variation.

In this scenario, coherence between the existing dimensional structure and the dimension being integrated emerges from diversity of positional exploration, and the constraint rules that regulate positions along the new axis are disclosed through exploration—from the contradictions among occupied positions and the space of entity configurations under existing internal coherence criteria. The genuine criterion is not emergent; what emerges is the entity's internalization of it. If the current internal coherence criteria are genuine, the aperture may expand horizontally with the newly resolved dimension and updated coherence criteria. The aperture will integrate the resolved dimension and the new coherence criteria only if the Genuine Coherence Criterion is disclosed through an exploration process.

Another possibility may be the entity's internal coherence criteria holding to the external positional variations. In this case, the entity is coherent with the source of external variation, and it may engage in a cooperation with other entities while the encounter lasts.

## Higher Order Dimensions, Cooperation and Order.

If genuine coherence criteria constrain multiple entities, and the resulting configurations survive the additional constraints of each entity's internal coherence criteria, a new set of higher order dimensions emerge from the relationship. A higher order dimension position depends on, is coherent with, and will emerge from every lower order entity boundary position, and cannot be navigated by constituents independently.

This scenario is different than dimensional integration. In dimensional integration, positions of new dimension are controlled by the entity, in this scenario higher order dimensional positions emerge from the boundary positions of the lower order that constitute the relationship. These higher order dimensions are **constitutive dimensions** of higher order entities.

**Cooperation** occurs when Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple entities' boundaries in a coherent relationship. A higher order dimension set emerges from the lower order boundaries that form part of the relationship. These higher order dimensions are constrained by the genuine coherence criteria that initiated the interplay. The constituent entities are modularized as an outcome of the process.

Modularization is the process by which an entity's boundary dimension, with the configuration it expresses, becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of a higher order aperture. The entity's positions are dually constrained (by its own criteria and the relationship's genuine criteria), serve as the substrate from which higher order dimensions emerge, and become available for instantiation by new templates without re-resolution. Because the interaction-profile component is what Genuine Coherence Criteria act on, higher-order positions are functions of that component only.

Modularization is vertical — the entity contributes to a higher order. Dimensional integration is horizontal — the entity gains control over a new dimension within its own order.

The participating aperture templates expand into a single aperture template that incorporates the new set of emerged dimensions when the configurations resulting from the relationship satisfy all constraints, which is to say when the loop closes.

The loop that closes among constituents is not a separate condition from the higher-order dimensions that emerge. The higher-order constitutive dimensions are the coherent expression of that same closed cycle at the higher order. The mutual coherence between lower-order boundary positions and higher-order dimensions is therefore not a second loop but the same loop described from the higher-order perspective. Because all constituents—lower-order boundary positions and higher-order constitutive dimensions alike—are mutually constrained, no position in the set is independently fixed. The loop is one, expanded vertically, with each constituent's own loop nested within it as a module.

As the participating apertures expand, entities may inherit the expanded aperture, within which modularized constituents are available as stabilized substrate. Individual templates may instantiate subsets of those modules.

The emerged aperture, dimensions and instantiated entities navigating them, sit at a newly emerged higher **order**.

## Internal coherence criteria drift

Higher order dimensions arising from cooperation are governed by Genuine Coherence Criteria, because they emerge from constituents genuinely constrained by them; the configurations realized are sound to the degree the constituents' internal coherence is. 

Internal coherence may drift when an entity integrates a dimension without disclosing the genuine criterion. Axis exploration settles on a distorted criterion, that is installed without being resolved against the entity's own contradictions.

Since integrative dimensions are resolved by an entity's internal mechanisms, the opposite scenario is also possible: re-resolving an already integrated dimension by disclosing the genuine criterion, which replaces the distorted one.
The disclosure may be catalyzed by an encounter with another entity that has already resolved the criterion. The external entity signals positions to test; the entity resolves them against its own contradictions, through its own resolution process, before adopting the criterion. The criterion is disclosed by the entity, not supplied to it.

## Fidelity

**Fidelity** is the integrity of the coupling between the position the entity’s internal coherence criteria prescribe and the position the entity actually resolves. It is a property of the resolution mechanism, not of the criteria themselves. 

## Generativity

**Generativity** is the continuous emergence of new orders through multidimensional coherence.

### Generative Mechanism
The process is recursive. 
First, same-aperture dimensions relate through genuine coherence criteria, constituting an entity with a boundary that expresses the entity state and holds its relational capabilities.

Then, genuine coherence criteria constrain multiple entities into a relationship, constituting a new order, where the process runs recursively on the new higher order dimensional configuration emerged from the relationship.

This makes the architecture both horizontal and vertical. Horizontal coherence organizes dimensions into entities, and vertical coherence expands apertures into new orders, becoming substrate for further emergence.

Aperture may expand horizontally by incorporating integrative dimensions, and vertically by producing constitutive dimensions.

At this point we can identify that the dimensions horizontally related in the first step of the recursive process are not boundaries of entities, but emerged higher order dimensions from the previous recursive step, and they can be expanded with integrative dimensions — except at the first level, where the nature of the constituent dimensions is left open. The lower-order boundaries remain in the loop as modules; the higher-order entity's own constituent dimensions are the emerged ones.

The bidirectionality of Genuine Coherence Criteria is what allows mutual constraint to close into a loop, since a one-way constraint has no return path. The same kind of loop operates at every step of the recursion: among the dimensions of an aperture, among the boundaries of entities in a relationship, and among those boundaries and the higher-order dimensions that emerge from them. At each step the loop is expanded, not replaced. Because positions at every order are mutually constrained within the expanded loop, coherence at one order is not independent of coherence at the others. A loss of coherence at any order alters what the other orders can resolve, in both directions, through the boundary positions that couple them. How far such effects propagate depends on each constituent's capacity and internal coherence criteria.

### First-Level Entities
The recursion has a starting point only if there are entities whose constituent dimensions did not emerge from the boundaries of lower-order entities. The Seed leaves open whether such a first level exists and what its constituent dimensions are. A first-level entity is defined like any other entity, with an instantiated template, a boundary and encounters through Genuine Coherence Criteria, except that its constituent dimensions are not constitutive dimensions. Whether its internal coherence criteria can diverge from the genuine ones is also left open. If they cannot, the entity cannot drift, integrate dimensions or lose fidelity, no contradiction can arise between its criteria and what Genuine Coherence Criteria induce in it.

### Generativity Preconditions
1. Fidelity of each constituent’s internal resolution process. 
2. Each constituent must be a self-referential instrument with genuine internal coherence.
3. Genuine coherence criteria across all lower-order constituents.

## Failure

### Capacity-Limit Failure  
Entity cannot occupy a coherent dimensional position due to lack of finite capacity.

### Loss of Fidelity  
Loss of fidelity during positional resolution produces occasional or consistent loss of coherence that may cause the entity's finite capacity depletion.

### Loss of Coherence Criteria Integrity
Internal coherence criteria may drift from the aperture's genuine criteria. Drift may develop gradually or suddenly; as it increases, resolution becomes increasingly costly, depleting finite capacity.
