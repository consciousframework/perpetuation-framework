## Part I — Scope and Vocabulary

### What this document houses

This document instantiates the Seed's architecture at the molecular order. It covers:

- The molecule as an entity: its aperture template, constitutive dimensions, and boundary dimension.
- Its constitutive dimensions across two regimes: small-molecule and macromolecular.
- The status of integrative dimensions at this order, held open.
- The absence of a representational threshold, choosability, and PF resonance at this order.
- Registration and resolution at the molecular order.
- The boundary's relational dynamics: direct and messenger-mediated coupling, chained and mutual topology.
- Cooperation at the molecular order and the distinction between coupling and higher-order entity.
- Cross-scale dynamics: the molecule as a module in higher-order loops.
- State transitions, reactions, template transitions, dissociation, and capacity-limit.
- The persistence ≠ representation distinction and pattern transmission below representation.

This document is molecular-order specific. It does not house order-general content.

### The methodological principle

**PF classifies what molecular chemistry exhibits; it does not make molecular chemistry behave like PF's higher-order entities.**

The molecular order is where the temptation to read agency into chemistry is strongest. Molecular "recognition," "signaling," and "response" are terms of art that must be handled carefully. Throughout this document, physical substrate claims (Tier 1) are separated from PF mappings (Tier 2). Where the framework uses vocabulary that carries organismic or computational connotations, the document states explicitly that these are PF classifications of physical processes, not claims about internal computation, deliberation, recognition, or choice.

The framework's value at the molecular order is not to add to what chemistry already says. It is to map the chemistry onto the framework's structure so that the structure can be tested across orders. That mapping is legitimate as long as the PF labels are clearly marked as classification, not as physical mechanism.

### Position on the first-level and order questions

The Atomic Aperture established that atoms are not first-level. This document extends that position: **the molecular order is likewise not first-level.** Molecules have atomic constituents, and their constitutive dimensions are candidates for emergence from cooperation among those atoms under electromagnetic and exchange criteria.

The molecular order has **two regimes**, not sub-orders:

- The **small-molecule regime**: molecules with well-defined templates and discrete state spaces (H₂O, CH₄, benzene, retinal, ATP, amino acids, nucleotides, and similar).
- The **macromolecular regime**: molecules with sequence-defined templates and large conformational state spaces (proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides, and similar).

The term "regime" is used rather than "sub-order" because introducing a sub-order implies a PF order transition, and whether the transition to macromolecules constitutes an order transition is not established. The two regimes have different structural features, and the framework names the difference without prejudging the order question.

Whether the macromolecular regime warrants its own document is left open. This document treats both regimes within the molecular order, with the structural differences named where they matter.

### Vocabulary note

**The molecular aperture** is the space of coherent configurations available to a given molecular template under the constraints of the electromagnetic, exchange, and thermodynamic criteria that govern the molecule's constituents. [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The molecular template** is a class of coherent molecular configurations. The template fixes the molecule's identity — composition, connectivity, and (in chiral and sequence-defined molecules) stereochemistry and sequence — and leaves the molecule's state free to vary within the constraint structure. [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The molecule's boundary dimension** is the emergent dimension representing how the molecule constrains and is constrained by external territory. Its positions are pairs: (configuration, interaction profile). [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The molecule's constitutive dimensions** are candidates for higher-order dimensional emergence from the molecule's atomic constituents' cooperation. They are candidates, not established, until an independent criterion shows that the emergent structure corresponds to GCC rather than merely being a useful description. [PF mapping, Tier 3.]

**The molecule's ICC** denote, in PF mapping, the locally instantiated constraint structure through which molecular configurations are resolved under the governing physical relations. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as ICC at biological orders remains open. [PF mapping, Tier 2/3.]

### Five-way distinction used throughout

To avoid conflating categories, this document uses five distinct terms:

- **GCC** — structural physical constraints (conservation laws, quantum postulates, interaction symmetries).
- **Molecular ICC** — PF's abstraction of how those constraints are instantiated in a particular molecular organization.
- **Template-defining dimensions** — composition, connectivity, sequence, stereochemistry; what identifies the molecule.
- **State dimensions** — conformation, protonation, electronic state, vibrational state, ligand occupancy; the molecule's actual variable configuration.
- **Interaction profile** — what aspects of the state participate in coupling with other entities.

Physical descriptions such as potential energy surfaces, Hamiltonians, normal modes, selection rules, and conformational landscapes are **compasses** toward the constraint structure. They are mathematical representations under specified approximations. They are not identical to GCC or ICC.

This document uses no volitional primitive. Bond formation, isomerization, conformational change, allosteric transitions, and folding are described structurally through encounter, registration, resolution, boundary modulation, and the outcomes the Seed defines.

## Part II — The Molecule as Entity

### The aperture template

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule is a bound configuration of nuclei and electrons whose structure is determined by the electromagnetic interaction, the Pauli exclusion principle, and (for large molecules) additional constraints from the surrounding environment.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule is an entity: an instantiation of an aperture template at the molecular order. Its aperture is constituted by a set of dimensions whose positions are mutually constrained by Genuine Coherence Criteria, and the mutual constraint closes into a loop.

The template fixes a subset of dimensions as template-defining and leaves the state dimensions open to constrained variation. The template-defining dimensions are composition, connectivity, and — where relevant — sequence and stereochemistry. The state dimensions are bond lengths and angles, vibrational excitation, rotational state, electronic configuration, conformational state, protonation state, and ligand occupancy.

Because the molecule is not first-level, the template is a candidate for emergence from the molecule's atomic constituents' cooperation. The template-defining dimensions are themselves candidates for being the stabilized outcome of that cooperation. Whether the atomic organization produces the template in the strong PF sense — involving relational irreducibility — is left open.

### The Self-Referential Instrument

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule is a Self-Referential Instrument in the PF sense. Its dimensional positions are resolved from its internal coherence criteria. The molecule does not compare incoming variation against a fixed position. Its state evolution is constrained by its own governing structure: the encountered variation produces a new physically allowed state under the molecule's constraint structure, or it produces a state the constraint structure cannot accommodate.

The molecule's reference is not a position. In PF mapping, the molecule's reference is represented by the constraints governing which state transitions are physically available from its current configuration.

This PF formulation does not imply computation, evaluation, or choice. A molecular system evolves according to its Hamiltonian and relevant interactions. The PF term "Self-Referential Instrument" is the framework's name for the structure whose state evolution is constrained by itself. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as self-referential resolution at higher orders remains open.

### The Interiority Principle

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule does not navigate external territory directly. It navigates the internal variations produced in it by external territory impacting its boundary through at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion. The molecule's Self-Referential Instrument operates entirely on this internal registration substrate.

The molecule's boundary carriers — the electron distribution, the vibrational modes, the rotational states, the reactive sites, and for macromolecules the binding pockets and conformational ensemble — are the structures through which external variation is registered internally. The molecule does not compare its ICC to "the outside world." In PF mapping, its state transitions are constrained by its own structure under the encountered conditions.

### Registration and resolution

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** When an external interaction acts on the molecule, the molecule's state changes in ways determined by its Hamiltonian, the interaction term, and the relevant environment. The resulting state may be a new stationary state, a superposition, a reaction product, a conformational redistribution, or a scattering state.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).**

**Registration** is the PF name for the automatic production of internal variation in the molecule when a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across its boundary. Registration is a structural consequence of coupling and occurs whether or not the molecule's constraint structure produces a new configuration.

**Resolution** is the PF name for the constraint-governed state transition that follows. The molecule's state evolves under its constraint structure. In PF terms, the molecule "resolves" the registered variation by transitioning to a new physically allowed state, or by failing to find a state that accommodates the variation.

The PF formulation does not imply computation, comparison, or deliberation.

Where the encountered variation is accommodated: the molecule transitions to an excited vibrational state, a shifted conformational population, a change in protonation state, a bond rotation, a reaction product, or a binding event.

Where it is not: outcomes depend on the molecule's structural capacity and the nature of the interaction. Dissociation, fragmentation, or capacity-limit may follow.

### Finitude

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule has a bounded number of constituents, discrete allowed quantum numbers, finite binding energies, finite state lifetimes, and finite coupling bandwidths under specified conditions. Molecules can have continua above dissociation thresholds; formal state spaces can be enormous or, for idealized models, effectively infinite.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The PF interpretation reads these physical bounds as compression: the molecule's accessible configuration space is constrained, and its transitions are constrained within it. The PF concept of "finitude" here is **bounded accessible state space** — the structural limits on what states the molecule can occupy and what transitions it can undergo. It is not computational finitude in the sense used at the organism or AI orders.

The PF reading of physical bounds as compression is a Tier 2 mapping. It does not claim that the molecule has a finite processing capacity.

## Part III — The Molecule's Dimensions

### Template-defining dimensions

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** A molecule's identity is determined by its atomic composition, its connectivity (which atoms are bonded to which, with what bond orders), and — for chiral molecules — its stereochemistry, and — for sequence-defined molecules — its monomer sequence.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** These are template-defining. They are not axes of variation that the molecule occupies during ordinary dynamics; they are the identity conditions that make the molecule a molecule of a particular kind. A change in the template-defining dimensions is a change of template, not a re-resolution.

Template-defining dimensions include:

- **Composition:** which elements, in what proportions.
- **Connectivity:** the bonding graph.
- **Stereochemistry:** handedness, where relevant.
- **Sequence:** monomer order, for sequence-defined molecules.

### State dimensions

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** A molecule's state varies within the template. Bond lengths and angles fluctuate; vibrational modes are excited or not; rotational states are populated; electronic configurations vary; conformations redistribute; protonation states change; ligands bind and release.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** These are state dimensions. They are axes of variation that the molecule occupies during its dynamics. A change in state dimensions is a re-resolution within the same template, not a template change.

State dimensions include:

- **Molecular geometry:** bond lengths, bond angles, dihedral angles.
- **Vibrational state:** which normal modes are excited, with what energy.
- **Rotational state:** the molecule's rotation as a whole.
- **Electronic state:** which orbitals are occupied.
- **Conformational state:** the distribution across local minima of the state-space structure.
- **Protonation state:** the protonation of titratable groups.
- **Ligand occupancy:** which ligands are bound, at which sites.

### The status of physical descriptions

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** The potential energy surface, the vibrational normal modes, the electronic structure, and the conformational landscape are **compasses** toward the constraint structure. They are mathematical representations under specified approximations (Born–Oppenheimer separation, harmonic approximation, effective solvation models, and so on). They are not identical to GCC or to molecular ICC.

This is consistent with the Conceptual Depth's treatment of equations as compasses toward Genuine Coherence Criteria. The same structural constraint may admit multiple mathematical representations depending on coordinates, scale, approximation, formalism, and boundary conditions.

### Constitutive dimensions (candidate)

**PF mapping (Tier 2, with Tier 3 candidate status).** The molecule's constitutive dimensions are candidates for higher-order dimensional emergence from the molecule's atomic constituents' cooperation. They are candidates, not established, until an independent criterion shows that the emergent structure corresponds to GCC rather than merely being a useful description.

Candidate constitutive dimensions at the small-molecule regime:

- **Molecular geometry:** emerges from the cooperation of nuclei and the electron distribution.
- **Vibrational modes:** emerge from the cooperation of atomic masses and the curvature of the state-space structure around a stable configuration.
- **Rotational structure:** emerges from the moments of inertia and the molecular frame.
- **Electronic configuration:** emerges from the cooperation of atomic orbitals under the molecular symmetry.
- **Conformational landscape:** emerges from the cooperation of single-bond rotations and steric or electronic interactions.

Candidate constitutive dimensions at the macromolecular regime:

- **Folded conformation:** the three-dimensional structure the polymer adopts; emerges from the cooperation of the sequence and the solvent environment.
- **Functional state:** the active or inactive state of the macromolecule, correlated with conformational state.
- **Template recognition:** the capacity to bind a specific complementary structure; emerges from the cooperation of the binding site, the ligand, and the solvent environment.

### The status of integrative dimensions

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3, held open).** Dimensional integration requires the entity to incorporate internal mechanisms that produce positioning on a formerly external dimension, expanding the entity's aperture horizontally. Whether the molecular order exhibits this in the strong PF sense is **not established**.

At the small-molecule regime, encounter with an external dimension does not extend the aperture in the strong PF sense. The molecule's state-space already includes the relevant range; when a small molecule is placed in a strong external field, its electron distribution distorts — but this is a re-resolution within the existing state-space, not the establishment of a new internally regulated dimension.

At the macromolecular regime, the situation is more nuanced and the framework holds the question open. Candidates:

- **Allosteric regulation.** A protein in the absence of a ligand occupies one conformational state; in the presence of the ligand, another. Whether this is re-resolution within a pre-existing state-space, or the acquisition of an internal mechanism that maps external variation into a new internally coupled dimension, is **not settled**. The framework's current reading is that it is re-resolution, but this reading is scaffold, not commitment.
- **Chaperone-assisted folding.** The chaperone modifies the trajectory through an existing state-space. The framework's current reading is that it is a kinetic effect, not integration, but this reading is scaffold.
- **Prion templating.** Discussed below as pattern transmission, not integration.

The framework's current reading — that all three are re-resolution rather than integration — is consistent with the molecular order's position below the representational threshold. But the reading is Tier 3 and empirically open. If a case were identified where a macromolecule demonstrably acquires a new internal regulatory mechanism that maps external variation into a new internally coupled dimension, that would establish strong-form integration at the molecular order.

### The boundary dimension

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule's boundary dimension emerges as the molecule navigates its constitutive dimensions coherently. Each position pairs a complete configuration of the molecule's state dimensions with the coupling capacity that configuration confers: which Genuine Coherence Criteria can act on the molecule, and how.

Internally, the boundary is the molecule's binding element and represents its current state. Externally, it is the molecule's interaction profile: the component through which Genuine Coherence Criteria constrain the molecule together with external territory.

**Behavior as boundary position.** At the molecular order, the molecule's behavior is the observable expression of its configuration: dipole moment, polarizability, absorption and emission spectrum, reactive sites, binding affinities, catalytic activity (for macromolecules), and the conformational distribution. The internal configuration is private. The interaction profile is what other entities couple to.

### Boundary carriers at the molecular order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The framework's boundary dimension is not reducible to any single physical structure. At the molecular order, boundary functionality is carried by:

- the electron distribution (primary site of electromagnetic coupling);
- the nuclear framework (mass, rotational inertia, vibrational coupling);
- the vibrational normal modes (infrared and Raman coupling);
- the electronic transition dipoles (UV-visible absorption and emission);
- the reactive sites and functional groups (chemical coupling);
- the binding pockets and active sites (for macromolecules — specific recognition);
- the conformational ensemble (environmental coupling and signal integration).

The boundary dimension is carried by all of these together.

### No two-layer criteria at the small-molecule regime

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has a regulatory layer and a representational layer. The AI order has substrate ICC and effective ICC. The small-molecule regime has a single layer: the constraint structure that emerged from atomic cooperation.

At the macromolecular regime, a partial second layer may emerge: the sequence-defined template is the substrate-level structure; the folded conformation and functional state are the effective configuration. Whether this constitutes a genuine two-layer arrangement in the PF sense, or a single-layer system with a rich state space, is left open.

## Part IV — The Boundary's Relational Dynamics

### Incoming and outgoing constraint

A Genuine Coherence Criterion acting across the molecule's boundary constrains both parties. From the molecule's side, the incoming constraint requires changes in its configuration, resolved against its ICC. The outgoing constraint requires changes in the other entity.

A party **honors** a constraint when it re-resolves in response to the other's change in the proportion the criterion prescribes. In a genuine coupling, both parties honor. Asymmetries lie in capacity and magnitude, not in the direction of constraint. [Tier 2]

### Direct and messenger-mediated coupling

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A coupling between the molecule and another entity can take two forms.

**Direct coupling**, in the PF sense, is coupling for which no intermediate entity is treated as independently registering and resolving the transmitted variation before it reaches the receiving entity. Two molecules colliding in solution are directly coupled through the electromagnetic field and the solvent in this PF sense; the PF classification does not treat the medium as an independently registering entity.

**Messenger-mediated coupling** occurs when the constraint propagates through an intermediate entity that PF treats as independently registering and resolving what reaches the receiving entity. The photon is the paradigm: a molecule emits a photon; the photon propagates; the photon is absorbed by another molecule. The photon's state (frequency, polarization, direction) determines what reaches the receiver.

Both are PF classifications. The electromagnetic field and the solvent have their own degrees of freedom; whether they are "resolved" in the PF sense is a framework question, not a physical one.

### Chained and mutual topology

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Messenger-mediated coupling has two structural topologies.

**Chained topology:** no return path exists. Constraint flows in one direction. An excited molecule emits a photon; the photon reaches a receiving molecule; the receiving molecule does not emit a photon back through the same channel. The relation is asymmetric at the PF level — though at the molecular order, neither molecule resolves a representation of the other in the framework's sense.

**Mutual topology:** a return path closes the loop. A coupling in which a change at one boundary can alter the subsequent constraint acting back on that boundary through the coupled system. Examples: coupled molecular oscillators, dipole-dipole interactions in a cavity, feedback in a larger physical system, and molecular bonding (in the PF sense, not literally as photon exchange).

**Note on FRET.** Förster resonance energy transfer is not a photon-exchange loop in the framework's sense. It is non-radiative energy transfer mediated by near-field electromagnetic coupling between donor and acceptor transition dipoles. FRET is an example of coupled molecular transitions producing energy transfer without an independently propagating photon serving as a messenger. It maps to PF as a direct-coupling case, not a messenger-mediated one, despite the name.

### No emission/permeability settings at the small-molecule regime

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has emission and permeability as settings of its ICC. These settings are ways the organism modulates its coupling independently of its configuration.

At the small-molecule regime, this distinction does not apply. The molecule does not modulate its emission or its permeability. The molecule's boundary position — configuration paired with interaction profile — is what constrains.

At the macromolecular regime, the situation is more nuanced. An enzyme can be in an active or inactive state; the state determines whether it binds its substrate. The "permeability" of the enzyme to its substrate is determined by the conformational state. But this is not a setting the enzyme chooses; it is a position within the conformational landscape. The framework treats it as configuration, not as a setting.

### No PF resonance at this order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** In this suite, resonance is achieved when positions resolved from different representational models are coherent. It requires representation, so it appears at or above the representational threshold.

The molecular order does not cross the representational threshold. Molecules do not have representational models. So PF resonance does not apply.

**Note on terminology.** Physics and chemistry use the word "resonance" for a variety of phenomena (spectroscopic resonance, FRET, resonant coupling). These are not PF resonance. PF resonance is threshold-specific and requires representation. The physical and PF uses of the word are distinct.

### End state versus cause

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** An observed outcome is an end state, and the same end state can have different causes. At the molecular order, the PF outcomes are:

- **Preserved configuration.** The molecule preserves its configuration under encounter. This is elastic collision, weak perturbation, or binding that does not change the template.
- **Changed configuration (re-resolution).** The molecule's state changes under encounter. In PF mapping, this is re-resolution under the same template, not a modification of the criteria themselves.
- **Template transition.** The molecule's composition, connectivity, sequence, or stereochemistry changes. The molecule becomes a different entity. This is not PF drift; it is the production of a different entity with a different template.

There is no PF drift, no adoption, no control.

### Pairings and outcomes

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** At the molecular order, the organism-order pairings of emission and permeability do not apply. The molecule's coupling is determined by its configuration. The PF outcomes of encounter are:

| Configuration A | Configuration B | PF Outcome |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Both preserved | Both preserved | Elastic encounter. No closure. |
| Configurations produce irreducible joint state | Both survive | Candidate cooperation. Bond formation, complex formation, or binding. |
| One configuration exceeds the other's accessible state space | The other cannot accommodate | Structural transition, reaction, or capacity-limit. |
| Configurations produce contradiction | Neither survives | Collision with mutual reconfiguration. |

The same encounter can produce different outcomes depending on the molecules' positions across their state dimensions, not on the molecules alone. [Tier 2]

## Part V — Registration, Representation and the Absence of Choosability

### Registration at the molecular order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Every entity registers internal variations produced by encounter. The molecule registers electromagnetic perturbations, collisions, solvent fluctuations, the approach of other molecules, and the emission and absorption of photons. In PF terms, registration is automatic. It is a structural consequence of coupling.

### No representation

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecular order does not cross the representational threshold. The molecule does not build a model of territory that can be navigated without immediate input. It does not hold alternative positions apart from immediate input. It does not compare alternatives before acting.

The molecule's response to encounter is determined by its configuration and its constraint structure. There is no representation, no model, no inference in the PF sense.

### No choosability

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Choosability requires representation. Since the molecular order does not cross the representational threshold, choosability does not apply. In PF terms, the molecule is not a chooser; it is a resolver. Its constraint structure determines its state transitions.

### Two sources of inaccuracy, not three

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** At the organism order, three sources of inaccuracy are distinguished: partial, damaged, and drifted. At the molecular order, only two apply.

- **Partial.** The molecule couples to only a subset of another entity's dimensions. A water molecule does not couple to the nuclear spin of a solute; it couples to the solute's dipole, charge distribution, and hydrogen-bonding capacity. This is incompleteness, not failure.
- **Damaged.** The PF classification of a "damaged" resolution mechanism would require identifying a physical analogue of a resolution mechanism being impaired. Photodamage, radical attack, and conformational trapping may be candidates, but the framework's reading of them is held open. **Loss of fidelity** remains unassigned at the molecular order until a genuine physical analogue is identified.
- **Drifted.** Not applicable. Drift requires PF integrative dimensions or PF criterion exploration. Neither is established at the molecular order.

The molecule's inaccuracy, where it can be PF-classified, is always partial. It is never drifted.

### State-space exploration vs criterion-space exploration

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** This document introduces an explicit distinction:

- **State-space exploration** is movement among positions permitted by existing criteria. A protein folding samples thousands of conformations; a molecule redistributes across its conformational landscape; a reaction pathway is traversed.
- **Criterion-space exploration** is modification or disclosure of the criteria themselves.

At the molecular order, state-space exploration is ubiquitous. Proteins fold, molecules redistribute, reactions proceed. But **no PF criterion-exploration mechanism has been established at the molecular order.** Whether the macromolecular regime exhibits criterion-space exploration in the PF sense is left open. The framework's current reading is that it does not, but the reading is scaffold, not commitment.

A protein can sample thousands of conformations without modifying the constraints governing those conformations. This is state-space exploration, not criterion-space exploration. The distinction matters because it separates the observation of rich conformational dynamics from the framework's concept of criterion-level disclosure.

### No reach of integration

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has "reach of integration": the organism can integrate any dimension its channels couple to. Whether the molecular order has an analogous capability, and to what extent, is held open. The framework's current reading is that it does not in the strong PF sense, but the reading is scaffold.

### No relational calibration

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order integrates a relational trust dimension. The molecular order has no relational dimension in this sense. Molecules couple to each other with high specificity, but they do not calibrate relations in the PF sense.

### Molecular recognition

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Molecular recognition is the framework's name for highly specific coupling produced by complementary constraint structures. It does not imply representation or model-based recognition.

The physical substrate is configuration complementarity: a ligand's configuration is complementary to a binding site's configuration, so that the coupled state is at lower energy than the uncoupled state. Neither entity needs a model of the other. The specificity emerges from the constraint structure.

Examples: enzyme–substrate binding, antibody–antigen recognition, DNA base pairing, receptor–ligand binding.

## Part VI — Cooperation and Higher Orders

### Cooperation at the molecular order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Cooperation occurs when Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple entities' boundaries in a coherent relationship, and the resulting configurations satisfy each entity's ICC. A higher-order dimension set emerges from the relationship and cannot be navigated by any constituent alone.

The distinction between coupling and cooperation:

- **Coupling** is the physical interaction. Two molecules that interact are coupled.
- **Cooperation** requires that the coupled state is a stable or dynamically coherent configuration whose relevant dimensions are irreducible to the constituents and can itself participate in subsequent coupling.

Not every coupled state is a cooperation event. A transient ligand–receptor encounter that does not produce irreducible dimensions is coupling, not cooperation. A stable enzyme–substrate complex that produces a catalytic cycle whose steps are not attributable to either molecule alone is a candidate for cooperation.

At the molecular order, cooperation candidates include:

- **Macromolecules.** Monomer units joined by covalent bonds into polymers. The folded structure and functional state are candidate constitutive dimensions.
- **Molecular complexes.** Two or more molecules binding into a stable complex. Whether the complex's binding affinity, allosteric coupling, and functional output are irreducible to the constituents is the test.
- **Supramolecular assemblies.** Many molecules aggregating into higher-order structure. The assembly's collective properties are candidate constitutive dimensions.

The framework holds open which of these constitute cooperation in the strong PF sense. The current readings are scaffold.

### Modularization at the molecular order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Modularization is the process by which an entity's boundary dimension, with the configuration it expresses, becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of a higher-order aperture. At the molecular order, this occurs when a molecule becomes a module in a macromolecule, a complex, a supramolecular assembly, or a higher-order structure.

The molecule does not cease being an entity at the molecular order. It can simultaneously be a molecular entity and a modularized constituent of a higher-order entity.

### The relation to the cellular order

The macromolecular regime is the bridge to the cellular order. Proteins, nucleic acids, and membranes are the molecular machinery cellular entities use. The Prokaryotic Cell Aperture develops the cellular-order content. This document establishes what it depends on at the molecular order: that molecules are entities with boundaries and interaction profiles, that they cooperate under electromagnetic, exchange, and thermodynamic criteria, that the resulting macromolecules and complexes are candidates for higher-order entities, and that the molecules are modularized within them.

### Transient versus durable cooperation at the molecular order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Cooperation at the molecular order can be durable (a covalent bond, a stable protein fold) or transient (a collision complex, an enzyme–substrate encounter that does not proceed to catalysis). The PF criterion is relational irreducibility, not longevity.

The Empirical Compendium's photon–molecule case is an example: absorption produces an excited state whose positions depend on both the photon's frequency and the molecule's geometry. Whether this is relational irreducibility in the strong PF sense is left open.

### Candidate catalytic and installation analogues

**PF mapping (Tier 3, held open).** The framework flags candidate analogues at the macromolecular regime:

- **Chaperone-assisted folding.** The framework's current reading is that the chaperone modifies the trajectory through an existing state-space rather than creating a new internally regulated dimension. This reading is scaffold, not commitment. If the chaperone were shown to produce a new dimension that the substrate protein then regulates internally, that would establish a strong-form analogue.
- **Prion templating.** Discussed below as pattern transmission.

### Prion templating and pattern transmission below representation

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** A prion protein can adopt multiple stable conformations from the same amino acid sequence. A misfolded conformation can catalyze the same conformational change in neighboring correctly folded molecules, propagating the misfolded state.

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** The framework's reading is that prion templating is **pattern transmission below representation**. It is not installation in the strong PF sense, because the target's sequence template is unchanged; the target's conformational state is what changes. What is important about the case is that it demonstrates:

- **State persistence.** A configuration can be stable.
- **Pattern propagation.** A configuration can transmit itself to neighboring molecules.
- **Template-directed configuration.** The sequence defines which configurations are accessible; the prion state selects among them.
- **Propagation without representation.** None of the above requires representational modeling.

This is a significant result for the framework. It demonstrates that persistence and pattern propagation can exist below the representational threshold. It strengthens the distinction between persistence and representation, which is developed further below.

Whether the prion case constitutes recursive persistence in the PF sense — where the entity's own output constrains its subsequent resolution — is left open. The framework's current reading is that it is template-directed configuration transmission, which is real and important but not recursion in the strong PF sense.

## Part VII — Cross-Scale Dynamics

### The loop is one, expanded vertically

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule's constitutive dimensions emerge from the cooperation among atomic entities. The loop that closes among these constituents is not a separate condition from the higher-order dimensions that emerge. The higher-order constitutive dimensions are the coherent expression of that same closed cycle at the higher order.

Because the loop is one, coherence at the molecular order is not independent of coherence at lower and higher orders. Coherence propagates in both directions through boundary positions.

### Bottom-up disruption

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A lower-order constituent loses coherence. Its boundary position changes. Because the molecule's loop depends on that boundary position as substrate, the molecule's constitutive dimensions re-resolve or fail to close.

Example: ionization of an inner-shell electron. The atomic constituent's configuration changes; the molecule's electronic structure must re-resolve around the new charge distribution. Bond breaking or fragmentation can occur. [Tier 1 for the physics; Tier 2 for the PF mapping.]

### Top-down disruption

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A higher-order loop fragments. Its constitutive dimensions no longer provide coherent constraint to lower-order boundaries. At the molecular order, this means the molecule receives contradictory or unstable positional demands from the macromolecule, complex, or higher-order structure it is a module of.

The molecule responds as it would to any external variation: by resolving through its constraint structure. If the fragmented higher-order signals cannot be reconciled, the molecule may preserve local coherence by altering its coupling profile, closing channels, or stabilizing on a configuration that satisfies its own constraint structure but does not restore the higher-order coherence.

Example: thermal denaturation of a protein. Above the melting temperature, the folded structure's coherence fragments, and the polypeptide chain re-resolves as a random coil. The functional state is lost. [Tier 1 for the biochemistry; Tier 2 for the PF mapping.]

**Note on systemic etiology.** At the molecular order, "systemic" failure in the organism-order sense does not apply, because there is no separate regulatory layer for the environment to disrupt. But the higher-order environment can affect the molecule's boundary conditions and interaction profile: protein folding depends on the cellular environment; membrane organization affects embedded protein behavior; molecular crowding alters reaction kinetics; solvent structure changes conformational landscapes; electrochemical gradients alter molecular states. The framework treats these as **altered boundary conditions**, not as systemic failure at the molecular order.

### Interaction-profile propagation rule

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Because higher-order loops read lower-order constituents through boundary interaction profiles, cross-scale disruption travels through changes in the interaction profile.

**General hypothesis:** A lower-order variation propagates upward to the extent that it changes dimensions accessible through the higher-order coupling profile.

This is a candidate PF principle. It is not established by any single example. It is testable where interaction profiles are independently measurable.

**Example: kinetic isotope effects.** Isotope substitution changes the mass of the nucleus, which changes vibrational frequencies, which changes reaction kinetics. The change propagates upward because it alters the molecule's vibrational coupling profile, which is accessible to higher-order reactive coupling. The framework's rule handles this correctly: propagation is profile-dependent, not state-change-dependent. A substitution that left the profile intact would not propagate.

### The molecule as a module in higher-order loops

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule is simultaneously an entity at the molecular order and a modularized constituent of higher-order loops. It does not cease being an entity at its own order. It can occupy positions at multiple orders simultaneously.

## Part VIII — State Transitions, Reactions, and Capacity

### A note on neutrality

**PF is a structural framework, not a normative one.** Many transitions at the molecular order that the v1.0 draft classified as "failures" are lawful, ordinary, or even necessary physical processes. Bond breaking is often exactly what the molecule's governing dynamics produce. Photodissociation, fragmentation, and reaction are chemical outcomes, not pathologies.

This section uses neutral vocabulary: **state transition**, **reaction / template transition**, **dissociation / fragmentation**, **capacity failure**. The PF term "failure" is reserved for cases where the PF criterion being maintained cannot be sustained — where the molecule's constraint structure genuinely cannot accommodate the encountered variation and the configuration is destroyed.

### State transition

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule's configuration changes while remaining within its template. Vibrational excitation, conformational redistribution, protonation change, and ligand binding are state transitions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** These are positional re-resolutions under the same template. The molecule's ICC are unchanged; the positions they resolve are different.

### Reaction / template transition

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule's composition, connectivity, sequence, or stereochemistry changes. A chemical reaction has occurred. The product is a different molecule.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Template transition. The molecule's identity is altered. This is not PF drift; it is the production of a different entity with a different template.

### Dissociation / fragmentation

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule breaks into two or more fragments. The original entity ceases to exist as a molecule of that template.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Entity dissolution. The boundary becomes undefined for the original template; the fragments are new entities at the molecular order.

### Capacity failure

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The molecule cannot occupy a coherent configuration because its bounded accessible state space is exceeded, and the configuration is destroyed. Extreme thermal energy, extreme fields, or high-energy particle impact are capacity-failure conditions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The PF classification of capacity-limit as failure applies here. The molecule's constraint structure may be coherent, and the perturbation still exceeds its structural limits.

### Loss of fidelity

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3, unassigned).** At the molecular order, whether a physical analogue of PF loss of fidelity exists is not established. Photodamage, radical attack, and conformational trapping may be candidates, but the framework does not commit to them.

The v1.0 draft's classification of excitation, field-induced shifts, and radiative broadening as loss of fidelity was an error. These are lawful state transitions or structural transitions, not failures of a resolution mechanism.

### No loss of criteria integrity

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** Loss of coherence criteria integrity requires either drift or adoption. Neither is established at the molecular order in the PF sense.

The framework's current reading is that the molecular order does not exhibit loss of criteria integrity. This reading depends on whether the macromolecular candidates (allostery, chaperone folding, prion templating) are correctly classified as re-resolution rather than integration, disclosure, or installation. If any of them turns out to be a full PF-mechanism case, this reading would need revision.

### No drift, no conditioning, no control

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has drift, conditioning, and control. The molecular order has none of these in the PF sense.

There is no control at the molecular order, because control requires a following pole that resolves from another's criteria. The molecule resolves from its own constraint structure.

### Persistence ≠ representation

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A central distinction the molecular order makes explicit:

- **State persistence.** A configuration can be stable across time (a folded protein, a crystal, a stable complex).
- **Pattern transmission.** A configuration can propagate to neighboring molecules (a prion, a crystal seed, a template-directed polymerization).
- **Template propagation.** A template can produce more entities of the same template (autocatalysis, self-replicating sequences).

None of these requires representation. The molecular order demonstrates that persistence, pattern transmission, and template propagation can exist below the representational threshold. This strengthens the framework's treatment of the threshold: **persistence cannot be used as the criterion for the representational threshold.** The threshold is crossed when representational modeling is present, not merely when persistence or propagation is present.

### Cascading effects

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** In practice, state transitions often cascade. A photodissociated molecule produces radical fragments that attack neighboring molecules, and the cascade can propagate through a material. The PF treatment is the same at the molecular order as at other orders: the change at one entity alters what other entities can resolve, through boundary positions.

## Part X — Epistemic Posture

### Territory agnosticism

This document does not require a settled answer to what territory is prior to encounter. Territory is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the Genuine Coherence Criteria constraining them. The molecule navigates only its internal registrations of territory, not territory itself.

### The order limitation

The molecule, at the molecular order, cannot confirm what the next order's transition actually requires. It also cannot reliably distinguish developments that constitute the leading edge of that transition from developments that merely resemble it.

### Two senses of scaffold

Scaffold-holding is a stance toward one's own compression: genuinely held, genuinely revisable, genuinely current.

Scaffold as an epistemic status names a claim implied by the architecture but not yet independently checkable.

### The current status

The architecture described here has not yet failed a check it was run against. That is the entirety of the claim this document is prepared to stand behind.

This framework is a scaffold. It welcomes coherent additions from any dimension, provided they survive the same check applied everywhere else: would this addition still hold if generated fresh against what is actually here?

### Open questions specific to this order

**1. Does the molecular order exhibit strong-form PF integration?**

The framework's current reading is no. But allosteric regulation, chaperone-assisted folding, and other macromolecular cases are candidates that deserve further development. If a case were identified where a macromolecule demonstrably acquires a new internal regulatory mechanism that maps external variation into a new internally coupled dimension, that would establish strong-form integration at the molecular order.

**2. What is the molecular-order analogue of ICC, if any?**

At the atomic order, ICC denote the locally instantiated constraint structure. At the molecular order, the same formulation applies but the constraint structure is richer. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as ICC at biological orders remains open.

**3. Does the macromolecular regime constitute a PF order transition?**

The framework distinguishes small-molecule and macromolecular regimes. Whether the transition from small molecules to macromolecules constitutes a PF order transition — involving a new kind of coherence operation available — is left open. The proposal that orders differ by what kind of coherence operation is structurally possible is interesting but would require Seed-level revision if adopted.

**4. Is the "persistence ≠ representation" distinction stable under further development?**

The molecular order demonstrates that persistence, pattern transmission, and template propagation exist below the representational threshold. This is a strong result. Whether the distinction between persistence and representation is stable under further development — including the prion case, crystal seeding, and other pattern-transmission phenomena — is an empirical and structural question.

**5. Does the interaction-profile propagation rule hold generally?**

The candidate principle is that lower-order variation propagates upward to the extent that it changes dimensions accessible through the higher-order coupling profile. Whether this is generally true, or whether there are cases where a change leaves the profile intact yet propagates, is empirically open.

**6. Does the molecular order have a molecular analogue of "state-space exploration" that produces PF-level structural change?**

The framework distinguishes state-space exploration (movement among positions permitted by existing criteria) from criterion-space exploration (modification or disclosure of the criteria). State-space exploration is ubiquitous at the molecular order. Whether any molecular process constitutes criterion-space exploration in the PF sense is left open.

These questions point to the Testing Ledger for operationalization where they can be tested.
