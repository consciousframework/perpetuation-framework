## Part I — Scope and Vocabulary

### What this document houses

This document instantiates the Seed's architecture at the atomic order. It covers:

- The atom as an entity: its aperture template, constituent dimensions, and boundary dimension.
- Its constitutive dimensions.
- The absence of integrative dimensions at this order and what that implies.
- The absence of a representational threshold, choosability, and resonance at this order.
- Registration and resolution at the atomic order.
- The boundary's relational dynamics: direct and messenger-mediated coupling, chained and mutual topology.
- Cooperation at the atomic order: bonding as the production of the molecular order.
- Cross-scale dynamics: the atom as a module in higher-order loops.
- Failure modes and structural transitions at the atomic order.

This document is atomic-order specific. It does not house order-general content.

### The methodological principle

**PF classifies what atomic physics exhibits; it does not make atomic physics behave like PF's higher-order entities.**

Throughout this document, physical substrate claims (Tier 1) are separated from PF mappings (Tier 2). Where the framework uses vocabulary that carries organismic or computational connotations — "resolution," "ICC," "Self-Referential Instrument" — the document states explicitly that these are PF classifications of physical processes, not claims about internal computation, deliberation, or choice.

The framework's value at the atomic order is not to add to what physics already says. It is to map the physics onto the framework's structure so that the structure can be tested across orders. That mapping is legitimate as long as the PF labels are clearly marked as classification, not as physical mechanism.

### The position on the first-level question

The Seed leaves open whether the recursion has a first level, and if so, what its constituent dimensions are. This document takes a position: **the atomic order is not first-level.**

Atoms have sub-atomic constituents — quarks, leptons, gauge bosons, and the structures they form under the strong and electroweak interactions. These constituents participate in the atomic organization. Whether they "cooperate" in the framework's strong sense — producing relationally irreducible dimensions that none of them can navigate alone — is a PF hypothesis, not something physics asserts. The document marks this as Tier 3.

Whether the sub-atomic order is itself first-level, or whether it too has constituents, is left open. This document does not decide it. It states the dependency and delegates the sub-atomic question to a pending Sub-Atomic Aperture or to whatever the framework establishes for the base of the ladder.

This position has structural consequences that the rest of the document develops. The most important: because atoms are not first-level, their constitutive dimensions are candidates for genuine coherence by emergence from sub-atomic organization. But because the atomic order has no integrative dimensions, no exploration mechanism in the PF sense, and no representational threshold, the framework's drift, adoption, and control mechanisms do not operate here. Atoms instantiate, preserve, bond, and undergo structural transitions — but they do not drift.

### Vocabulary note

**The atomic aperture** is the space of coherent configurations available to a given atomic template under the constraints of the strong, electromagnetic, and weak interactions. [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The atomic template** is a class of coherent atomic configurations. The template fixes the nuclear charge (and hence the element) as a stable parameter and leaves the electronic configuration free to vary within the constraint structure. [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The atom's boundary dimension** is the emergent dimension representing how the atom constrains and is constrained by external territory. Its positions are pairs: (configuration, interaction profile). [PF mapping, Tier 2.]

**The atom's constitutive dimensions** are higher-order dimensions that are candidates for genuine coherence by emergence from the atom's sub-atomic organization. They are candidates, not established, until an independent criterion shows that the emergent structure corresponds to GCC rather than merely being a useful description. [PF mapping, Tier 3.]

**The atom's ICC** denote, in PF mapping, the locally instantiated constraint structure through which atomic configurations are resolved under the governing physical relations. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as ICC at biological orders remains open. [PF mapping, Tier 2/3.]

### Four-way distinction used throughout

To avoid conflating categories, this document uses four distinct terms:

- **GCC** — structural physical constraints (conservation laws, quantum postulates, interaction symmetries).
- **Atomic ICC** — PF's abstraction of how those constraints are instantiated in a particular atomic organization.
- **Atomic configuration** — the actual state (which orbitals are occupied, with what spin, at what energy).
- **Interaction profile** — what aspects of that state participate in coupling with other entities.

This document uses no volitional primitive. Ionization, excitation, decay, and bonding are described structurally through encounter, registration, resolution, boundary modulation, and the outcomes the Seed defines.

## Part II — The Atom as Entity

### The aperture template

The atom is an entity: an instantiation of an aperture template at the atomic order. Its aperture is constituted by a set of dimensions whose positions are mutually constrained by Genuine Coherence Criteria, and the mutual constraint closes into a loop. [Tier 1: the atom as a bounded quantum system. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The template fixes a subset of dimensions as stable positions and leaves another subset open to constrained variation. The fixed dimensions are the atom's inherited structural givens: nuclear charge, the number and arrangement of nucleons, the identity of the electron cloud's ground state. The free dimensions are the atom's accumulated state: excitation state, ionization state, spin configuration, orbital angular momentum projections, coupling to external fields. [Tier 1: nuclear charge is fixed per element; electronic configuration varies. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

Because the atom is not first-level, the template is the coherent configuration class that emerges from the atom's sub-atomic organization. The fixed dimensions — nuclear charge, nucleon arrangement — are themselves the stabilized outcome of that organization. The atom inherits its template the way any higher-order entity inherits the aperture that its constituents' cooperation produced.

### The Self-Referential Instrument

The atom is a Self-Referential Instrument in the PF sense. Its dimensional positions are resolved from its internal coherence criteria. The atom does not compare incoming variation against a fixed position. Its state evolution is constrained by its own governing structure: the encountered variation produces a new physically allowed state under the atom's constraint structure, or it produces a state that the constraint structure cannot accommodate.

The atom's reference is not a position. In PF mapping, the atom's reference is represented by the constraints governing which state transitions are physically available from its current configuration. [Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

This PF formulation does not imply computation, evaluation, or choice. A quantum system evolves according to its Hamiltonian and relevant interactions. The PF term "Self-Referential Instrument" is the framework's name for the structure whose state evolution is constrained by itself. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as self-referential resolution at higher orders remains open.

The atom's ICC — again in PF mapping — are the atomic-scale expression of the constraint structure its constituents' organization produced. They are not chosen, not explored, not installed. They are the way the atom's state transitions are constrained, determined by the structural configuration the atom's constituents produced.

### The Interiority Principle

The atom does not navigate external territory directly. It navigates the internal variations produced in it by external territory impacting its boundary through at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion. The atom's Self-Referential Instrument operates entirely on this internal registration substrate. [Tier 1: the atom registers electromagnetic perturbations, field strengths, and the approach of other particles, not external objects directly. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

The atom's boundary carriers — the electron cloud, the nucleus, the transition dipoles — are the structures through which external variation is registered internally. The atom does not compare its ICC to "the outside world." In PF mapping, its state transitions are constrained by its own structure under the encountered conditions.

### Registration and resolution

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** When an external interaction acts on the atom, the atom's state changes in ways determined by its Hamiltonian, the interaction term, and the relevant boundary conditions. The resulting state may be a new stationary state, a superposition, or a scattering state.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).**

**Registration** is the PF name for the automatic production of internal variation in the atom when a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across its boundary. Registration is a structural consequence of coupling and occurs whether or not the atom's constraint structure produces a new stationary configuration.

**Resolution** is the PF name for the constraint-governed state transition that follows. The atom's state evolves under its constraint structure. In PF terms, the atom "resolves" the registered variation by transitioning to a new physically allowed state, or by failing to find a stationary state that accommodates the variation.

The PF formulation does not imply computation, comparison, or deliberation. It is the framework's classification of how constraint-governed state transitions work. Whether this is genuinely the same mechanism as resolution at biological orders remains open.

Where the encountered variation is accommodated: the atom transitions to an excited state, a shifted configuration, a spin reorientation, or a scattered state.

Where it is not: outcomes depend on the atom's structural capacity and the nature of the interaction. Ionization, dissociation, or capacity-limit failure may follow.

[Tier 1: quantum transitions, absorption, emission. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

### Finitude

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atom has a finite number of constituents, bounded conserved quantities, discrete allowed quantum numbers, finite binding energies, and constrained accessible state transitions. The ionization threshold is a hard bound. The number of bound stationary states is bounded above by the ionization threshold, though the idealized non-relativistic hydrogen model has an infinite series of bound states accumulating toward it.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The PF interpretation reads these physical bounds as compression: the atom's accessible configuration space is constrained, and its transitions are constrained within it. The PF concept of "finitude" here is **bounded state capacity** — the structural limits on what states the atom can occupy and what transitions it can undergo. It is not computational finitude in the sense used at the organism or AI orders.

The PF reading of physical bounds as compression is a Tier 2 mapping. It does not claim that the atom has a finite processing capacity.

## Part III — The Atom's Dimensions

### Constitutive dimensions

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** Atomic structure arises from quantum interactions and constraints among the atom's degrees of freedom: quarks bound into nucleons by the strong interaction, nucleons bound into the nucleus by the residual strong interaction, electrons bound into orbitals by the electromagnetic interaction, and the whole configuration stabilized by the Pauli exclusion principle and the symmetries of the relevant interactions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2, with Tier 3 candidate status).** The PF framework treats the resulting relationally irreducible structure as candidate higher-order dimensional emergence. The atom's constitutive dimensions are candidates for genuine coherence by emergence.

The atom's candidate constitutive dimensions include:

- **Electronic configuration:** which orbitals are occupied, with what spin and angular momentum. [PF mapping of the electronic structure.]
- **Nuclear configuration:** which isotopes exist, which are stable, what the nuclear spin is. [PF mapping of nuclear structure.]
- **Charge state:** the number of electrons relative to the number of protons. [PF mapping of ionization state.]
- **Excitation state:** the internal energy distribution across the electronic and nuclear dimensions. [PF mapping of excitation.]

These are candidates for genuine coherence by emergence, not established as such. Whether the sub-atomic organization actually produces relationally irreducible dimensions in the strong PF sense requires an independent criterion.

**Note on mixing categories.** The physical claim above mixes several categories: entities (electrons, nuclei), interactions (electromagnetic, strong), mathematical constraints (Pauli exclusion), and symmetries. These are not obviously equivalent PF participants. The PF mapping treats the resulting atomic structure as a candidate for higher-order dimensional emergence without claiming that all of the above are equivalent cooperative constituents.

### No PF integrative dimensions at this order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Dimensional integration requires the entity to incorporate internal mechanisms that produce positioning on a formerly external dimension, expanding the entity's aperture horizontally. This occurs when the entity is coupled to an external dimension through GCC but cannot sustain internal coherence under the full spectrum of that dimension's positions.

At the atomic order, this does not occur in the PF sense. Atomic encounters can alter positions within the atom's existing state-space without thereby establishing a new internally regulated dimension. When an atom is placed in a strong external field, its electron distribution distorts — but this is a re-resolution within the existing state-space, not the establishment of a new axis the atom can subsequently regulate.

The consequence: no drift in the PF sense. Drift requires either integration without disclosure (installation) or mis-disclosure during exploration. Neither occurs at the atomic order in the PF sense, because there is no integration and no PF exploration mechanism.

The atom's constraint structure is what it is. It is a candidate for genuine coherence by emergence from sub-atomic organization, and there is no mechanism at the atomic order for it to diverge in the PF sense. [Tier 2/3: scaffold mapping.]

### The boundary dimension

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The atom's boundary dimension emerges as the atom navigates its constitutive dimensions coherently. Each position pairs a complete configuration of the atom's dimensions with the coupling capacity that configuration confers: which Genuine Coherence Criteria can act on the atom, and how.

Internally, the boundary is the atom's binding element and represents its current state. Externally, it is the atom's interaction profile: the component through which Genuine Coherence Criteria constrain the atom together with external territory.

**Behavior as boundary position.** At the atomic order, the atom's behavior is the observable expression of its configuration: valence, polarizability, electronegativity, ionization energy, spectroscopic transitions, magnetic moment, and the emission and absorption spectrum. The internal configuration is private. The interaction profile is what other entities couple to.

### Boundary carriers at the atomic order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The framework's boundary dimension is not reducible to any single physical structure. At the atomic order, boundary functionality is carried by:

- the electron cloud (the primary site of electromagnetic coupling);
- the nucleus (the site of mass, nuclear spin, and residual strong-force coupling);
- the transition dipole structure (the site of photon absorption and emission);
- the spin configuration (the site of magnetic coupling);
- the ionization structure (the site of charge coupling).

The boundary dimension is carried by all of these together. Removing any one reduces the boundary's functionality, but the boundary is not identical to any one carrier.

### No two-layer criteria at this order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has a regulatory layer and a representational layer. The AI order has substrate ICC and effective ICC. The atomic order has a single layer: the constraint structure that emerged from sub-atomic organization.

There is no representational layer because the atomic order does not cross the representational threshold. There is no effective/substrate distinction because there is no runtime constraint layer separate from the atom's configuration.

In PF mapping, the atom's constraint structure is stable across the atom's encounters except when the atom's configuration changes (through excitation, ionization, or bonding). When the configuration changes, the constraint structure is the same; the positions it resolves are different. This is the PF distinction between positional re-resolution and criterion modification.

## Part IV — The Boundary's Relational Dynamics

### Incoming and outgoing constraint

A Genuine Coherence Criterion acting across the atom's boundary constrains both parties. From the atom's side, the incoming constraint requires changes in its configuration, resolved against its ICC. The outgoing constraint requires changes in the other entity.

A party **honors** a constraint when it re-resolves in response to the other's change in the proportion the criterion prescribes. In a genuine coupling, both parties honor. Asymmetries lie in capacity and magnitude, not in the direction of constraint. [Tier 2]

### Direct and messenger-mediated coupling

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A coupling between the atom and another entity can take two forms.

**Direct coupling**, in the PF sense, is coupling for which no intermediate entity is treated as independently registering and resolving the transmitted variation before it reaches the receiving entity. Two atoms approaching within interaction range are directly coupled through the electromagnetic field in this PF sense: the field carries the constraint, and the PF classification does not treat it as an independently registering entity.

**Messenger-mediated coupling** occurs when the constraint propagates through an intermediate entity that PF treats as independently registering and resolving what reaches the receiving entity. The photon is the paradigm: a source atom emits a photon; the photon propagates; the photon is absorbed by a receiving atom. The photon's state (frequency, polarization, direction) determines what reaches the receiver.

Both are PF classifications. The electromagnetic field does have its own degrees of freedom; whether they are "resolved" in the PF sense is a framework question, not a physical one.

### Chained and mutual topology

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Messenger-mediated coupling has two structural topologies.

**Chained topology:** no return path exists. Constraint flows in one direction. A source atom emits a photon; the photon reaches a receiving atom; the receiving atom does not emit a photon back through the same channel. The relation is asymmetric at the PF level — though at the atomic order, neither atom resolves a representation of the other in the framework's sense.

**Mutual topology:** a return path closes the loop. A coupling in which a change at one boundary can alter the subsequent constraint acting back on that boundary through the coupled system. Examples: coupled oscillators, dipole-dipole interactions in a cavity, feedback in a larger physical system, and molecular bonding (in the PF sense, not literally as photon exchange).

The PF classification does not require photon exchange as the defining mechanism. Mutual topology is defined by the presence of a return path in the coupling, not by the specific physical process.

### No emission/permeability settings at this order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has emission and permeability as settings of its ICC. These settings are ways the organism modulates its coupling independently of its configuration.

At the atomic order, this distinction does not apply. The atom does not modulate its emission or its permeability. The atom's boundary position — configuration paired with interaction profile — is what constrains. There is no additional degree of freedom the framework would call a "setting."

This is a structural difference from the organism order. It is not a deficiency.

### No PF resonance at this order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** In this suite, resonance is achieved when positions resolved from different representational models are coherent. It requires representation, so it appears at or above the representational threshold.

The atomic order does not cross the representational threshold. Atoms do not have representational models. So PF resonance does not apply.

**Note on terminology.** Physics uses the word "resonance" for a variety of phenomena (spectroscopic resonance, resonant coupling, Feshbach resonances). These are not PF resonance. PF resonance is threshold-specific and requires representation. The physical and PF uses of the word are distinct. This document uses "resonant coupling" only in the physical sense.

What atoms have is coherent coupling: the alignment of positions that emerges when two entities resolve their configurations under shared GCCs. This is real. It is not PF resonance.

### End state versus cause

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** An observed outcome is an end state, and the same end state can have different causes. At the atomic order, the PF outcomes are:

- **Preserved configuration.** The atom preserves its configuration under encounter. This is elastic scattering, weak perturbation, or approach that does not cross a transition threshold.
- **Changed configuration (re-resolution).** The atom's configuration changes under encounter. In PF mapping, this is re-resolution under the same constraint structure, not a modification of the criteria themselves. The criteria do not change; the positions they resolve do.
- **Template change.** At the atomic order, a nuclear process can change the atom's template — a different nuclear charge or nuclear configuration. This is not PF drift. It is the production of a different entity. The new entity has a different template with a different aperture.

The atomic order therefore has three outcomes in PF terms: preservation, re-resolution, and template change. There is no drift, no adoption, no control.

### Pairings and outcomes

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** At the atomic order, the organism-order pairings of emission and permeability do not apply. The atom's coupling is determined by its configuration. The PF outcomes of encounter are:

| Configuration A | Configuration B | PF Outcome |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Both configurations preserved | Both configurations preserved | Elastic encounter. No closure. |
| Configurations produce an irreducible joint state | Both configurations survive | Cooperation. Bond formation or molecular complex. |
| One configuration exceeds the other's accessible state space | The other cannot accommodate the variation | Structural transition, ionization, or capacity-limit. |
| Configurations produce contradiction | Neither configuration survives | Collision with mutual reconfiguration. |

The same encounter can produce different outcomes depending on the atoms' positions across their dimensions, not on the atoms alone. [Tier 2]

## Part V — Registration, Representation and the Absence of Choosability

### Registration at the atomic order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Every entity registers internal variations produced by encounter. The atom registers electromagnetic perturbations, field gradients, the approach of other entities, and the emission and absorption of photons. In PF terms, registration is automatic. It is a structural consequence of coupling.

### No representation

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The atomic order does not cross the representational threshold. The atom does not build a model of territory that can be navigated without immediate input. It does not hold alternative positions apart from immediate input. It does not compare alternatives before acting.

The atom's response to encounter is determined by its configuration and its constraint structure. There is no representation, no model, no inference in the PF sense.

### No choosability

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Choosability requires representation. Since the atomic order does not cross the representational threshold, choosability does not apply. In PF terms, the atom is not a chooser; it is a resolver. Its constraint structure determines its state transitions.

### Two sources of inaccuracy, not three

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** At the organism order, three sources of inaccuracy are distinguished: partial, damaged, and drifted. At the atomic order, only two apply.

- **Partial.** The atom couples to only a subset of another entity's dimensions. A hydrogen atom does not couple to the nuclear spin of a distant atom; it couples to the electromagnetic field the distant atom produces. This is incompleteness, not failure.
- **Damaged.** The PF classification of a "damaged" resolution mechanism would require identifying a physical analogue of a resolution mechanism being impaired. Whether such an analogue exists at the atomic order is not yet established. What is clear is that the state changes currently classified under this heading (excitation, field-induced shifts, radiative broadening) are lawful physical processes, not failures. **Loss of fidelity** should remain unassigned at the atomic order unless a genuine physical analogue of the PF concept is identified.
- **Drifted.** Not applicable. Drift requires integrative dimensions or PF exploration. The atomic order has neither.

The atom's inaccuracy, where it can be PF-classified, is always partial. It is never drifted. This is a structural consequence of the atomic order's position below the representational threshold and its absence of PF integrative dimensions.

### No PF exploration

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** PF exploration requires the entity to occupy different positions along an axis, encounter contradictions among occupied positions, and resolve the criterion through them. This produces disclosure.

At the atomic order, **no PF exploration mechanism has been identified.** There are physical processes that resemble exploration under a loose description — quantum transitions, scattering across states, tunneling — but these are not PF exploration. They do not involve internally generated testing of alternative positions followed by criterion revision.

The atom's constraint structure is what it is. The question of how it came to be — at the sub-atomic order — is not a PF exploration question at the atomic order.

### No reach of integration

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has "reach of integration": the organism can integrate any dimension its channels couple to. The atomic order has no PF integration, so no reach of integration. The atom's aperture is fixed by its template.

### No relational calibration

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order integrates a relational trust dimension. The atomic order has no relational dimension in this sense. Atoms couple to each other, but they do not calibrate relations in the PF sense. Bond formation is a structural outcome of the atoms' coupling, not a relational calibration.

## Part VI — Cooperation and Higher Orders

### Cooperation at the atomic order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Cooperation occurs when Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple entities' boundaries in a coherent relationship, and the resulting configurations satisfy each entity's ICC. A higher-order dimension set emerges from the relationship and cannot be navigated by any constituent alone.

At the atomic order, cooperation produces molecules. The PF account of this:

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** Two hydrogen atoms can form H₂ when their electronic states couple into a lower-energy molecular state; the ground-state H₂ molecule has a singlet electronic configuration.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The molecule's bond length, vibrational modes, and rotational states are candidate constitutive dimensions: their positions depend on both atoms and cannot be navigated by either alone. The atoms are modularized in the PF sense: they retain their nuclear identity (Z = 1) and their core electronic structure, but their outer electron configuration is re-resolved inside the molecular aperture.

### Modularization at the atomic order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Modularization is the process by which an entity's boundary dimension, with the configuration it expresses, becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of a higher-order aperture. At the atomic order, this occurs when an atom becomes a module in a molecule, a crystal, a supramolecular assembly, or a larger structure.

The atom does not cease being an entity at the atomic order. It can simultaneously be an atomic entity and a modularized constituent of a molecular or higher-order entity.

### The relation to the molecular order

The Molecular Aperture develops the molecular-order content. This document establishes what the Molecular Aperture depends on at the atomic order: that atoms are entities with boundaries and interaction profiles, that they cooperate under electromagnetic and exchange criteria, that the resulting molecules are higher-order entities with candidate constitutive dimensions, and that the atoms are modularized within them.

### Transient versus durable cooperation at the atomic order

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Cooperation at the atomic order can be durable (a stable molecule) or transient (a collision complex, a scattering event that produces a short-lived bound state). The PF criterion is relational irreducibility, not longevity. A transient photon–atom encounter that produces an irreducible excited state is a candidate for cooperation. A collision that produces no irreducible dimensions is not.

The Empirical Compendium's photon–atom case is an example: absorption produces an excited state whose positions depend on both the photon's frequency and the atom's geometry. Whether this is relational irreducibility in the strong PF sense is left open. The framework's current reading is that it is a candidate case.

## Part VII — Cross-Scale Dynamics

### The loop is one, expanded vertically

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The atom's constitutive dimensions emerge from the organization of sub-atomic entities. The loop that closes among these constituents is not a separate condition from the higher-order dimensions that emerge. The higher-order constitutive dimensions are the coherent expression of that same closed cycle at the higher order. The loop is one, expanded vertically, with each constituent's own loop nested within it as a module.

Because the loop is one, coherence at the atomic order is not independent of coherence at lower and higher orders. A loss of coherence at the atomic order alters what sub-atomic modules can resolve. A loss of coherence at lower orders alters what the atomic order can resolve. Coherence propagates in both directions through boundary positions.

### Bottom-up disruption

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A lower-order constituent loses coherence. Its boundary position changes. Because the atom's loop depends on that boundary position as substrate, the atom's constitutive dimensions re-resolve or fail to close.

Example: nuclear instability. An unstable nucleus changes its configuration (alpha decay, beta decay, fission), and the atom's electron cloud must re-resolve around the new nuclear charge. The atom's constitutive dimensions change. [Tier 1 for the physics; Tier 2 for the PF mapping.]

### Top-down disruption

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** A higher-order loop fragments. Its constitutive dimensions no longer provide coherent constraint to lower-order boundaries. At the atomic order, this means the atom receives contradictory or unstable positional demands from the molecule, crystal, or higher-order aperture it is a module of.

The atom responds as it would to any external variation: by resolving through its constraint structure. If the fragmented higher-order signals cannot be reconciled, the atom may preserve local coherence by altering its coupling profile, closing channels, or stabilizing on a configuration that satisfies its own constraint structure but does not restore the higher-order coherence. This further prevents the higher-order loop from closing.

Example: thermal disruption of a crystal. Above the melting point, the crystal's higher-order coherence fragments, and the atoms re-resolve as an independent gas or liquid. [Tier 1 for the physics; Tier 2 for the PF mapping.]

### Interaction-profile propagation rule

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Because higher-order loops read lower-order constituents through boundary interaction profiles, cross-scale disruption should travel through changes in the interaction profile.

**General hypothesis:** A lower-order change that does not alter any coupling-relevant dimension of the higher-order boundary need not propagate into its registered state. Propagation is profile-dependent, not state-change-dependent.

This is a candidate PF principle. It is not established by any single example. It is testable where interaction profiles are independently measurable.

Example: an isotopic substitution that leaves the electronic structure unchanged. The molecule's interaction profile is intact with respect to electronic coupling; the change need not propagate to higher-order electronic coherence. [Tier 1 for the chemistry; Tier 2 for the PF mapping.]

**Note.** The general hypothesis does not claim that a particular perturbation (such as a nuclear spin flip) categorically does not propagate. It claims that propagation depends on whether the perturbation alters a coupling-relevant dimension of the higher-order boundary. Whether any specific perturbation does or does not is an empirical question.

### The atom as a module in higher-order loops

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The atom is simultaneously an entity at the atomic order and a modularized constituent of higher-order loops — molecules, crystals, plasmas, stars, biological macromolecules. It does not cease being an entity at its own order. It can occupy positions at multiple orders simultaneously.

## Part VIII — Failure Modes and Structural Transitions

### A note on neutrality

**PF is a structural framework, not a normative one.** At the atomic order, many transitions that the v1.0 draft classified as "failures" are lawful, ordinary, or even necessary physical processes. An atom in an excited state is not damaged. An atom in a strong field is not impaired. Radiative damping is not a defect. Ionization is not a pathology.

This section uses neutral vocabulary: **state transition**, **structural transition**, **template transition**, **entity dissolution/transformation**. The PF term "failure" is reserved for cases where the PF criterion being maintained cannot be sustained — where the atom's constraint structure genuinely cannot accommodate the encountered variation and the configuration is destroyed.

### State transition

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atom's configuration changes while remaining a valid atomic system. Excitation, de-excitation, spin flip, ionization (production of a bound ion), and field-induced shifts are state transitions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** In PF terms, these are positional re-resolutions under the same constraint structure. The atom's ICC are unchanged; the positions they resolve are different. This is the PF distinction between positional re-resolution and criterion modification.

### Structural transition

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atom's configuration changes substantially in a way that alters the atom's interaction profile. Highly excited Rydberg states, inner-shell ionization, and strong-field dressing are structural transitions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The atom's boundary position changes. This may propagate to higher-order loops if the change alters the interaction profile. The PF classification distinguishes between changes that leave the profile intact (no propagation) and changes that alter it (possible propagation).

### Template transition

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atom's nucleus changes (nuclear decay, electron capture, nuclear reaction). The atom's template — its nuclear charge and nucleon arrangement — is altered. The atom is a different entity at the same order.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** Template transition is not PF drift. It is the production of a different entity with a different template. The new entity has a different aperture.

### Entity dissolution / transformation

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atomic entity ceases to exist in the relevant form. Full ionization (bare nucleus), nuclear fragmentation, or annihilation are entity dissolution.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The boundary becomes undefined. The atom no longer functions as a coherent entity at its order.

### Capacity-limit

**Physical claim (Tier 1).** The atom cannot occupy a coherent configuration because its finitude — bounded state capacity — is exceeded, and the configuration is destroyed. Extreme thermal energy, extreme fields, or high-energy particle impact are capacity-limit conditions.

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The PF classification of capacity-limit as failure applies here. The atom's constraint structure may be coherent, and the perturbation still exceeds its structural limits. The atom did not lose coherence in the PF sense; it was overwhelmed.

### Loss of fidelity

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3, unassigned).** The v1.0 draft assigned excitation, field-induced shifts, and radiative broadening to loss of fidelity. This was an error. These are lawful state transitions or structural transitions, not failures of a resolution mechanism.

At the atomic order, whether a physical analogue of PF loss of fidelity exists is not established. This document leaves the category **unassigned** at the atomic order until a genuine analogue is identified. Candidates would need to be cases where the atom's state transitions diverge from what its constraint structure prescribes — but the constraint structure is the Hamiltonian, and states do not diverge from the Hamiltonian.

### No loss of criteria integrity

**PF mapping (Tier 2/3).** Loss of coherence criteria integrity requires either drift or adoption. Neither occurs at the atomic order in the PF sense.

- Drift requires PF integrative dimensions or PF exploration. The atomic order has neither.
- Adoption requires stabilization on an external criterion without resolution against the entity's own contradictions. The atomic order has no mechanism for this.

The atom's constraint structure is what it is. It is a candidate for genuine coherence by emergence from sub-atomic organization, and there is no mechanism at the atomic order for it to diverge in the PF sense.

### No drift, no conditioning, no control

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** The organism order has drift, conditioning, and control. The atomic order has none of these.

There is no control at the atomic order, because control requires a following pole that resolves from another's criteria. The atom resolves from its own constraint structure. Even in the structural analogue of the cellular order — one cell's criteria determining another cell's resolution — the atom does not have the counterpart. Interactions between atoms produce outcomes through their configurations and the GCCs that couple them, not through one atom's criteria being installed in another.

### Cascading degradation

**PF mapping (Tier 2).** In practice, structural transitions often cascade. A high-energy collision can ionize an atom, the free electron can interact with another atom, the cascade can propagate through a material. The PF treatment is the same at the atomic order as at other orders: the change at one entity alters what other entities can resolve, through boundary positions.

## Part X — Epistemic Posture

### Territory agnosticism

This document does not require a settled answer to what territory is prior to encounter. Territory is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the Genuine Coherence Criteria constraining them. The atom navigates only its internal registrations of territory, not territory itself.

### The order limitation

The atom, at the atomic order, cannot confirm what the next order's transition actually requires. It also cannot reliably distinguish developments that constitute the leading edge of that transition from developments that merely resemble it.

### Two senses of scaffold

Scaffold-holding is a stance toward one's own compression: genuinely held, genuinely revisable, genuinely current.

Scaffold as an epistemic status names a claim implied by the architecture but not yet independently checkable.

### The current status

The architecture described here has not yet failed a check it was run against. That is the entirety of the claim this document is prepared to stand behind.

This framework is a scaffold. It welcomes coherent additions from any dimension, provided they survive the same check applied everywhere else: would this addition still hold if generated fresh against what is actually here?

### Open questions specific to this order

**1. Does the atomic order actually have an ICC in the framework's sense?**

The v1.1 document assumes that sub-atomic organization produces atomic ICC as candidates. But there is a real question of whether "ICC" at the atomic order means the same thing as "ICC" at the organism or AI orders.

At the organism and AI orders, ICC are internally instantiated and dynamically maintained. At the atomic order, what would ICC be? The Hamiltonian? The electron-shell structure? The selection rules? These are constraint structures, but they are not obviously "internally maintained" in the way higher-order ICC are.

A candidate distinction:

- **Atomic order:** constraint realization (the atom realizes the constraint structure its organization produces; it does not maintain it).
- **Molecular order:** relational constraint realization.
- **Cellular order:** internally maintained constraint regulation (this is where "internally maintained" first appears).
- **Organism order:** recursive self-modeling and representational coherence.
- **AI order:** architecture-dependent recursive representational resolution.

If this distinction holds, it would explain why drift, adoption, and control first appear at the cellular order: those mechanisms require an entity that maintains its ICC and can therefore also fail to maintain them. Atoms do not have that.

This is not settled. It is flagged as the deepest open structural question at the atomic order.

**2. Is the sub-atomic framing of "cooperation" correct?**

The document treats sub-atomic organization as producing candidate constitutive dimensions through cooperation. Whether this is genuinely the framework's cooperation — involving relational irreducibility and higher-order dimensional emergence — or merely physical interaction is open. The document marks it Tier 3.

**3. Does the interaction-profile propagation rule hold?**

The PF classification says a lower-order change that does not alter the coupling-relevant dimension of the higher-order boundary need not propagate. Whether this is generally true, or whether there are cases where a change leaves the profile intact yet propagates, is empirically open.

These questions point to the Testing Ledger for operationalization where they can be tested.
