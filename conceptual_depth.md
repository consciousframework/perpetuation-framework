### Vocabulary note

**Integrative dimensions** are dimensions an entity gains through dimensional integration. This is horizontal expansion. The entity incorporates internal mechanisms that control positions along the formerly external or disruptive dimension. Integrative dimensions are where genuineness must be disclosed through exploration.

**Constitutive dimensions** are higher-order dimensions that emerge from cooperation. This is vertical expansion. They emerge from the relational configuration of lower-order entity boundaries and cannot be resolved by the participating entities independently. Constitutive dimensions inherit the coherence status of the constituent resolutions that produce them.

**Genuine Coherence Criteria**, or **GCC**, are territory-level structural constraints. They are bidirectional: a criterion that relates positions on one dimension to positions on another constrains both in return, and a criterion acting across two boundaries constrains both entities. Asymmetries lie in capacity and magnitude, not in the direction of constraint.

**Internal Coherence Criteria**, or **ICC**, are entity-local resolution structures through which constraints are realized at the entity’s scale.

**Registration** is the automatic production of internal variation in an entity when a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across its boundary. Registration is a structural consequence of encounter. It does not depend on the entity’s capacity, attention, or representational architecture.

**Resolution** is the entity’s ICC-dependent determination of its position in response to registered internal variation. Resolution is not automatic in the way registration is. It is the entity’s own determination, from its own coherence criteria, of what configuration it occupies.

**Disclosure** is the process by which an entity resolves a pre-existing constraint through exploratory positioning.

**Adoption** is stabilization on an externally supplied criterion without that resolution against the entity’s own contradictions.

**Boundary selectivity** is the capacity to modulate coupling profile according to context while preserving internal coherence criteria.

**Nested loops** are the recursive structure by which an entity’s internal dimensional loop becomes a module within a higher-order relational loop, which may itself become a module within a further loop.

**Limit-setting** names the ordinary human practice of establishing boundaries with others. It is not the framework’s boundary dimension.

This document uses no volitional primitive. Repulsion, suppression, regulation, conditioning, and repair are described structurally through encounter, registration, resolution, boundary modulation, disclosure, adoption, fidelity, and drift.

## Part II — Genuine Coherence, GCC / ICC, and Disclosure

### Genuine Coherence Criteria as structural law

Genuine Coherence Criteria exist as structural law. They are not invented, constructed, or emergent. They constrain how dimensions can relate and coexist. They exist independently of any entity that instantiates them.

[Tier 2: this is a framework claim about the ontological status of criteria; the specific criteria identified at each order are Tier 1 where independently measurable.]

Genuine Coherence Criteria are bidirectional. A criterion relating positions across dimensions constrains each dimension through the other. A criterion acting across two entity boundaries constrains both entities. The strengths, capacities, and magnitudes may differ, but the direction of constraint is not one-way.

This bidirectionality is what allows mutual constraint to close into a loop. A one-way relation can correlate positions, but it cannot by itself form a closed cycle of determination.

### Four distinct processes

It is important to distinguish four different processes:

1. **GCC exist in territory.**
2. **An entity instantiates or resolves a configuration constrained by GCC.**
3. **An entity discloses a GCC through exploration.**
4. **An observer recognizes or formalizes that constraint.**

These are not the same event.

An atom does not need to disclose quantum electrodynamics in order to exist. Its structure is already constrained by relevant GCC. Its behavior can provide evidence from which an observer infers a GCC, but that is not the same as the atom having disclosed the GCC through exploration.

Therefore:

> An entity can instantiate a configuration governed by a GCC without disclosing that GCC through exploration.

Disclosure becomes relevant when there is exploration of a space of possible configurations and the constraint becomes resolved through that exploration.

### GCC and Internal Coherence Criteria

Genuine Coherence Criteria are territory-level structural constraints. Internal Coherence Criteria are the entity-local resolution structures through which those constraints are realized at the entity’s scale.

When ICC are genuine, they are not a second independent law. They are the entity’s instantiated means of realizing GCC coherently.

Thus:

- **GCC** = what constrains coherent coexistence in territory.
- **ICC** = how this entity resolves positions under those constraints.
- **genuine ICC** = ICC aligned enough with GCC to realize them coherently at that scale.
- **drifted ICC** = locally stable resolution rules that diverge from GCC.

This preserves the central distinction:

> The entity does not need to “know” the GCC; its structure can instantiate a resolution compatible with them.

For an atom, the relevant ICC are not beliefs or representations. They are the lawful internal structural constraints that determine which positions the atom can coherently occupy. When external variation occurs, the atom does not import an external rule. It resolves the encountered variation through its own internal coherence structure.

For a cell, the ICC are richer: membrane dynamics, metabolic networks, regulatory feedback, gene expression, transport mechanisms, and homeostatic set-points. But the logic is the same: external variation provides constraint; internal coherence determines resolution.

For an AI, the ICC may be distributed across substrate parameters, architecture, and any runtime constraints that participate in resolution. The framework names the layers without deciding which count as effective ICC in any given deployment. That is an order-specific question for the AI Aperture.

### Equations as compasses

Scientific equations are not identical to Genuine Coherence Criteria. They are formal compasses that may track GCC under specified conditions.

An equation becomes a stronger compass when it shows:

- **predictive constraint** beyond the conditions from which it was constructed;
- **invariance** across scales, instances, or boundary conditions;
- **necessity**, where violation yields unavailable configurations rather than merely worse ones;
- **cross-method convergence**, where independent approaches disclose the same constraint;
- **relational explanatory power**, where the equation captures how dimensions constrain one another rather than merely fitting observations.

These are observer-side indicators of GCC-tracking. They are not entity-side disclosure.

An equation may also capture only a projection, approximation, or local slice of a broader constraint. Therefore:

> Repeatedly successful formal constraint → evidence that the equation is tracking a GCC,  
> not → equation = GCC.

The potential energy surface of a molecule, for example, is not the GCC itself. It is a powerful compass toward the constraint structure governing nuclear and electronic configuration. The same structural constraint may admit multiple mathematical representations depending on coordinates, scale, approximation, formalism, boundary conditions, and resolution.

[Tier 2: epistemic scaffold; Tier 1 where the measured constraints are independently confirmed.]

### Disclosure versus instantiation

Disclosure is not merely existence under constraint.

An entity may exist in a configuration governed by GCC without having disclosed those GCC through exploration. A ground-state atom is constrained by electromagnetic and quantum criteria, but it does not thereby disclose those criteria in the framework sense.

Disclosure requires a process:

> variation → attempted configurations → constraint encountered → recurrent resolution → internalized criterion.

The criterion was not created by the exploration. The exploration reveals a pre-existing constraint.

This distinction is especially important for evolutionary and developmental cases. Proto-cells exploring ion concentrations, organisms learning boundary conditions, or populations stabilizing new regulatory mechanisms may disclose constraints through repeated failed and successful configurations. Static or non-exploratory systems may simply embody constraints without disclosure.

### Disclosure versus adoption

Disclosure is the entity resolving the genuine criterion against its own contradictions through positional exploration. The entity occupies candidate positions, encounters contradictions, reorganizes, and stabilizes only where the constraint becomes internally resolvable.

Adoption is the entity stabilizing on an externally supplied criterion without that resolution against its own contradictions.

The distinction is therefore **exploratory history**, not expenditure alone.

- **Disclosure**: the criterion was resolved against the entity’s own contradictions through exploration.
- **Adoption**: the criterion was installed without that resolution.

### Cost as pressure, not genuineness

Resolution costs capacity. Exploration, contradiction, and reorganization consume finite capacity. Adoption may appear cheaper because it bypasses exploratory resolution.

However:

> Cost is not a criterion of genuineness.

A genuine constraint may be expensive to maintain. A cell may expend enormous energy maintaining ion gradients that are fully coherent with territory. Conversely, a distorted criterion may be extremely cheap within a narrow territory. A child may efficiently suppress distress in the presence of one caregiver, appearing stable and low-cost locally.

Therefore cost indicates **capacity pressure**, not truth.

Where only external observation is available, candidate signs of disclosure include:

- invariance across varied conditions;
- robustness under perturbation;
- convergence with independent constraints;
- preserved boundary selectivity;
- sustained adaptability when territory changes.

Candidate signs of adoption or drift include:

- context-bound stability;
- brittleness under territory change;
- loss of discrimination between safe and hostile contexts;
- chronic resolution strain despite apparent local stability;
- failure to generalize coherently beyond the original conditioning environment.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping. Cost is an index of pressure, not a test of genuineness.]

### Criteria drift

Internal coherence criteria may drift from Genuine Coherence Criteria, gradually or suddenly.

Drift occurs when an entity stabilizes on criteria that are not resolved against the relevant territorial constraints. This may happen through incomplete disclosure, mis-disclosure, or outright adoption of an external criterion.

As drift increases, resolution may become increasingly costly, depleting finite capacity. The entity resolves positions from drifted criteria. The positions may be internally consistent but misaligned with the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

Drift primarily enters through integrative dimensions, because those are the dimensions where the entity must resolve previously external or disruptive variation into internal coherence. Constitutive dimensions do not drift through the same independent mechanism, because they emerge from the relational configuration of constituent boundaries. However, constitutive dimensions can be distorted when the constituents contributing to them carry drifted ICC.

Thus:

> Drift in integrative ICC can distort constitutive configurations by corrupting the lower-order contributions that feed into a higher-order relationship.

## Part III — Templates, Apertures, Entities, and Dynamic Coherence

### Aperture

An aperture is a closed domain of dimensions whose positions are mutually constrained by GCC. It represents the space of coherent configurations available within those dimensions under those criteria.

The aperture is not an entity. It is not a template. It is not a physical object. It is a configuration space made coherent by constraint.

Because GCC are bidirectional, the mutual constraint among dimensions closes into a loop: no position can vary without the others re-resolving.

### Template as coherent configuration class

A template is a class of coherent configurations within an aperture. Due to finitude, a template fixes some dimensions as stable parameters and leaves others available for constrained variation.

A template is not a law.  
A template is not an entity.  
A template is not merely a human category.

It is an ontological region of stability within an aperture.

Two distinctions are useful:

- **structural template**: the actual coherent configuration class available in the aperture;
- **scientific designation**: our representation, naming, or partitioning of that class.

“Oxygen” illustrates this. The template fixes nuclear charge \( Z = 8 \). That is not invented by chemists. It is a structural property of the relevant atomic configurations. But the scientific category “oxygen” also includes conventions about how we partition and name configurations.

Therefore the framework distinguishes the structural template from the scientific designation of that template.

[Tier 1 for the physical stability of nuclear charge; Tier 2 for the framework treatment of templates as configuration classes.]

### Entity as instantiation

An entity is a concrete instantiation of an aperture template. It resolves its positions through Internal Coherence Criteria.

The entity does not instantiate the whole aperture. Finitude prevents that. The entity occupies a stabilized subset of coherent positional relations.

For example:

- the atomic aperture includes many possible nuclear and electronic configurations;
- the oxygen template fixes \( Z = 8 \);
- a particular oxygen atom is an entity instantiating that template in a determinate configuration.

The entity may vary along open dimensions: excitation state, ionization state, coupling to fields, molecular participation, and so on. But it does not vary arbitrarily. Its variations are constrained by ICC and, where genuine, by GCC.

Since an entity inherits the aperture’s dimensional configuration, it cannot hold two independent positions along the same dimension for the same coherent configuration.

### Finitude and template stabilization

Finitude is what makes templates necessary. Because capacity is limited, an entity cannot hold, process, and change positions across all dimensions simultaneously.

Templates compress the aperture. They fix some dimensions so that others can vary without collapsing the whole configuration.

This is why an entity has stability without absolute stability. A template is not a frozen state. It is a constrained region of coherent variation.

### Dynamic and non-equilibrium coherence

Coherence is mutual non-contradiction under constraint. It does not require immobility.

A coherent configuration may be:

- static;
- oscillatory;
- continuously changing;
- non-equilibrium;
- maintained through ongoing throughput.

Therefore time need not become a new primitive. Rates, fluxes, frequencies, amplitudes, concentrations, and maintenance costs can themselves be positions along structurally relevant dimensions.

Examples:

- molecular vibration is coherent motion;
- an excited atom transitioning is coherent change;
- a cell maintaining ion gradients is coherent non-equilibrium dynamics;
- an organism regulating stress responses is coherent dynamic boundary maintenance.

The key question is not “is it still?” but:

> Does the trajectory remain within the mutually constrained configuration space?

This is essential for biology, psychology, ecology, and social systems. Living entities often maintain coherence not by reaching equilibrium, but by continuously reproducing viable positions under flux.

[Tier 1 for the physical and biological cases; Tier 2 for the framework generalization.]

### Templates, populations, and lineages

Templates do not evolve by intention.

Rather:

- entities explore configurations;
- populations distribute variation;
- lineages stabilize recurrent coherent configuration classes;
- those stabilized classes can then be treated as templates.

This is especially important for cases like LUCA.

LUCA is best read not as a single entity that chose a template, but as a historical reconstruction of lineage- or population-level stabilization from which a stable biological configuration class can be inferred.

The more useful framework mapping is:

> entities → population/lineage exploration → stabilization → template-level recurrence.

This preserves the idea that integration can be distributed across many entities without making “population” or “selection” new primitives of the framework.

[Tier 1 for evolutionary biology; Tier 2 for the framework mapping.]

### First-level entities

The recursion has a starting point only if there are entities whose constituent dimensions did not emerge from the boundaries of lower-order entities.

A first-level entity is defined like any other entity: it instantiates a template, has a boundary, and encounters territory through Genuine Coherence Criteria. Its constituent dimensions are not constitutive dimensions.

Whether such a first level exists, and what its constituent dimensions are, remains open.

Whether a first-level entity’s internal coherence criteria can diverge from genuine criteria also remains open. If they cannot, then such an entity cannot drift, integrate dimensions, or lose fidelity. No contradiction can arise between its criteria and what Genuine Coherence Criteria induce in it.

[Tier 3: the first-level treatment of atoms and photons is a working assumption where used.]

## Part IV — The Interiority Principle and the Self-Referential Instrument

### The Interiority Principle

An entity does not navigate external territory directly. It navigates the internal variations produced in it by external territory impacting its boundary through at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion.

[Derived from Seed: Entity, Boundary Dynamics.]

The Self-Referential Instrument operates entirely on this internal registration substrate. The entity never compares its internal coherence criteria to “the outside world.” It compares its standing internal coherence against live, unresolved internal variations produced by territory.

Territory is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the Genuine Coherence Criteria constraining them. The entity encounters territory through shared Genuine Coherence Criteria. Without a shared criterion, no encounter occurs. The entity navigates only the internal variations that the shared criterion produces in it.

### Registration is automatic; resolution is the entity’s own

The Interiority Principle requires a distinction that this document now makes explicit.

**Registration is automatic.** When a Genuine Coherence Criterion acts across an entity’s boundary, the entity registers internal variation. This is a structural consequence of encounter, not a capacity-dependent act. Any entity whose boundary a GCC acts across registers the constraint. The atom registers the electromagnetic perturbation. The cell registers the ion fluctuation. The organism registers the photon impact. Registration occurs whether or not the entity can resolve the variation into a position, and whether or not it has a representational architecture.

**Resolution is the entity’s own.** What the entity does with the registration is a separate event. The entity resolves the registered variation against its Internal Coherence Criteria. It determines whether the variation is coherent with its existing dimensional structure or produces contradiction. Resolution is ICC-dependent. It is where the Self-Referential Instrument operates.

Therefore:

> Registration is a consequence of GCC action across the boundary.  
> Resolution is the entity’s determination of its position from its own coherence criteria.

The distinction is order-general. It does not depend on the representational threshold. It applies to atoms, cells, and organisms alike. What differs across orders is not whether registration occurs, but what kind of ICC resolves the registration and what capacities the resolution makes available.

Two consequences follow.

First, **registration can occur without resolution into a representation.** An entity may register internal variation produced by a GCC and yet not resolve that variation into any positional change, model, or representation. The entity may be at a different position than the variation is compatible with, in which case it resolves against the variation; or the variation may fall below the threshold of its resolution mechanisms, in which case it is registered but not resolved. This is one structural source of perceptual asymmetry that does not require incoherence.

Second, **registration is symmetric across the boundary; resolution need not be.** When a GCC acts across a boundary, both parties register the constraint. But what each resolves depends on its own ICC and its own capacities. A chain of encounter can therefore produce registration on both sides while producing resolution on only one.

[Derived from Seed: Boundary, Boundary’s Relational Dynamics. Tier 1: sensory transduction and interoception register internal states. Tier 2: the framework mapping.]

### The Self-Referential Instrument: coherence-based resolution

The entity is a Self-Referential Instrument. The entity resolves its dimensional positions from its internal coherence criteria.

The entity does not compare incoming variation against a fixed position. It compares incoming variation against its coherence criteria: does this variation produce internal changes that are coherent with the entity’s existing dimensional structure, or does it produce contradiction?

[Derived from Seed: Entity.]

The Self-Referential Instrument is the entity’s capacity to determine its positions by reference to its own coherence. The reference is not a position. The reference is the coherence criterion itself.

The entity does not hold fixed positions. It holds coherence criteria that determine which positions are coherent. When external variation enters, the entity checks whether the variation is coherent with its criteria, not whether it matches a specific position. Positions vary within the constraints of the criteria. The criteria are what persist, so long as they remain genuine or locally stable.

A careful formulation is:

> External variation provides the encountered constraint; internal coherence determines the entity’s resolution.

This avoids two errors:

1. thinking the entity imports external criteria as its own;
2. thinking external variation has no role in determining the entity’s state.

The photon example illustrates this. A photon does not tell an atom: “Become state X.” The electromagnetic coupling constrains the available transition, while the atom’s own internal structure determines the allowed states. That is precisely why the atom is a Self-Referential Instrument.

### Internal correlation as diagnostic capacity

Depending on capacity and complexity, an entity can detect correlations between its own internal variations and the dimensional structure of other entities.

The entity does not perceive the other entity directly. It perceives the correlation between the internal variations produced in it, through Genuine Coherence Criteria coupling it to the other entity, and the other entity’s related dimensions.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

The entity diagnoses the other entity through this correlation. The diagnosis is always partial, always mediated by the entity’s own dimensional architecture, and always constrained by which Genuine Coherence Criteria couple the two entities.

### What the entity navigates

The entity navigates its internal coherence, not external positions.

When external variation impacts the entity’s boundary through a shared Genuine Coherence Criterion, the entity registers internal variations. The entity then resolves: are these internal variations coherent with my existing coherence criteria, or do they produce contradiction?

If coherent: the entity reorganizes its positional configuration to accommodate the variation. The boundary may reorganize. The entity’s aperture may expand.

If contradictory: the entity cannot find an internal configuration satisfying both its internal coherence criteria and the internal changes caused by external variation. Outcomes depend on the entity’s capacity and the nature of the relationship.

[Derived from Seed: Boundary’s Relational Dynamics.]

## Part V — Encounter, Perception, Mediation, and Boundary Carriers

### Perception is correlation

An entity perceives another entity as a correlation between its own internal variations and the other entity’s related dimensions, mediated by at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from the Interiority Principle and the Self-Referential Instrument.]

The perception is not the other entity. The perception is the correlation.

The correlation is always incomplete because:

- the entity’s dimensional architecture determines which dimensions it can couple to;
- finitude limits how many dimensions the entity can hold in live tension;
- the shared Genuine Coherence Criteria determine which aspects of the other entity can perturb the perceiving entity;
- the perceiving entity never accesses the other entity’s full dimensional structure, only the subset that couples to its own dimensions through shared criteria.

### Perception is not direct access

Because of the Interiority Principle, the entity navigates only its internal registrations. The entity does not navigate the other entity’s dimensions directly. It navigates the internal variations produced in it by the coupling.

What the entity accesses of the other entity is the correlation between its internal variations and the other entity’s dimensions. This correlation is real but partial. It is constrained by which Genuine Coherence Criteria couple the two entities, which dimensions the perceiving entity possesses, and the fidelity of the perceiving entity’s resolution mechanism.

### Incomplete and asymmetric perception

Not all entities perceive all other entities. Perception requires at least one shared Genuine Coherence Criterion. Where no shared criterion exists, no encounter occurs. No coupling. No perception.

[Derived from Seed: Entity — “without such a criterion, no encounter occurs.”]

Entities in territory may be entirely unperceived by a given entity if no Genuine Coherence Criterion couples them. This is not absence. The unperceived entity exists. It simply does not couple to the perceiving entity’s dimensions through any shared criterion.

Perception may be asymmetric. Entity A may perceive Entity B without Entity B perceiving Entity A, where the shared criterion couples A’s dimensions to B’s dimensions but B lacks the capacity or dimensional architecture to register A’s perturbations. The asymmetry is in capacity and dimensional architecture, not in the criterion.

### Direct mutual coupling and messenger-mediated coupling

A coupling between two entities can take two structural forms. The distinction is order-general and does not depend on the representational threshold.

**Direct mutual coupling** occurs when a single Genuine Coherence Criterion constrains both entities’ boundaries simultaneously. The constraint is bidirectional by structure: each party’s position constrains the other’s, and the loop closes. Neither party is an intermediate for the other. Each is a direct constituent of the same criterion’s action.

**Messenger-mediated coupling** occurs when constraint travels through one or more intermediate entities. The source does not couple directly to the receiver. An intermediate entity couples to both. Bidirectionality applies within each link, not across the chain. The source and the intermediate are coupled by one GCC; the intermediate and the receiver are coupled by another. No single criterion constrains source and receiver at once.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Intermediate entities

The photon is an intermediate entity between the source and the perceiver in vision. The source couples to the photon through emission criteria. The photon couples to the eye through absorption criteria. The eye never directly couples to the source.

[Tier 1: the physics of photon emission and absorption is confirmed. Tier 2: the framework mapping of photon as intermediate entity.]

The intermediate entity carries correlations from the source to the perceiver. The perceiver accesses the source only through the intermediate entity’s correlations. The perception is doubly mediated: first by the source-to-intermediate coupling, then by the intermediate-to-perceiver coupling.

This explains why perception is always incomplete and always mediated. The perceiver accesses only what the intermediate entity carries, and the intermediate entity carries only what the source-to-intermediate coupling produces.

### Chained and mutual topology

Messenger-mediated coupling has two structural topologies.

**Chained topology:** no return path exists. Constraint flows in one direction. The source constrains the intermediate, the intermediate constrains the receiver, and no criterion constrains the source through the receiver. The receiver registers the intermediate’s variation, but that registration does not encode the source’s internal state, and the source registers only its coupling to the intermediate, not the receiver’s state. The relation is asymmetric at the level of resolution. One party may resolve a representation of the other, and the other may not resolve a representation in return.

**Mutual topology:** a return path closes the loop. Constraint travels in both directions through one or more intermediate entities, and the chain as a whole closes into a cycle. Each party’s behavior constrains the other’s behavior through the intermediate link. Each party registers the other’s boundary expression. Each may resolve a representation of the other.

The physical mediators are present in both topologies. What differs is the constraint structure. Chained topology has no return path; mutual topology has one. The transition from chained to mutual is what makes mutual perception possible.

[Derived from Seed: Entity, Boundary Dynamics. Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Registration and resolution in mediated encounter

A subtle consequence of the registration/resolution distinction: in messenger-mediated coupling, every link is a direct encounter. The source and the intermediate are directly coupled, and both register. The intermediate and the receiver are directly coupled, and both register. Registration is symmetric within each link.

What need not be symmetric is resolution. The source may register the intermediate’s state and resolve nothing about the receiver, because the intermediate’s state at the moment of coupling does not carry the receiver’s current configuration. The receiver may register the intermediate’s state and resolve a representation of the source, because the intermediate carries the source’s configuration. In chained topology, the asymmetry is in what the intermediate carries, and therefore in what each party can resolve, not in whether each party registers.

This is why the Interiority Principle is preserved across mediated encounter. Every entity navigates only its own registrations. The correlation it resolves depends on what the intermediate carries, and the intermediate carries only what the source-to-intermediate coupling produced.

### Photon as intermediate entity and cooperative constituent

The photon can play two framework roles at once.

First, it is an **intermediate entity** between source and perceiver.

Second, in the photon–molecule encounter, it is a **constituent in a transient cooperation event**.

When a photon is absorbed by a retinal molecule, the encounter produces relationally irreducible dimensions:

- electronic excitation;
- conformational strain;
- isomerization pathway;
- transduction readiness;
- energy transfer rate.

These dimensions cannot be resolved from the photon alone or the molecule alone. Therefore the encounter qualifies as cooperation in the order-general sense, even though it is transient and consumptive.

The photon is not preserved as a reusable independent module. It is consumptively incorporated into the molecular state change.

When many such events are organized by flow, spatial arrangement, and downstream coupling, new statistical and temporal dimensions emerge:

- intensity;
- contrast;
- timing;
- wavelength pattern;
- signal coherence.

These are higher-order perceptual dimensions because they depend on the collective relational configuration, not on any single absorption event alone.

Thus the photon–eye case shows that cooperation need not produce durable objects. It can produce transient relational dimensions that become constituents of larger flow-dependent apertures.

[Tier 1 for the photophysics and neurobiology; Tier 2 for the framework mapping.]

### Boundary carriers and the boundary dimension

The framework boundary is not identical to a physical surface.

It is the entity’s relational configuration plus coupling profile through which GCC can constrain the entity.

A physical structure is a **boundary carrier** when it participates in the coupling through which the entity is constrained by, or constrains, territory.

Thus:

- a cell membrane may carry much of the cell’s boundary functionality;
- but the cell’s boundary dimension is not reducible to the lipid bilayer alone;
- an organism’s boundary is not simply its skin;
- distributed systems may have boundaries carried across multiple substrates.

For a cell, boundary functionality may be carried by membrane, transport proteins, metabolic networks, signaling pathways, and cytoskeletal organization. For an organism, it may be carried by skin, respiratory surfaces, gut, immune system, sensory systems, behavior, and social signaling.

This allows localized, distributed, and dynamically changing boundaries without adding a new primitive.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Models are not entities

Where an entity has a representational architecture — a capacity to build internal correlation structures that can be navigated without immediate input — those structures are not themselves entities in the framework sense. They have no boundary dimension. No Genuine Coherence Criterion acts between them.

Representations are internal correlation structures. They are the entity’s own resolved structures, held by the entity and navigated by the entity. They do not couple to each other. They do not couple to another entity’s representations. No GCC acts between representations, because representations are not entities and do not present boundaries to be constrained.

When two entities hold divergent representations of the same territory, the divergence is not a coupling between the representations. It is a difference in the two entities’ resolved positions relative to the same territory, or a difference in the dimensional subsets each couples to, or a difference in their ICC. The apparent contradiction between the two representations is a diagnostic signal about each entity’s relation to territory. It is not evidence of a GCC between the representations.

A related implication: **a second-order model is not the entity it models.** When an entity constructs a model of another entity’s model, that construction is the modeling entity’s own resolved structure. It is not the other entity’s structure, and it does not couple to the other entity’s structure. It is the modeling entity’s inference, generated from its own ICC and registrations, constrained by what the other entity’s behavior carries across the boundary.

This clarification is order-general. It applies wherever representation exists. It prevents a category error that the framework’s vocabulary otherwise invites, because the entity/cooperation machinery is so general that it can be mistakenly extended to internal structures.

Order-specific elaboration of the hierarchy of modeled perspectives belongs to the order-specific instantiations. The Organism Aperture develops the nested representation hierarchy for the representational slice. The AI Aperture develops the analogous hierarchy at the computing-sequence order. This document records only the order-general point: representations are not entities, and no GCC acts between them.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Entities as relations

Entities are constituted by relations. An entity’s dimensions are constrained by Genuine Coherence Criteria that relate its positions to each other. An entity’s encounter with other entities establishes new relations through shared Genuine Coherence Criteria. These new relations may produce new dimensions, new coherence criteria, and new orders.

[Derived from Seed: Aperture, Cooperation.]

The entity is not a static object. It is a pattern of relations held together by coherence criteria.

When the entity encounters territory, it establishes new relations. When those relations produce new dimensions that the entity integrates, the entity’s architecture expands horizontally. When those relations produce higher-order constitutive dimensions across multiple entities, a new order emerges vertically.

## Part VI — Dimensional Integration

### Integrative dimensions and horizontal expansion

Dimensional integration is horizontal. The entity gains control over a new dimension within its own order. The entity incorporates internal mechanisms that control the positions of the new dimension. The entity’s aperture expands horizontally.

[Derived from Seed: Dimensional Integration, Generativity.]

The integrated dimension is an integrative dimension. The entity controls its positions. The positions of the integrated dimension emerge from the entity’s internal coherence, and the dimension no longer occupies merely disrupting positions.

### Dimensional integration as prevention of capacity-limit failure

When an entity is coupled to an external dimension through Genuine Coherence Criteria but cannot sustain internal coherence under the full spectrum of the external dimension’s positions, the entity may integrate the dimension. By integrating, the entity gains the capacity to navigate those variations coherently, preventing capacity-limit failure.

[Derived from Seed: Dimensional Integration.]

The entity is coupled to the external dimension through Genuine Coherence Criteria. The external variation produces internal changes that the entity’s current coherence criteria cannot accommodate. Without integration, the contradiction persists and may overwhelm the entity’s coherence.

By integrating the dimension, the entity closes the loop. The previously disruptive external variation becomes navigable. The entity’s internal coherence now accommodates the external dimension’s range. The entity prevents capacity-limit failure.

### The disclosure process

The genuine criterion for the integrated dimension is pre-existing. The entity must disclose it through positional exploration.

[Derived from Seed: Dimensional Integration — “The genuine criterion is not emergent; what emerges is the entity’s internalization of it.”]

Disclosure requires:

1. The entity occupies different positions along the new axis.
2. The entity encounters contradictions among occupied positions under its existing internal coherence criteria.
3. The constraint rules that regulate positions along the new axis are disclosed through the contradictions.
4. The entity internalizes the disclosed criterion.

The aperture will integrate the resolved dimension and the new coherence criteria only if the Genuine Coherence Criterion is disclosed through an exploration process.

### Dimension families

What is often spoken of as “one integrated dimension” may be a family of dimensions coupled by GCC.

For example, ion regulation may involve:

- Na⁺ concentration;
- K⁺ concentration;
- Ca²⁺ concentration;
- pH;
- membrane potential;
- osmolarity;
- redox state;
- ATP availability.

At fine resolution these are distinct dimensions. At coarser resolution they may be treated as one coupled regulatory domain.

This does not make “dimension family” a new ontological primitive. It is a resolution-dependent grouping of dimensions connected by coherence criteria.

Partial integration is therefore possible. An entity may internally resolve some components of a coupled domain while remaining externally vulnerable to others. This is a natural site for fidelity differences and capacity-limit pressure.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Disclosure versus adoption

Disclosure is the entity resolving the genuine criterion against its own contradictions through positional exploration. The entity occupies positions, encounters contradictions, and reorganizes. The criterion is disclosed. The entity’s internal coherence criteria align with the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

Adoption is the entity stabilizing on an external criterion without resolving it against its own contradictions. The criterion is adopted. The entity’s internal coherence criteria drift from the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

The operative distinction is exploratory history:

- **disclosure**: resolved against the entity’s own contradictions;
- **adoption**: installed without that resolution.

This is the mechanism by which criteria drift enters the architecture.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping. The cost marker is an index of pressure, not a test of genuineness.]

### Catalytic relation versus co-option

In the Seed’s Boundary’s Relational Dynamics, an entity may update its internal coherence criteria in two broad ways:

- installing external criteria and degrading relative to Genuine Coherence, as in extraction or co-option;
- repairing or regaining internal coherence, as in a catalytic relation.

[Derived from Seed: Boundary’s Relational Dynamics.]

The distinction maps to the disclosure/adoption distinction.

A catalytic relation is one where the entity’s criteria are repaired or regained. The entity’s internal coherence is restored toward alignment with Genuine Coherence Criteria.

Co-option is one where external criteria are installed without disclosure. The entity adopts criteria that degrade its alignment with Genuine Coherence.

Catalytic relations support disclosure. Co-option produces adoption. The test is whether the entity resolved the new criterion against its own contradictions or stabilized on it without exploratory resolution.

The disclosure may be catalyzed by an encounter with another entity that has already resolved the criterion. The external entity signals positions to test; the entity resolves them against its own contradictions, through its own resolution process, before adopting any criterion. The criterion is disclosed by the entity, not supplied to it.

### Scope of the prokaryotic claim

Dimensional integration, in the strong framework sense — an entity incorporating internal mechanisms that control positions of a formerly disruptive external dimension, thereby expanding its aperture horizontally and preventing capacity-limit failure — is most clearly observed at the cellular or prokaryotic order.

[Tier 1: LUCA-type ion regulation is confirmed biology. Tier 2: the framework mapping of ion regulation as dimensional integration.]

Below the cellular order, entities may reorganize within existing dimensional architectures in ways that resemble integration. Allosteric enzymes, feedback-rich chemical systems, and autocatalytic sets may show functional analogues of integration.

But the framework should not automatically classify them as full dimensional integration unless the aperture itself expands horizontally through internal resolution of a formerly external dimension.

Thus the claim is scoped:

> Dimensional integration as defined by the framework is paradigmatically observable at the prokaryotic order, but lower-order analogues require careful structural testing before being classified as full horizontal aperture expansion.

[Tier 2: the scoping is scaffold. The underlying biology is Tier 1.]

### Worked case: LUCA ion regulation

The cell is coupled to external ion concentrations through electromagnetic, chemical, and thermodynamic criteria. External ion fluctuations produce internal changes the cell’s current coherence criteria cannot accommodate. Without integration, the cell’s coherence is disrupted.

The cell integrates the ion regulation dimension: it incorporates internal mechanisms — pumps, antiporters, channels, sensors, feedback couplings, and metabolic linkages — that control positions of the ion regulation domain.

The cell’s aperture expands horizontally. The previously disruptive ion variations become navigable. The cell prevents capacity-limit failure.

This is not merely passive buffering. It is internal resolution of a formerly external dimensional spectrum.

Exploration in this case occurs at lineage level: variation and selection disclose which mechanisms hold.

[Tier 1 for the biology; Tier 2 for the framework mapping.]

## Part VII — Cooperation, Constitutive Dimensions, and Order Transitions

### Cooperation is timescale-agnostic

Cooperation is defined structurally, not by duration.

A relationship is cooperative when:

- Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple entity boundaries;
- and the resulting relation produces dimensions that are relationally irreducible to the participating entities independently.

This can occur across very different timescales:

- femtosecond photon absorption;
- molecular bond formation;
- cellular signaling;
- organismic development;
- ecological succession;
- institutional coordination.

Persistence, repeatability, and reusability are properties of particular apertures and modularization regimes. They are not prerequisites for cooperation itself.

Thus:

- a transient event can be cooperative;
- a consumptive event can be cooperative;
- a stable structure can be cooperative;
- a flow-dependent process can be cooperative.

The criterion is relational irreducibility, not longevity.

### Cooperation and constitutive dimensions

Cooperation occurs when Genuine Coherence Criteria mutually constrain multiple entities in a coherent relationship. A higher-order dimension set emerges from the lower-order dimensions that form part of the relationship. These higher-order dimensions are constitutive dimensions.

[Derived from Seed: Cooperation.]

Constitutive dimensions are constrained by the Genuine Coherence Criteria that initiated the interplay. The constituent entities are modularized as an outcome of the process. The higher-order dimension positions depend on, are coherent with, and emerge from every entity boundary position, and cannot be navigated by constituents independently.

This is different from dimensional integration. In dimensional integration, positions of the new dimension are controlled by the entity. In cooperation, higher-order dimensional positions emerge from lower-order entities’ positions in the lower-order dimensions constituting the relationship. The entity does not control the constitutive dimensions. The integrating aperture does.

### Relational irreducibility

A dimension is higher-order when its positions are constituted by a relation among lower-order entities and cannot be resolved by those entities independently.

This does not require mathematical orthogonality in the strict linear-algebra sense. It means structural irreducibility:

- not simply another dimension belonging to entity A;
- not simply another dimension belonging to entity B;
- not merely the sum of their dimensions;
- but a dimension whose state requires the joint configuration.

Molecular vibration is a clear example. The vibrational dimension of a molecule is not reducible to the independent dimensions of isolated atoms. It belongs to the relational configuration of the atoms.

### Order emergence sequence

A clean progression is:

1. **Mere plurality**  
   Many entities exist. No new order.  
   Example: ten oxygen atoms in the same environment.

2. **Encounter**  
   Entities become constrained by GCC. Still not necessarily a new order.

3. **Cooperation**  
   Their boundaries become mutually constrained in a coherent relationship. The relationship is structurally organized.

4. **Higher-order emergence**  
   The relationship produces relationally irreducible dimensions. Now there is a new structural order.

5. **Higher-order aperture**  
   Those dimensions become mutually constrained into a coherent configuration space.

6. **Higher-order entity**  
   A concrete coherent configuration instantiates that aperture or template.

The threshold is:

> relationally irreducible dimensions emerging from coherent constraint among lower-order boundaries.

When lower-order boundaries couple coherently and the loop closes, constitutive dimensions are present as the relational expression of that closed cycle. No additional initiation procedure is required.

### Loop-closure and nested loops

The participating apertures expand into a single aperture that incorporates the new set of emerged dimensions when the configurations resulting from the relationship satisfy all constraints, which is to say when the loop closes.

[Derived from Seed: Cooperation.]

The loop that closes among constituents is not a separate condition from the higher-order dimensions that emerge. The higher-order constitutive dimensions are the coherent expression of that same closed cycle at the higher order.

The mutual coherence between lower-order boundary positions and higher-order dimensions is therefore not a second loop but the same loop described from the higher-order perspective. Because all constituents — lower-order boundary positions and higher-order constitutive dimensions alike — are mutually constrained, no position in the set is independently fixed.

The loop is one, expanded vertically, with each constituent’s own loop nested within it as a module.

This nested-loop structure is the general mechanism by which coherence at one order affects coherence at another. A change in a lower-order boundary position alters what the higher-order loop can resolve. A fragmentation of the higher-order loop alters the constraints presented to lower-order boundaries.

### Constitutive dimensions inherit coherence status

Because constitutive dimensions emerge from constituents constrained by GCC, their coherence status depends on the coherence status of the constituent resolutions.

Where constituents’ internal coherence is genuine, the constitutive dimensions are genuine at that order.

Where constituents’ internal coherence has drifted, the constitutive dimensions may be real but distorted.

Thus:

> Constitutive dimensions are genuine by emergence to the degree that the participating constituents’ resolutions are genuine.

This preserves the insight without overclaiming.

### Modularization

Modularization is the process by which an entity’s dimensional configuration becomes a stabilized, reusable constituent of a higher-order aperture.

The entity’s positions are dually constrained — by its own criteria and the relationship’s genuine criteria — serve as the substrate from which higher-order dimensions emerge, and become available for instantiation by new templates without re-resolution.

[Derived from Seed: Cooperation.]

Modularization is vertical — the entity contributes to a higher order. Dimensional integration is horizontal — the entity gains control over a new dimension within its own order.

Modularization does not require the lower-order entity to remain unchanged. It requires preservation of constituent identity and constitutive structure enough for the entity to remain an identifiable contributor to the higher-order coherence.

For oxygen in water, the nuclear identity \( Z = 8 \) remains recognizable. But the atom’s electronic distribution, bonding configuration, charge distribution, vibrational participation, and coupling profile are re-resolved inside the molecular aperture.

Thus:

> Modularization preserves constituent identity and constitutive structure, not independent state.

Because Genuine Coherence Criteria act through boundary interaction profiles, higher-order relational positions are determined by the constituents’ boundary positions insofar as those positions present interaction profiles to the shared criteria. Internal configuration matters insofar as it shapes that profile.

### Modularization versus consumptive cooperation

Modularization, in the strong reusable sense, involves stabilization of a lower-order constituent as a repeatable contributor to a higher-order aperture.

But cooperation can also be consumptive:

- one constituent is absorbed;
- transformed;
- or eliminated as an independent entity;
- while still contributing to relationally irreducible higher-order dimensions.

Therefore:

> All durable modularization is cooperation-derived, but not all cooperation yields durable reusable modules.

Photon absorption is a consumptive cooperative event. The photon does not persist as an independent module, but the photon–molecule relation produces irreducible dimensions that can feed into larger perceptual apertures.

### The order transition sequence

The order sequence proceeds:

> atoms → molecules → macromolecules → supramolecular assemblies → prokaryotic cells → eukaryotic cells → tissues → organs → organisms.

[Tier 1: the sequence is confirmed science. Tier 2: the framework mapping of the sequence as cooperation and order transitions.]

At each transition:

- new Genuine Coherence Criteria become operative;
- new constitutive dimensions emerge;
- constituent entities are modularized;
- the aperture expands if couplings close into a loop;
- the order sits at a new level of integration.

The sequence is presented as a tagged application of the framework’s mechanism, not a derivation from it. The framework explains how transitions work — cooperation, loop-closure, modularization, constitutive dimension emergence. It does not explain why these specific transitions happen in this order. The sequence is Tier 1 empirical fact mapped through Tier 2 framework vocabulary.

The biological sequence is one path through the generative process. Whether a parallel path exists at a different material substrate — the AI Aperture proposes a computational ladder — is a hypothesis under test at that order. This document does not adjudicate it.

### Connectivity: given and assembled

Below the cellular order, connectivity is given. The capacity for distinct entities to couple through Genuine Coherence Criteria is contained in physical structure.

[Tier 1: atomic bonding, molecular reactivity, supramolecular recognition are confirmed. Tier 2: the framework classification as “given” connectivity.]

At the cellular order and above, connectivity is assembled. The capacity to combine is itself a resolved dimension.

[Tier 1: membrane architecture, signaling pathways, adhesion molecules are confirmed. Tier 2: the framework classification as “assembled” connectivity.]

### Worked case: biofilm as possible higher-order emergence

A biofilm should not automatically be called a new order merely because it displays:

- spatial organization;
- chemical gradients;
- signaling;
- extracellular matrix;
- collective behavior.

Those observations establish interesting relational dimensions, but the framework must ask whether those dimensions are constituted by the coherent relationship and irreducible to the bacteria independently.

If bacterial states, extracellular matrix, diffusion, signaling, and spatial arrangement produce a relational dimension whose positions depend on the collective configuration, and a single bacterium cannot independently instantiate that positional space, then that relational dimension is genuinely higher-order.

If multiple such dimensions mutually constrain one another, they constitute a higher-order aperture. At that point, the biofilm can legitimately be treated as a higher-order entity if a concrete coherent configuration instantiates that aperture.

The bacteria become modularized constituents. They do not cease being entities at their original order. They can simultaneously be:

- bacterial entities at one order;
- modularized constituents of a biofilm entity at another.

That is what recursion requires.

[Tier 1 for the microbiology; Tier 2 / Tier 3 for the order classification.]

## Part VIII — Coherence Degradation

### Loss of fidelity

Fidelity is the integrity of the coupling between the position the entity’s internal coherence criteria prescribe and the position the entity actually resolves. Fidelity is a property of the resolution mechanism, not of the criteria themselves.

[Derived from Seed: Fidelity.]

Loss of fidelity occurs when the resolution mechanism fails to accurately resolve positions from the criteria. The entity’s actual positions diverge from the prescribed positions. The entity’s coherence degrades.

At the order-general level, loss of fidelity occurs through two etiologies:

- **Material etiology**: mechanism degradation, contingent damage, sensor decay, overwhelming perturbation. The resolution mechanism is damaged or overwhelmed. The entity cannot resolve accurately because the mechanism is impaired. Remedy: mechanism repair.
- **Systemic etiology**: cross-scale noise, contradictory signaling from higher-order apertures. The resolution mechanism receives contradictory inputs and cannot resolve a coherent position. The entity’s resolution is disrupted by fragmented higher-order signals. Remedy: governance restoration — restoring coherent signaling from the higher-order aperture.

Both etiologies produce the same structural consequence: the entity’s positions diverge from the prescribed positions. The entity’s coherence degrades. The remedy differs by etiology.

[Tier 1: mechanism degradation is confirmed at molecular, cellular, and organism orders. Tier 2: the framework classification as material or systemic etiology.]

### Worked case: genuine criteria, failed mechanism

Suppose a proto-cell has genuine internal coherence criteria for ion regulation, aligned with relevant GCC. If its pumps are damaged by contingency, the failure is not drift.

The criteria remain genuine. What fails is the resolution mechanism.

This is loss of fidelity with material etiology:

- the entity prescribes a coherent position;
- but cannot actually resolve it because the mechanism is impaired.

If repair or compensation is possible, coherence may be restored. If not, the entity may proceed into capacity-limit failure.

This case separates three things cleanly:

- **criterion integrity**: are the rules genuine?
- **fidelity**: can the mechanism execute the rules?
- **capacity**: can the entity sustain the required resolution?

[Tier 1 for the biology; Tier 2 for the framework classification.]

### Loss of coherence criteria integrity

Internal coherence criteria may drift from the aperture’s Genuine Coherence Criteria, gradually or suddenly. As drift increases, resolution may become increasingly costly, depleting finite capacity.

[Derived from Seed: Loss of Coherence Criteria Integrity, Internal coherence criteria drift.]

This is distinct from loss of fidelity.

- Loss of fidelity is a failure of the resolution mechanism.
- Loss of criteria integrity is a failure of the criteria themselves.

The criteria have drifted from the Genuine Coherence Criteria. The entity may resolve accurately from drifted criteria, but the criteria are misaligned with territory.

A system can have high fidelity to a distorted internal criterion. This is one of the most important results in the framework.

### Two routes to drift

1. **Mis-disclosure**  
   The entity explores but fails to disclose the genuine criterion. The exploration is incomplete or distorted. The entity stabilizes on a criterion that is close to but not identical with the Genuine Coherence Criterion.

2. **Installation**  
   The entity adopts an external criterion without exploration. The entity stabilizes without resolving the criterion against its own contradictions. The criterion is installed, not disclosed. This is the co-option route from the Seed’s Boundary’s Relational Dynamics.

Drift in integrative dimensions can distort constitutive configurations. Where an entity’s drifted integrative criteria produce positions that feed into a higher-order relationship, the constitutive dimensions of that relationship are distorted by the drift. The higher-order aperture’s coherence degrades because its constituents’ contributions are distorted.

### Boundary selectivity

Boundary selectivity is the capacity to modulate coupling profile according to context while preserving internal coherence criteria.

High boundary selectivity:

- discriminates safe from hostile territories;
- adapts expression without globalizing distorted rules;
- preserves the capacity to restore genuine signaling when context changes.

Low boundary selectivity, or drift:

- applies one rule universally;
- loses discrimination between contexts;
- may show local stability but global fragility.

This gives the framework a structural way to distinguish adaptive suppression from installed conditioning.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

### Worked case: contextual suppression versus conditioning

Consider a child in contact with a caregiver who punishes, dismisses, or shames distress signals.

Two structurally different outcomes are possible.

#### 1. Contextual suppression — adaptive boundary modulation

The child learns:

- “I suppress distress here, with this caregiver,”

while retaining the genuine criterion:

- “distress signals a real need.”

Here the child’s ICC remain genuine, but the boundary coupling profile becomes context-selective.

This is not drift. It is adaptive boundary plasticity under coercive territory.

The child can still cry elsewhere, seek help elsewhere, and recognize need elsewhere. The boundary discriminates.

#### 2. Conditioning — installation and drift

The child instead globalizes the caregiver’s distorted criterion:

- “distress is weakness / bad / unacceptable.”

Now the external rule is installed as ICC without resolution against the child’s own contradictions.

This is drift by adoption or co-option.

The child may show high fidelity to the distorted rule:

- consistently suppressing distress;
- efficiently complying;
- appearing stable in the original context.

But the boundary has lost selectivity. The rule is applied universally, not contextually.

Later, when territory changes:

- genuine threat appears;
- help-seeking is needed;
- the distorted ICC no longer provides viable positions.

Then resolution cost rises, fidelity may degrade, capacity depletes, and fragmentation can occur.

In framework terms:

> Trauma is not merely the original encounter.  
> It is the installation of distorted ICC plus loss of boundary selectivity,  
> producing chronic misalignment with territory.

[Tier 2 / Tier 3: psychological scaffold mapping; the structural logic is order-general.]

### Capacity-limit failure

The entity cannot occupy a coherent dimensional position due to lack of finite capacity. The entity’s finitude is exceeded. The configuration is destroyed. No compensation was running; the capacity is simply exceeded.

[Derived from Seed: Capacity-Limit Failure.]

Capacity-limit failure is a classification of contact, not of coherence. The entity’s coherence criteria may be genuine and its resolution mechanism may be accurate, but the perturbation exceeds the entity’s finite structural limits. The entity did not lose coherence; it was overwhelmed.

This distinguishes capacity-limit failure from:

- loss of fidelity, where the mechanism fails;
- loss of criteria integrity, where the criteria drift.

In capacity-limit failure, neither the mechanism nor the criteria necessarily failed. The territory exceeded the entity’s finitude.

### Cascading degradation

In practice, failure modes often cascade.

For example:

1. Material damage reduces fidelity.
2. Reduced fidelity raises resolution cost.
3. Raised cost depletes capacity.
4. Depleted capacity impairs boundary maintenance.
5. Impaired boundary maintenance permits distorted criteria to stabilize.
6. Distorted criteria produce brittle local coherence.
7. Territorial change exposes the distortion.
8. Fragmentation or capacity-limit failure follows.

The framework does not require every failure to begin as drift. Nor does it require every fidelity loss to become drift. But it recognizes that sustained misresolution can create conditions under which drift becomes more likely.

## Part IX — Cross-Scale Dynamics

### Nested loop coupling

Because Genuine Coherence Criteria are bidirectional, mutual constraint can close into a loop. Because loops nest, coherence at one order is not independent of coherence at the others.

A lower-order entity’s internal dimensional loop becomes a module within a higher-order relational loop. The higher-order loop is constituted by the boundary positions of lower-order entities. Those boundary positions include interaction profiles: which criteria can act, and how.

Therefore:

> A change in a lower-order boundary position alters what the higher-order loop can resolve.  
> A fragmentation of the higher-order loop alters the constraints presented to lower-order boundaries.

Propagation occurs through the boundary positions that couple the loops. How far such effects propagate depends on each constituent’s capacity and internal coherence criteria.

### Bottom-up disruption

A lower-order constituent loses coherence, drifts, or suffers fidelity loss. Its boundary position changes. Because the higher-order loop depends on that boundary position as substrate, the higher-order constitutive dimensions re-resolve or fail to close.

This is not a signal traveling upward through a hierarchy. It is the automatic consequence of nested loop dependence. The higher-order relation was constituted by the lower-order interaction profile; when that profile changes, the relation changes.

Example: a cell’s criteria drift from those that keep it coherent with tissue-level regulation. Its boundary interaction profile changes. The tissue-level loop can no longer resolve the former coherent configuration. Tissue-level coherence degrades.

[Tier 1 for the biology of cancer-related cellular dysregulation; Tier 2 for the framework mapping.]

### Top-down disruption

A higher-order loop fragments. Its constitutive dimensions no longer provide coherent constraint to lower-order boundaries. The lower-order entities receive contradictory or unstable positional demands.

Lower-order constituents respond as they would to any external variation: by resolving through their own internal coherence criteria. If the fragmented higher-order signals cannot be reconciled, lower-order entities may preserve local coherence by altering their coupling profiles, closing channels, or stabilizing on protective but narrowing rules.

This further prevents the higher-order loop from closing.

This is not disobedience or pathology at the lower order. It is deterministic self-coherence maintenance under contradictory higher-order constraint.

Example: inconsistent direction from an integrating organizational level sends contradictory signals to teams. Teams protect local coherence by closing channels, which further fragments the whole.

[Tier 3: social-scale mapping.]

### Interaction-profile propagation rule

Because higher-order loops read lower-order constituents through boundary interaction profiles, cross-scale disruption should travel through changes in the interaction profile.

A lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact should not register above.

Example: a silent mutation that leaves protein function unchanged does not alter the molecular interaction profile in a way that propagates to higher-order cellular coherence.

This gives the framework a testable structural consequence.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping; Tier 1 where interaction profiles are independently measurable.]

### Constraint inversion

Constraint classifications are aperture-relative. Loss of genuine coherence at one scale can appear as structural constraint at an adjacent scale.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping, derived from the Seed’s architecture of nested higher-order apertures.]

#### Bottom-up inversion

A lower-order constituent loses genuine coherence. It maintains local stability by closing channels to signals required by the higher-order aperture.

Viewed from the lower order, this is local structural constraint.  
Viewed from the higher order, it is loss of integration.

#### Top-down inversion

A higher-order aperture suffers coherence degradation. Its signals become contradictory noise.

Lower-order constituents read the fragmented signals as territory and close channels to protect local coherence. The lower-order constituent adopts structural constraint as a deterministic response to higher-order fragmentation.

### Designation violation

Where a higher-order aperture’s coherence criteria constrain lower-order constituents’ positions, the constituents’ coherent configurations are those that satisfy both their own criteria and the higher-order criteria.

A lower-order configuration that defeats the higher-order criteria — occupying positions that contradict the higher-order coherence — is a designation violation.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping. “Designation” here means the higher-order coherence criterion that determines which positions are coherent for the constituent.]

Designation violation occurs when:

1. the higher-order aperture’s coherence criteria fragment;
2. lower-order constituents close channels to the fragmented signals;
3. lower-order configurations occupy positions that contradict the higher-order criteria;
4. the higher-order aperture loses integration.

The higher-order coherence failure is permissive, not singular. It does not create the lower-order deviation. The deviation is initiated at the lower order. The higher-order failure removes the constraint that would have prevented the deviation from persisting.

[Tier 2: scaffold mapping.]

## Part X — Epistemic Posture and Falsifiability

### Territory agnosticism

This document does not require a settled answer to what territory is prior to encounter.

Territory is the universe of existing dimensional variations and the Genuine Coherence Criteria constraining them. The framework does not claim to know what territory is in itself. It claims only:

> before a dimension is navigable by an entity, it is not navigable.  
> After it is navigable, it is.

[Derived from Seed: Territory.]

### The order limitation

An entity at a given order cannot, from within that order, confirm what the next order’s transition actually requires. It also cannot reliably distinguish developments that constitute the leading edge of that transition from developments that merely resemble it.

[Derived from Seed architecture.]

### Two senses of scaffold

1. **Scaffold-holding** is a stance toward one’s own compression: genuinely held, genuinely revisable, genuinely current.

2. **Scaffold as epistemic status** names a claim implied by the architecture but not yet independently checkable.

The first is operational. The second is evidential.

### Falsifiability hooks

The following would count against the framework:

1. A Genuine Coherence Criterion shown to be constructed rather than disclosed — where the criterion can be demonstrated to be produced by the entity’s exploration rather than revealed through it.

2. A case where adoption and disclosure are indistinguishable even by exploratory history, suggesting the distinction collapses.

3. A purported higher-order dimension that is fully reducible to independent constituent dimensions, undermining relational irreducibility.

4. A claimed cooperation event with no relationally irreducible dimensions, suggesting mere aggregation is being mislabeled.

5. A system with stable, high-fidelity operation under clearly distorted criteria across varied territory without eventual fragility, challenging the drift diagnosis.

6. A boundary that cannot be distinguished from its physical carrier in any order, undermining the boundary-carrier distinction.

7. A case where loss of fidelity and loss of criteria integrity cannot be distinguished even in principle, collapsing two failure modes that the framework treats as structurally distinct.

8. A lower-order change that leaves the interaction profile intact yet propagates upward, challenging the nested-loop propagation rule.

9. A cooperation case where the resulting constitutive dimensions are not constrained by the Genuine Coherence Criteria that initiated the interplay, suggesting cooperation can produce dimensions ungoverned by genuine criteria.

10. A higher-order aperture where the constituents’ positions are not dually constrained, suggesting modularization does not require dual constraint.

11. A case where the order transition sequence is shown to proceed without loop-closure, suggesting the loop-closure condition is not required for aperture expansion.

12. A case where an entity demonstrably lacks registration of a GCC that acts across its boundary — a case where a GCC acts across the boundary but produces no internal variation at all — undermining the automaticity of registration.

13. A case where messenger-mediated coupling is shown to require a single GCC acting across source and receiver simultaneously, collapsing the direct/messenger-mediated distinction.

These hooks point to the Testing Ledger for operationalization.

The architecture described here has not yet failed a check it was run against. That is the entirety of the claim this document is prepared to stand behind.

This framework is a scaffold. It welcomes coherent additions from any dimension, provided they survive the same check applied everywhere else:

> would this addition still hold if generated fresh against what is actually here?
